🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

New VAT Group Regime: A new 'portaria' (administrative rule), published around May 29-30, 2026, approved the tax declaration model for the Value Added Tax (VAT) Group Regime. Previously, individual companies within an economic group managed their VAT obligations separately. Now, economic groups in Portugal can consolidate their VAT balances (amounts to pay or recover from the State), with the Tax and Customs Authority providing a pre-filled group declaration for confirmation by the dominant entity. This regime will apply to tax periods commencing from July 1, 2026. This simplifies VAT compliance and potentially improves cash flow management for large economic groups.

Administrative Cooperation in Taxation (Lei n.º 26/2026): Published in the Diário da República on June 3, 2026, and entering into force on June 8, 2026, this law transposes EU Directives 2023/2226 and 2025/872. It expands administrative cooperation in taxation by introducing mandatory and automatic exchange of information related to crypto-assets (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework - CARF) and the GloBE (Global Anti-Base Erosion, or Pillar Two) complementary tax, thereby updating previous rules that focused on traditional financial assets. This significantly increases transparency and regulatory scrutiny over crypto-asset transactions and multinational corporate taxation.

Revision of Urban Planning and Construction Licensing Regime (Decreto-Lei n.º 108/2026): This decree-law, published on May 29, 2026, revises the existing Legal Regime for Urbanization and Building (RJUE), aiming to simplify and streamline urbanistic operations licensing. Previously, obtaining permits involved more complex and lengthy licensing procedures. Now, for buildings or fractions where works were already subject to licensing, prior communication, or exemption, their subsequent use requires only a "mere prior communication"; for other cases, a "prior communication with a deadline" is sufficient, allowing for municipal inspection, thereby reducing bureaucratic hurdles and accelerating project approvals. This facilitates and speeds up the licensing process for construction and urban rehabilitation projects, potentially increasing housing supply and reducing costs and delays for developers and homeowners.

Housing Package: Reduced VAT on Construction: Under Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026 (approved on May 20, 2026), a reduced VAT rate of 6% will apply to qualifying construction and rehabilitation works from July 1, 2026. Previously, these works were generally subject to the standard 23% VAT rate. This reduced rate is specifically for properties intended for sale for permanent own housing or exclusive residential leasing, provided the sale price or monthly rent does not exceed moderate-value ceilings. This incentivizes the construction and rehabilitation of affordable and moderate-rent housing by lowering development costs.

Why it matters: The most significant change for Portuguese citizens and businesses is the set of measures aimed at streamlining construction and increasing transparency in taxation, particularly concerning the new VAT group regime and crypto-assets.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Amendments to the Prospectus Regulation (EU Listing Act): Previously, issuers followed the general Prospectus Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/1129) for the format, content, scrutiny, and approval of prospectuses. Now, from June 5, 2026, amendments introduced by the EU Listing Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2809) apply, significantly changing these requirements for prospectuses, particularly impacting EMEA bond issuers. This includes new rules for disclosing inside information in protracted processes, delayed disclosure, permission for trading during closed periods for persons discharging managerial responsibilities (PDMRs), and updated indicators of market manipulation to reflect algorithmic trading. Base prospectuses approved before June 5, 2026, are grandfathered for their 12-month life. Businesses and investors in financial markets, especially those involved in bond issuance, must comply with these updated capital market disclosure requirements to streamline listings on EU markets.

EU Pay Transparency Directive (Directive (EU) 2023/970): Previously, there was no comprehensive, legally binding EU-wide framework specifically mandating pay transparency across all Member States. Now, Member States are required to transpose this Directive into national law by June 7, 2026. This obliges employers to provide pay transparency measures, such as informing job applicants of salary ranges and allowing employees to request information on their individual pay level and the average pay levels for categories of workers doing the same work or work of equal value. Companies with 150 or more employees will have phased-in reporting obligations from 2027. Portuguese businesses must adapt their recruitment and remuneration practices to comply with these transparency rules, as Portugal is among the Member States with a rapidly approaching transposition deadline.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1183 on Preferential Origin of Goods: Previously, procedural rules on the preferential origin of goods were governed by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447. Now, Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1183, published on June 3, 2026, amends Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447 to update these procedural rules. Businesses involved in importing or exporting goods with preferential origin within the EU and with third countries must now adhere to the new administrative processes for determining and verifying preferential origin, affecting customs declarations and trade agreements.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1189 on Antimicrobial Medicinal Products: Previously, restrictions on the use of certain antimicrobial medicinal products were governed by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/405, with Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2598 also in effect. Now, Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1189, published on June 4, 2026, amends the application of these restrictions under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/405 and explicitly repeals Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2598. This consolidates and updates the regulatory framework for controlling and limiting the use of antimicrobials, primarily impacting pharmaceutical companies and businesses in the agricultural sector, particularly those involved in livestock farming.

European Commission Infringement Procedures against Portugal for Non-Transposition of Directives: Previously, Portugal was legally obliged to fully transpose several EU directives into national law by their respective deadlines, including the Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (Directive (EU) 2024/825), Directive (EU) 2024/782 on the recognition of professional qualifications, Directive (EU) 2016/1919 on legal aid for suspects, and Regulation (EU) 2023/2405 (ReFuelEU Aviation) concerning penalties. Now, as of June 4, 2026, the European Commission has initiated infringement procedures against Portugal (among other Member States) by sending letters of formal notice for failing to communicate complete transposition of these directives. Portugal now has two months to respond and complete the transposition measures, or face further legal steps, including reasoned opinions and potential referral to the Court of Justice of the European Union. Portuguese businesses and citizens are directly impacted by the delayed implementation, as they do not yet benefit from the full protections or frameworks intended by these EU-mandated rules.

🇺🇸 US Policy

Executive Order on Strengthening Customs Enforcement: President Donald J. Trump signed an Executive Order on June 3, 2026, directing the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) to implement comprehensive reforms to customs enforcement. This order increases bonding requirements for importers of record (IORs), subjects foreign IORs to heightened requirements for formal entry, and authorizes only U.S. IORs to file informal entries. It also imposes a "good standing" requirement on all IORs, increases vetting for individuals and entities involved in imports, establishes new disclosure and certification requirements to combat duty evasion, and sets a 50% minimum penalty floor for customs law violations, while streamlining the seizure and disposal of non-compliant imports. Previously, foreign IORs could file informal entries, and CBP had more discretion in reducing penalties for customs violations. This action aims to bolster domestic industry and revenue by deterring trade fraud and enhancing compliance.

Executive Order on Federal Workforce Accountability: President Donald J. Trump signed an Executive Order on June 3, 2026, titled "Implementing Schedule Policy/Career in the Excepted Service." This order reclassifies approximately 8,000 senior policy-influencing federal positions, primarily GS-15 or Senior Level equivalents, into "Schedule Policy/Career" within the excepted service. This change makes these positions at-will, enabling their removal for poor performance, misconduct, corruption, or subversion of Presidential directives without the extensive procedural hurdles that previously made such removals difficult. This action aims to enhance the President's ability to ensure federal employees align with and implement administration policies, potentially accelerating policy execution and increasing accountability.

State Department Designations of Brazilian Criminal Organizations as Foreign Terrorist Organizations (FTOs): Effective June 5, 2026, the U.S. Department of State designated Brazil's two largest criminal organizations, Primeiro Comando da Capital (PCC) and Comando Vermelho (CV), as Foreign Terrorist Organizations (FTOs). Previously, while some actions were taken against these groups, the FTO designation significantly broadens the legal and financial tools available to the U.S. government against them and their associates. This action significantly increases compliance and sanctions risks for companies and financial institutions globally, particularly those operating in Latin America.

USTR Proposed Section 301 Tariffs on Forced Labor Goods: On June 2, 2026, the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) announced proposed Section 301 tariffs ranging from 10% to 37.5% on imports from 60 U.S. trading partners. These proposed duties are intended to address the investigated economies' alleged failures to prohibit or effectively enforce bans on goods produced with forced labor. The proposed rates are tiered, with 10% for economies that have imposed or committed to forced labor import prohibitions, and 12.5% for others, with a 37.5% tariff specifically proposed for Brazil. These duties are not yet effective; USTR is accepting public comments until July 6, 2026, and will hold public hearings on July 7, 2026. This proposal, if implemented, could reshape global supply chains and increase import costs for affected goods, particularly from countries identified as having issues with forced labor.

Specifically, it expands the category of derivative products subject to a temporarily reduced 15% ad valorem duty to include agricultural equipment and certain residential heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems and components. This action aims to provide relief for specific domestic manufacturing sectors that rely on these imported metal derivatives as inputs, potentially lowering costs for these industries.
