🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Novas Regras para Crédito à Habitação e ao Consumo: As novas diretrizes do Banco de Portugal entraram em vigor a 1 de agosto de 2026. Anteriormente, a taxa de esforço máxima para a aprovação de créditos era de 50%. Agora, esta desce para 45% do rendimento líquido mensal, considerando todos os empréstimos do cliente. Os prazos máximos dos empréstimos passam a depender apenas da idade do mutuário mais velho, fixando-se em até 40 anos para quem tem até 35 anos (anteriormente, por exemplo, entre 30 e 35 anos o limite era 37 anos), e até 35 anos para quem tem mais de 35 anos. Adicionalmente, a margem de exceção que os bancos tinham para aprovar créditos acima do limite da taxa de esforço foi apertada de 15% para 10% do montante total de créditos concedidos por semestre. Isto significa que os bancos terão menos flexibilidade para conceder empréstimos com taxas de esforço mais elevadas.

Lei de Autorização para a Prestação Social Única (PSU): A Lei n.º 36/2026, de 27 de julho, foi publicada no Diário da República, autorizando o Governo a criar a Prestação Social Única no subsistema de solidariedade da segurança social. Anteriormente, as famílias em situação de carência económica podiam aceder a diversas prestações sociais separadas, como o Rendimento Social de Inserção, Pensão Social de Velhice, ou Subsídio Social de Desemprego. Agora, esta lei estabelece a agregação de 13 destas prestações numa única, simplificando os apoios. Foi fixado um valor de referência da PSU em 50% do Indexante dos Apoios Sociais (IAS), o que representa um aumento de 8,5% do valor de referência do Rendimento Social de Inserção (RSI).

Prorrogação da Entrada em Vigor do Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação (RJUE): O Decreto-Lei n.º 155-B/2026, de 31 de julho, foi publicado, alterando a data de entrada em vigor do Decreto-Lei n.º 108/2026, de 29 de maio, que revista o Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação. Anteriormente, as reformas ao RJUE, que visam flexibilizar procedimentos e agilizar prazos, estavam previstas para entrar em vigor antes. Agora, a sua entrada em vigor é adiada para 1 de outubro de 2026. Esta prorrogação concede aos municípios mais tempo para adaptarem e parametrizarem as suas plataformas informáticas e para implementarem a regulamentação complementar necessária.

Alterações à Declaração Periódica do IVA e Regime de Grupos de IVA: A Portaria n.º 298/2026/1, de 16 de julho, embora publicada fora dos últimos 7 dias, introduziu alterações no modelo da declaração periódica do IVA, com efeitos a partir de 1 de julho de 2026, para acomodar novas regras que estão em vigor. Anteriormente, as empresas eram tratadas como entidades de IVA individuais e o formulário de declaração não refletia o regime de grupos de IVA nem as regularizações específicas da taxa reduzida de IVA para a habitação. Agora, o regime de grupos de IVA permite que empresas relacionadas sejam tratadas como uma única entidade para efeitos de IVA, compensando saldos entre membros e desconsiderando transações internas. A declaração periódica do IVA passa a ter campos específicos para o regime de grupos de IVA e para as regularizações de imposto associadas à taxa reduzida de 6% de IVA aplicada à construção e reabilitação de habitação vendida ou arrendada a preços moderados, que entrou em vigor em 1 de julho de 2026.

Why it matters: As novas e mais restritivas regras para o crédito à habitação e ao consumo, em vigor desde 1 de agosto, terão um impacto direto na capacidade de endividamento de famílias e empresas portuguesas.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU AI Act – Transparency Obligations (Article 50): Previously, explicit transparency obligations for most AI systems were not formally in force. Now, from August 2, 2026, providers and deployers of AI systems must inform users when they are interacting with an AI system, such as chatbots. All AI-generated or manipulated content, including deepfakes, must be clearly labeled, including with machine-readable marks. Deployers must also inform individuals when emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems are in use. Compliance for machine-readable watermarking of AI-generated content from systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, is extended to December 2, 2026; for new systems, it applies from August 2, 2026. This means companies, including those in Portugal, operating or developing AI systems that interact with EU citizens or generate content, must implement disclosure mechanisms and labeling.

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (AI Omnibus) amending the EU AI Act: Previously, many high-risk AI systems had compliance deadlines set for August 2, 2026, and the AI Office's oversight of General-Purpose AI (GPAI) models was less centralized. Now, this Regulation, which entered into force on July 27, 2026, delays the application date for obligations concerning stand-alone high-risk AI systems (e.g., in employment, credit scoring, law enforcement) from August 2, 2026, to December 2, 2027. For high-risk AI systems embedded in products (e.g., medical devices), the deadline is extended to August 2, 2028. It also introduces new prohibitions, applicable from December 2, 2026, against AI systems designed to generate non-consensual intimate images or child sexual abuse material. The AI Office's powers are strengthened, giving it exclusive supervisory responsibility for certain GPAI models and AI systems integrated into very large online platforms. Portuguese businesses developing or deploying high-risk AI systems gain significant additional time for compliance, while all AI developers must ensure their systems are not used for newly prohibited content, and those with GPAI models face centralized oversight.

New EU Gas Market Rules: Previously, the EU gas market operated under a different regulatory framework with less emphasis on consumer protection in certain areas. Now, from August 5, 2026, new rules will apply to create a more secure and integrated European gas market, aiming to reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels. These rules strengthen consumer rights by ensuring free choice of supplier, providing better information on offers, and offering protection in case of disputes. Vulnerable consumers and those affected by energy poverty are particularly protected from disconnection of gas supply, including during the energy transition. This will affect energy companies and gas suppliers across the EU, including in Portugal, by requiring adherence to new market integration and consumer protection standards, potentially leading to operational and contractual changes.

EU ESG Ratings Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/3005): Previously, ESG rating providers operating in the EU did not require formal authorization or recognition from the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA). Now, the Regulation, which entered into application on July 2, 2026, mandates that ESG rating providers notify ESMA of their intention to apply for authorization or recognition by August 2, 2026. The full application submission to ESMA is required by November 2, 2026. This directly affects all ESG rating providers operating within the EU, including any Portuguese providers, by imposing new regulatory oversight and compliance requirements to ensure transparency and integrity in ESG ratings for investors.

Council Regulation (EU) 2026/1867 amending Regulation (EU) No 267/2012: Previously, restrictive measures against Iran were governed by Regulation (EU) No 267/2012. Now, Council Regulation (EU) 2026/1867, published in the Official Journal on July 30, 2026, amends these existing measures, including the repeal of Regulation (EU) No 961/2010. Specific changes concern updates to sanctions against Iran. EU businesses and financial institutions, including those in Portugal, involved in trade or financial transactions with Iran must immediately review their compliance frameworks to adhere to the updated restrictive measures.

🇺🇸 US Policy

CMS FY 2027 Hospital Inpatient and Long-Term Care Hospital Payment Rule (CMS-1849-F): On July 31, 2026, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) issued a final rule updating Medicare payment policies and rates for inpatient and long-term care hospitals under the Medicare hospital Inpatient Prospective Payment System (IPPS) and Long-Term Care Hospital Prospective Payment System (LTCH PPS) for fiscal year (FY) 2027. The rule provides a 2.3% increase in payment rates for both IPPS hospitals and LTCHs, expected to increase IPPS payments by approximately $2.1 billion and LTCH PPS payments by $54 million in FY 2027. It also expands the Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement (CJR) Model nationwide, effective January 1, 2028. Previously, the CJR Model was not mandatory nationwide. This rule significantly adjusts funding for a wide range of hospital services, impacting healthcare providers' revenues and the scope of bundled payment models for joint replacements.

USTR Tariffs on 60 Countries for Insufficient Forced Labor Import Prohibitions: On July 31, 2026, the Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) announced new tariffs on 60 countries under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974. These tariffs are imposed because these countries have not enforced bans on imports of products made with forced labor. The additional rates of duty are applicable to products entered for consumption on or after July 24, 2026. Previously, these tariffs were not in place for these specific reasons. This action marks a significant escalation in U.S. trade policy aimed at combating forced labor globally, potentially increasing import costs and pressuring targeted nations to revise their import regulations.

Treasury Sanctions on Iran's Strait of Hormuz Extortion Network: On July 29, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated two firms, Persian Gulf Marine Insurance Company and HormuzSafe Marine Services Authority, as integral to an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC)-backed scheme that forces commercial vessels to purchase mandatory maritime "insurance" to transit the Strait of Hormuz. All property and interests in property of these designated entities subject to U.S. jurisdiction are blocked. Previously, these specific entities were not sanctioned for this activity. These sanctions aim to disrupt a revenue stream for the Iranian regime and enhance maritime security in the critical Strait of Hormuz, potentially impacting shipping costs and regional geopolitical stability.

OPM Interim Final Rule Removing Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (UGESP) References: The Office of Personnel Management (OPM) issued an interim final rule, effective July 31, 2026, removing references to the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (UGESP) from Federal civil service regulations. This action conforms OPM's regulations to a June 9, 2026, Office of Legal Counsel opinion that found UGESP unlawful. Previously, UGESP provided a framework that federal agencies were expected to follow for employee selection. This deregulatory action changes the framework for federal employment selection, giving agencies more flexibility while still requiring job-related, merit-based, and professionally sound selection procedures compliant with other anti-discrimination laws.

Secure America Act Enactment: The Secure America Act, a fiscal year 2027 reconciliation bill, was signed into law by President Donald Trump this week, prior to July 31, 2026. The bill funds much of the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), particularly Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Customs and Border Patrol (CBP). Previously, the funding for FY 2027 was not yet enacted. This enactment secures significant funding for critical homeland security operations, impacting border enforcement, immigration policies, and the operational capabilities of DHS agencies.
