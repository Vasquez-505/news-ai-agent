Regime de Identificação em Espaços Públicos (Decreto da Assembleia da República 106/XVII): Este diploma estabelece regras de segurança e prevenção relativas à identificação de cidadãos em espaços públicos. Entrou em vigor a 23 de setembro de 2026. Anteriormente, não existia uma lei nacional que regulamentasse explicitamente a obrigatoriedade de remoção de coberturas faciais para efeitos de identificação em todos os espaços públicos. A nova regra impõe que, quando solicitado por autoridades competentes para identificação, os cidadãos devem remover quaisquer coberturas faciais, sob pena de multas que podem ascender a 3.000 euros. O impacto prático é que qualquer pessoa em espaços públicos com o rosto coberto por motivos religiosos ou outros deverá revelar o rosto para identificação perante as autoridades, alterando a prática anterior onde tal não era universalmente exigido por lei.

O impacto prático é que os processos urbanísticos e de construção continuam a ser regidos pelas regras anteriores até 1 de outubro de 2026, proporcionando um período adicional de preparação para as mudanças significativas na simplificação dos licenciamentos.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Union Customs Code (UCC) Reform (Regulation (EU) 2026/2108): This reform establishes a new Union Customs Code, a European Union Customs Authority (EUCA), and a central EU Customs Data Hub, shifting the customs model from fragmented national systems and transaction-based declarations to a data-led approach. Businesses are now required to provide customs and product information through a single EU Customs Data Hub. For e-commerce, the previous €150 customs duty exemption for low-value consignments is abolished and replaced by a temporary €3 customs duty from July 1, 2026, until July 2028, after which normal duties will apply. A new EU-wide handling fee for small parcels will also be introduced from November 2026. The Data Hub becomes mandatory for e-commerce by July 1, 2028, and for all traders by March 1, 2034, with Member States having 12 months from September 20, 2026, to fully implement the new rules. The reform simplifies and digitalises customs procedures for businesses, particularly e-commerce, across the EU, but it increases costs for low-value e-commerce imports and places greater responsibility on importers for data quality and product compliance, which is particularly relevant to Portuguese import/export and e-commerce businesses.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2099 on cross-border digital ID for health data: This regulation establishes requirements for an interoperable, cross-border identification and authentication mechanism for natural persons, health professionals, and healthcare providers to exchange personal electronic health data via the MyHealth@EU network. Previously, there was less harmonized or no standardized cross-border digital identity verification for health data. Now, identity verification must integrate directly with national digital ID schemes recognized under the eIDAS framework. Member States must determine and notify the Commission of a set of healthcare attributes for identifying individuals in electronic health records for cross-border exchange by March 26, 2028. Competent authorities must then issue these attributes as electronic attestations to European Digital Identity Wallets upon request by March 26, 2029. Authentication for online patient requests must initially meet at least a substantial assurance level under eIDAS, moving to a high assurance level from March 2030, with similar requirements for health professionals by March 2032. This enhances the security and ease of cross-border access to electronic health records for EU citizens and health professionals, promoting digital health services and data exchange across Member States, including Portugal.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2101 imposing definitive anti-dumping duty on pea protein from China: This regulation imposes a definitive anti-dumping duty on imports of pea protein originating in the People's Republic of China and definitively collects the provisional duty that was previously imposed. Previously, provisional anti-dumping duties were in place. Now, EU importers of pea protein from China face a permanent, definitive anti-dumping duty following an investigation that found material injury to the Union industry from dumped imports. This aims to protect EU pea protein producers from unfair competition, but it may increase costs for Portuguese food and feed manufacturers reliant on imported pea protein from China.

Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2112 authorising genetically modified soybean GMB151 × DAS-44406-6: This decision authorises the placing on the market of foods, food ingredients, and feed containing, consisting of, or produced from genetically modified soybean GMB151 × DAS-44406-6. Previously, this specific genetically modified soybean was not authorised for market placement in the EU. Now, following a favourable scientific opinion from the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), this GM soybean is permitted for import and use in food and feed products within the EU, with the authorisation valid for 10 years and subject to EU labelling and traceability rules. This expands the range of authorised GM products in the EU food and feed market, potentially affecting sourcing and production for businesses in these sectors.

Why it matters: The entry into force of the comprehensive Union Customs Code Reform, particularly its impact on e-commerce and the move towards a single EU Customs Data Hub, represents the most significant EU-level change this week, aiming to digitalize and streamline trade while increasing compliance requirements for businesses.
