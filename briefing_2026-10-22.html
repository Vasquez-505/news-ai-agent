🇪🇺 EU Policy

Revised Carcinogens, Mutagens and Reprotoxic Substances Directive (CMRD): This directive sets new Occupational Exposure Limit Values (OELs) for cobalt and its inorganic compounds, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, 1,4-dioxane, and isoprene, and adds welding fumes to the list of hazardous substances in Annex I. Previously, specific EU-wide OELs were not established for these substances, and welding fumes were not explicitly included, meaning worker exposure was less stringently regulated. Now, companies must comply with these new limits and revised safety protocols, with Member States required to transpose the rules into national law within two years of its entry into force, which occurs upon publication in the Official Journal. This significantly strengthens worker protection against work-related cancers and other illnesses across all EU industries.

Amendment to the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) Market Stability Reserve (MSR): A provisional political agreement has been reached to amend the MSR, allowing it to retain up to 700 million emission allowances as a buffer. Previously, the MSR automatically invalidated all emission allowances exceeding 400 million, leading to less flexibility in market response. This amendment aims to reinforce the MSR's long-term operational capacity, enhancing the stability and predictability of the EU carbon market. This provides greater certainty for businesses and investors, encouraging investment in industrial decarbonisation efforts.

Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2324 lifting rule of law measures for Hungary: This decision formally lifts measures that protected the Union budget against breaches of the principles of the rule of law in Hungary, and repeals the previous Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/2506. Previously, Hungary faced restrictions on accessing certain EU funds due to rule of law concerns, impacting its national budget and potentially foreign investment. With the lifting of these measures, Hungary can now access previously withheld EU funds, which could significantly alter the financial and investment landscape within the country.

Council Decision (EU) 2026/2351 on Iceland's participation in EU Space Programmes: This decision concludes an agreement between the European Union and Iceland, establishing the rules for Iceland's participation in the GOVSATCOM component of the Union Space Programme and the Union Secure Connectivity Programme, and for accessing their services. Previously, Iceland did not have a formal framework for participating in and accessing these specific EU space and secure connectivity initiatives. This agreement fosters closer cooperation in space and secure communication technologies, potentially opening new avenues for tech and security-related businesses in both the EU and Iceland.
