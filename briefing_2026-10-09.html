Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 on Air Passenger Rights: This regulation substantially amends existing air passenger rights legislation, namely Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 and Council Regulation (EC) No 2027/97. Previously, re-routing rights were less comprehensive; now, air carriers must consider competing flights and other transport modes for re-routing if the passenger chooses the earliest opportunity. Previously, there were fewer specific provisions for personal items; now, airlines must allow one small personal item (up to 40x30x15 cm or fitting under the seat) free of charge in the cabin. The regulation also introduces stricter reimbursement obligations and enhanced protections for Persons with Reduced Mobility (PRMs), including offering temporary replacements for lost, delayed, or damaged mobility equipment. Airlines must comply with the new provisions on passenger rights by October 23, 2027, although the regulation formally enters into force on October 22, 2026. This will significantly strengthen consumer protections for Portuguese and other EU citizens traveling by air, while requiring airlines, including Portuguese carriers, to adapt their operational and claims handling procedures.

Sixth Revision of the Carcinogens, Mutagens and Reprotoxic Substances Directive (CMRD): The European Parliament formally approved new rules under this directive. Previously, occupational exposure limits for certain dangerous chemicals were either absent or less stringent; now, the directive sets new occupational exposure limit values for cobalt and inorganic cobalt compounds (used in battery production), polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (used in steel, iron, and aluminium production), and 1,4-dioxane (used in chemical and textile production). It also introduces a long-term occupational exposure limit value for isoprene (used in the chemical and rubber industries). Employers must ensure additional protection measures for firefighters and provide appropriate training to workers exposed to these hazardous substances. Member States will be required to transpose these new rules into national law, typically within 12-24 months of the Parliament's approval on October 6, 2026. This directive will enhance the health and safety of workers across the EU, including in Portuguese industries and for Portuguese emergency services personnel, by mandating stricter controls on exposure to dangerous chemicals.

Application of the Energy Efficiency Directive (Directive (EU) 2023/1791) Audit Requirements: Previously, while the directive was adopted in 2023, the mandatory energy audit requirements for certain companies were not yet in effect. Now, companies with an annual energy consumption exceeding 10 terajoules (TJ) must carry out a regular, independent energy audit by October 2026, unless they already operate a certified energy management system. This obligation applies to numerous organizations across various sectors, including industrial manufacturing, logistics, distribution, food processing, and cold chain operations. This mandates concrete steps towards energy efficiency for many Portuguese businesses, compelling them to identify and implement energy-saving measures this month.

Council Decision (EU) 2026/2256 on CBAM Financial Contribution to EEA Agreement: This Council Decision establishes the position to be adopted on behalf of the European Union within the EEA Joint Committee. Previously, the EEA Agreement did not explicitly detail financial contributions from EEA EFTA states related to the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). Now, the decision sets the stage for amending Protocol 10 and Protocol 31 of the EEA Agreement to include a CBAM financial contribution. This formalizes how EEA EFTA countries will financially participate in the EU's carbon pricing framework, ensuring a more consistent application of carbon costs across the wider European Economic Area. The decision was published in EUR-Lex on October 8, 2026, with the actual amendments and subsequent financial obligations to follow. This will impact trade and economic relations between EU member states, including Portugal, and EEA EFTA countries, particularly concerning carbon-intensive imports.
