🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Alteração ao Código dos Contratos Públicos (Decreto-Lei n.º 177/2026, de 4 de setembro): Este diploma simplifica e atualiza as regras de contratação pública, elevando os limiares para a adoção de procedimentos mais céleres. Anteriormente, os limites para o ajuste direto e a consulta prévia eram mais baixos; agora, os ajustes diretos são possíveis para contratos de valor estimado inferior a €150.000, e as consultas prévias para contratos de valor estimado inferior a €1.000.000, além de permitir a integração de sistemas digitais e inteligência artificial. O objetivo é tornar a contratação pública mais eficiente e menos burocrática, com um impacto prático significativo para as empresas que contratam com o Estado e para a celeridade dos investimentos públicos.

Novo Regime de Perda Alargada de Bens: Entrou em vigor uma alteração legislativa que permite ao Estado confiscar bens provenientes de atividades criminosas mesmo sem uma condenação formal do arguido. Anteriormente, a confiscação de bens exigia geralmente uma condenação; agora, os lucros obtidos através de crimes podem ser confiscados desde que tenha sido instaurado um procedimento criminal. Esta medida visa reforçar a recuperação de património ilícito e tem um impacto direto nos procedimentos judiciais e na segurança jurídica dos cidadãos e empresas envolvidos em processos criminais.

Reestruturação do Instituto da Mobilidade e dos Transportes, I. P. (IMT, I. P.) (Decreto-Lei n.º 179/2026, de 7 de setembro): Este decreto-lei reestrutura o IMT, I. P., transferindo as competências relacionadas com a segurança ferroviária e outros sistemas guiados para a Autoridade da Mobilidade e dos Transportes (AMT), com exceção do licenciamento para a prestação de serviços de transporte ferroviário. Anteriormente, o IMT, I. P., acumulava estas responsabilidades; agora, procura-se dotar o IMT de uma estrutura com maior capacidade para apoiar um sistema de mobilidade e transportes mais intermodal e digitalizado, tendo um impacto prático na gestão e regulação do setor dos transportes.

Nova Linha de Crédito de €1.5 Mil Milhões para Habitação: O Governo português e o Banco Europeu de Investimento (BEI) aprovaram uma nova linha de crédito de 1,5 mil milhões de euros, destinada ao financiamento de 50.000 novas habitações. Anteriormente, não existia esta linha de financiamento específica; agora, esta medida pretende aumentar a oferta de habitação, facilitando o acesso ao crédito para o desenvolvimento de projetos habitacionais e beneficiando diretamente cidadãos e empresas do setor da construção e imobiliário.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU Customs Reform (Final Council Approval): This landmark reform assigns non-EU e-commerce platforms the responsibility of being the importer for goods sold into the EU, ensuring they handle all customs formalities and duty payments, rather than the final EU consumer. Previously, the liability for customs often fell on the consumer or was less clearly defined for e-commerce. Now, non-EU e-commerce platforms selling into the EU are explicitly considered the importer and are subject to a new system of penalties for non-compliance, which can include fines of up to 6% of the company's annual import value. Additionally, an EU-wide handling fee on small parcels will be introduced by November 1, 2026, and a new "trust and check traders" scheme will offer simplified customs procedures for reliable businesses. This is particularly relevant for businesses engaged in e-commerce, affecting import and export operations, especially those dealing with Portugal.

Revised European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) Finalised: The revised European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) and a voluntary sustainability reporting standard have cleared the final stage of the EU legislative process, with the scrutiny period by the European Parliament and Council expiring without objection on September 8, 2026. Previously, companies adhered to various national or international sustainability reporting frameworks, or less stringent EU guidelines. Now, these standards are final and will apply to financial years beginning on or after January 1, 2027, with the first reports due in 2028. This requires affected companies, including many large businesses and listed SMEs, to prepare comprehensive sustainability reports covering environmental, social, and governance impacts, significantly impacting investors and financial markets by increasing transparency and comparability of sustainability data.

Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) - Mandatory Incident Reporting: From September 11, 2026, manufacturers of products with digital elements are required to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe security incidents to relevant authorities. Previously, there were no harmonised EU-wide mandatory, time-bound reporting obligations of this nature under the CRA. Now, organisations must submit an early warning within 24 hours of awareness, a more detailed notification within 72 hours, and a final report within 14 days for vulnerabilities (or one month for severe incidents) once a corrective measure is available. This obligation applies even to products placed on the market before the CRA's full application in December 2027. This significantly impacts tech companies and manufacturers across all sectors operating in the EU, including those in Portugal, by imposing stringent cybersecurity incident reporting duties.

EU Data Act - "Access-by-Design" Obligation: As of September 12, 2026, connected products and related services placed on the EU market must be designed to make relevant data easily, securely, and directly accessible to users by default, free of charge, where relevant and technically feasible. Previously, the core data access obligations under the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) required data holders to provide access upon user request, applicable since September 12, 2025. Now, for new products and services, the design itself must inherently facilitate data access. This significantly impacts manufacturers and providers of connected devices and services, including those in the tech and IoT sectors, by mandating a proactive approach to data accessibility.

Commission Delegated Regulation on Central Contact Points for Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs): On September 8, 2026, the European Commission adopted a Delegated Regulation amending Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/1108 to include criteria for the appointment of central contact points for Crypto-Asset Service Providers and rules on their functions. Previously, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/1108 established regulatory technical standards for central contact points in other financial sectors. Now, the scope is explicitly extended to CASPs, requiring a consistent approach to their appointment and functions across all sectors. This directly impacts crypto-asset service providers and financial market regulators in Portugal and across the EU, ensuring a more harmonised oversight of the burgeoning crypto market.

Why it matters: This week marks a significant shift towards enhanced accountability and transparency for businesses operating in the EU, with new rules entering into force for e-commerce customs, corporate sustainability reporting, cybersecurity incident disclosure, and data access by design in connected products.
