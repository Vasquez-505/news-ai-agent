O novo regime visa promover a igualdade de oportunidades no acesso e frequência do ensino superior, através de apoios sociais diretos (bolsas de estudo) e indiretos (residências estudantis, apoio psicológico, medidas de adaptação para estudantes com necessidades educativas específicas). Anteriormente, o sistema existente não conseguia contrariar eficazmente a reprodução das desigualdades sociais; agora, procura-se uma intervenção pública mais ativa. O impacto prático é o aumento do apoio financeiro e de serviços para estudantes de contextos socioeconómicos menos favorecidos, facilitando o acesso e permanência no ensino superior.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

New EU Air Passenger Rights (Regulation expected to be adopted after recent approval): Previously, passengers faced varying airline policies on minor name corrections, printed boarding passes, and seating arrangements for vulnerable travelers. Now, airlines will be required to offer free seat assignments for children under 14 next to accompanying adults, and for pregnant individuals and those with reduced mobility to sit next to a companion. Furthermore, airlines must rectify minor name spelling errors on tickets and provide printed boarding passes free of charge. Passengers are also guaranteed one item of hand luggage (maximum 40x30x15cm) free of charge. Existing compensation rules for delays and cancellations (e.g., €250 for short-haul, triggered after 3 hours delay) are maintained. These rules are expected to enter into application in mid-2027. This greatly enhances consumer protection and standardizes airline services across the EU, directly benefiting Portuguese travelers and requiring adjustments from airlines operating in or from Portugal.

Regulation (EU) 2026/1768 amending agricultural support rules: Previously, Regulations (EU) 2021/2115 and 2021/2116 provided frameworks for the European Agricultural Fund for Rural Development (EAFRD) and direct payments with less immediate flexibility for market disruptions. Now, this amending regulation introduces a specific type of intervention to provide exceptional temporary support under the EAFRD and allows for the adaptation of direct payments allocations for the calendar year 2027. It also provides more flexible rules on payments of advances, specifically to address the increased fertiliser prices due to the Middle East crisis. This regulation entered into force on July 21, 2026. This provides crucial financial flexibility and support for farmers and agricultural businesses across the EU, including those in Portugal, facing economic pressures from rising input costs.

Commission Recommendation on the 'Methane' Regulation Sanctions Postponement: Previously, Member States were expected to ensure compliance with the monitoring, reporting, and verification (MRV) obligations for methane emissions under the 'methane' regulation (2024/1787) with earlier potential for sanctions. Now, the European Commission, through recommendations published on July 20, 2026, has decided to postpone the application of sanctions for Member States until 2030, in cases where importers fail to submit their methane emission declarations. This means Member States and affected importers of gas, coal, and oil have an additional three years to implement compliance methodologies without immediate punitive measures.

Why it matters: This week saw significant advancements in consumer protection for air travel and crucial financial relief and flexibility for the agricultural sector, alongside an important adjustment to environmental regulation enforcement.

🇺🇸 US Policy

New 50% Tariffs on Canadian Goods: President Donald J. Trump signed three proclamations on July 20, 2026, under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, imposing additional 50% tariffs on approximately $20 billion worth of Canadian imports, including motor vehicles, alcoholic beverages, and dairy products. These tariffs, which are in response to Canada's alleged discriminatory trade practices against U.S. products, will take effect on August 19, 2026, and will apply to covered goods regardless of their status under the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA). Previously, many of these goods either entered the U.S. duty-free or at lower tariff rates under existing trade agreements. This action escalates trade tensions with a key North American partner, potentially impacting supply chains and increasing costs for consumers and businesses reliant on Canadian imports.

Executive Order on Securing America's Defense Supply Chains: President Donald J. Trump signed an Executive Order on July 20, 2026, that tightens the rules for U.S. defense contractors seeking waivers to procure critical minerals and other materials from prohibited foreign suppliers, such as China. The order directs the Department of Defense to initiate regulatory action for more comprehensive supply chain mapping from raw materials to finished products and mandates that contractors must prove they have exhausted alternatives and present mitigation plans to onshore supply chains to obtain waivers. Previously, obtaining such waivers may have been easier without explicit requirements for extensive domestic sourcing plans or comprehensive supply chain mapping. This order aims to enhance national security by reducing reliance on foreign adversaries in defense production, but it may increase compliance burdens and costs for defense contractors.

CFTC Enforcement Advisory on Self-Reporting and Cooperation: The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) issued an Enforcement Advisory on July 21, 2026, detailing how civil penalty reductions may apply to firms that voluntarily disclose breaches, cooperate with investigators, and remediate issues across derivatives and digital commodity markets, explicitly including crypto firms. This advisory clarifies the incentives for self-reporting, aiming to promote better compliance, surveillance, and governance within the regulated markets. Previously, the specific benefits of self-reporting for penalty reductions were less formally outlined. This guidance provides a clearer framework for regulated entities, particularly in the evolving crypto sector, to manage enforcement risks and potentially mitigate financial penalties through proactive compliance.

New 25% Tariffs on Brazilian Goods: At the direction of President Trump, the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) imposed a 25% tariff on certain imports from Brazil, effective July 22, 2026. This action follows a year-long Section 301 investigation that determined Brazil's trade practices, including disparate tariff treatment for U.S. ethanol, lack of intellectual property protections, deforestation of the Amazon rainforest, and digital trade barriers, were unreasonable and burdened U.S. commerce. Previously, these specific imports from Brazil were not subject to these additional tariffs. These tariffs are designed to address perceived unfair trade practices by Brazil, potentially affecting import costs and supply chains for certain Brazilian goods entering the U.S. market.

Why it matters: The U.S. government has taken decisive steps this week to aggressively re-assert protectionist trade policies and strengthen domestic supply chain security, indicating a significant shift in international economic relations.
