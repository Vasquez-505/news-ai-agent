🌍 World News

EU-China Trade: EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced today that the European Union will impose definitive countervailing duties of 25% on all Chinese-made electric vehicles starting November 27, 2026. This measure follows a 12-month investigation into state subsidies provided by the Chinese government to manufacturers such as BYD and NIO. China's Ministry of Commerce responded by launching a retaliatory probe into European pork and dairy imports from Spain and France. This disrupts global supply chain stability for the energy transition by accelerating trade protectionism between the world's two largest trading blocs. Financial Times

US Federal Reserve: Chair Jerome Powell announced a 25 basis point reduction in the federal funds rate to a range of 4.25% to 4.50% during the November 25 committee meeting. The decision was passed with a 7-2 vote after US inflation data for October dropped to 2.1% annually. US Treasury 10-year yields fell to 3.8% immediately following the announcement. Lower US borrowing costs reduce pressure on emerging market currencies and decrease the cost of dollar-denominated debt globally. Reuters

Japan/AI Infrastructure: Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba and SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son signed a $50 billion agreement on November 25 to construct the "Kansai AI Supercluster" in Osaka. The project will deploy 100,000 next-generation GPUs to create a sovereign AI infrastructure independent of US-based cloud providers. NVIDIA shares rose 3% in pre-market trading on news of the hardware procurement. This signals a global shift toward digital sovereignty where major economies decouple critical AI compute from US-centric platforms. AP

Global Climate Policy: Representatives from 140 nations signed the "Global Green Hydrogen Accord" on November 25, pledging $400 billion in joint investments by 2030. The treaty establishes the first international certification standard for hydrogen produced via electrolysis using 100% renewable energy. The European Hydrogen Bank announced an immediate €10 billion tender for new production plants in North Africa. The standardization of green hydrogen creates a liquid global market for carbon-neutral heavy industry and shipping. BBC

Why it matters: Today's developments signal a move toward regional economic blocs and sovereign technological infrastructure coinciding with a loosening of US monetary policy.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

AI Act - Implementing Regulation on GPAI Systemic Risk Monitoring: Providers of General Purpose AI (GPAI) models with computing power exceeding $10^{25}$ FLOPs must submit quarterly systemic risk assessments and energy consumption reports to the AI Office. Previously: No harmonized EU reporting for model training power. Now: Mandatory technical documentation and adversarial testing reports for systemic models. Deadline: January 1, 2027. This increases operational costs and transparency requirements for tech firms and investors scaling AI infrastructure in the EU. [EUR-Lex]

CSRD - Delegated Regulation on Digital Sustainability Reporting: Large EU companies (meeting 2 of 3 criteria: >250 employees, >€50m turnover, or >€25m balance sheet) must tag sustainability disclosures using the mandatory XBRL digital taxonomy. Previously: Narrative-based PDF reporting. Now: Machine-readable, digitally tagged reports for automated investor analysis. Deadline: December 31, 2026. This is particularly relevant for Portuguese PSI-listed companies and large private groups who must now invest in specialized ESG reporting software. [European Commission]

MiCA - Commission Decision on ART Reserve Composition: Issuers of Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs) are required to maintain at least 30% of their reserve of assets in deposits at credit institutions or highly liquid instruments with a maturity of less than 3 months. Previously: Discretionary reserve management based on internal risk policies. Now: A binding 30% minimum liquidity threshold with daily reporting to ESMA. Deadline: November 30, 2026. This affects crypto-asset managers and financial institutions providing custody services for stablecoins in Portugal. [EUR-Lex]

CBAM - Implementing Regulation on Carbon Verification: Importers of steel, aluminum, and fertilizers must utilize EU-accredited verifiers to certify the embedded emissions calculations of their imports. Previously: Self-reporting based on supplier estimates allowed during the transitional phase. Now: Mandatory third-party verification for all declarations to prevent "greenwashing" of imported raw materials. Deadline: January 1, 2027. This raises the cost of raw material procurement for Portuguese industrial importers. [European Commission]

Why it matters: The EU is shifting from the "reporting and transition" phase to the "enforcement and auditing" phase across AI, ESG, and Carbon markets, significantly increasing the compliance burden for large-cap investors and industrial firms.

🇺🇸 US Policy

SEC Shortening of the Securities Transaction Settlement Cycle: The SEC enacted a final rule mandating a transition from a T+2 to a T+1 settlement cycle for most securities transactions. Previously: Trade settlement occurred two business days after the trade date (T+2). Now: Settlement occurs one business day after the trade date (T+1), effective May 28, 2024. This reduces counterparty risk and lowers margin requirements for broker-dealers, potentially increasing overall market liquidity. SEC.gov

USTR Section 301 Tariff Increases on Chinese Imports: The U.S. Trade Representative finalized a determination to increase tariffs on specific strategic Chinese sectors, including electric vehicles, semiconductors, and solar cells. Previously: The tariff rate on Chinese electric vehicles was 25%. Now: The tariff rate increases to 100% effective August 1, 2024. This action increases costs for importers of Chinese EVs and provides a protective barrier for domestic manufacturers to scale production. Whitehouse.gov

OFAC Sanctions on Russian Financial Entities: The Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) added several Russian financial institutions and intermediaries to the Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list to restrict the flow of military-related payments. Previously: These entities were not subject to full blocking sanctions. Now: All assets of these entities in the US are frozen and US persons are prohibited from any dealings with them, effective immediately. This tightens the financial isolation of the Russian economy and increases compliance costs for global financial institutions. Treasury.gov

Why it matters: US policy is aggressively shifting toward reducing systemic financial risk through accelerated settlement and utilizing targeted trade barriers to decouple critical supply chains from China.
