The ongoing dispute over transit routes and Iran's assertion of control over the Strait of Hormuz carries significant geopolitical risks for global energy supply, as approximately one-fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas passes through this waterway.

⚡ AI & Productivity

Mistral AI: Mistral AI shipped OCR 4, a significant update to its document intelligence model that extracts and structures content (media, text, tables, equations) as ordered, interleaved text and images, specifically for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. This version provides a structured representation with bounding boxes, block classification, and inline confidence scores, improving upon previous versions that primarily offered clean text extraction. The model supports 170 languages across 10 language groups, processes up to 2,000 pages per minute on a single GPU, and can be self-hosted for $4 per 1,000 pages via API, with discounts for batch processing. Practical impact: Developers can now build more accurate and robust RAG pipelines and AI agents that not only extract raw text but also understand the structure and context of documents, enabling automated workflows like intelligent form filling or compliance checks with built-in citation capabilities.

Google DeepMind (Gemini 3.5 Flash): Google DeepMind integrated a native "computer use" capability directly into its Gemini 3.5 Flash model, enabling AI agents to perceive and interact with graphical user interfaces across browsers, mobile, and desktops. This new feature allows agents to take screenshots, interpret visual elements, click buttons, fill forms, and operate applications without requiring bespoke API integrations. This is a built-in capability, offering seamless agentic UI interaction, unlike previous methods that relied on external tools or custom API wrappers. Practical impact: Knowledge workers can now deploy AI agents to automate complex, multi-application tasks that previously required human supervision, such as automatically navigating a website to extract specific data, inputting it into an enterprise resource planning (ERP) system, and generating a summary report.

OpenAI (ChatGPT - GPT-5.5 Instant): OpenAI released an update to GPT-5.5 Instant, the default model in ChatGPT, significantly enhancing its conversational quality, context handling, and instruction following. This update improves the model's ability to identify underlying user goals and maintain context across multi-turn conversations, while more reliably adhering to complex instructions with multiple constraints. It replaces GPT-5.3 Instant, delivering improvements in accuracy, clarity, conciseness, image understanding, and performance on STEM questions, alongside reduced hallucinations and more cohesive product and local recommendations. Practical impact: Users of ChatGPT will experience more intuitive and effective interactions for tasks requiring nuanced understanding and adherence to detailed requirements, such as collaborating on a complex research plan that involves multiple interdependencies and conditional logic, receiving responses that address all constraints without needing repeated clarification.

xAI (Grok 4.3 on Amazon Bedrock & Grok for Word): xAI made Grok 4.3 generally available on Amazon Bedrock, providing AWS users with a frontier AI model featuring a 1-million-token context window, configurable reasoning levels (none, low, medium, high), and an industry-leading low hallucination rate for enterprise AI applications. 🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Regulamentos e Tabelas de Preços do Serviço Nacional de Saúde (SNS): A Portaria n.º 274/2026/1, de 25 de junho, aprova os novos Regulamentos e Tabelas de Preços para as instituições e serviços integrados no Serviço Nacional de Saúde (SNS). Esta atualização inclui a revisão da nomenclatura, criação e eliminação de códigos, e ajuste de ponderadores para tabelas de Meios Complementares de Diagnóstico e Terapêutica (MCDT). Anteriormente, vigoravam regulamentos e tabelas mais antigas; agora, as regras foram revistas, incluindo a estipulação de que, em casos de reinternamento no mesmo hospital num período de 72 horas, apenas o Grupo de Diagnósticos Homogéneos (GDH) do último internamento é cobrado, salvo exceções para episódios clinicamente não relacionados ou de foro oncológico. O impacto prático é uma alteração na forma como os serviços de saúde são classificados e faturados, podendo influenciar os custos para os utentes e exigindo adaptação por parte dos prestadores de cuidados.

Registo Automático de Novas Empresas na Segurança Social: A partir de 25 de junho de 2026, a constituição de uma nova empresa no Instituto dos Registos e do Notariado (IRN) passará a gerar automaticamente o seu registo no Portal da Segurança Social. Previamente, os empresários tinham de efetuar o registo da empresa na Segurança Social de forma manual após a sua constituição. Esta medida elimina a necessidade de preenchimento de formulários adicionais ou deslocações, sendo enviado um código de verificação para a sede social da empresa para ativar a conta. O impacto prático é uma significativa redução da burocracia e do tempo necessário para os empreendedores iniciarem a sua atividade, simplificando os procedimentos administrativos.

Regime Jurídico do Contrato de Aproveitamento Energético Renovável e Licenciamento de UPAC: A Lei n.º 29/2026, de 23 de junho, institui o regime jurídico do Contrato de Aproveitamento Energético Renovável (CAER) e determina o deferimento tácito no licenciamento de unidades de produção para autoconsumo (UPAC) a partir de fontes renováveis. Anteriormente, não existia um quadro legal tão formalizado para a cedência de direitos de aproveitamento energético de imóveis (como solos urbanos não construídos ou telhados), nem o licenciamento de UPAC beneficiava de deferimento tácito. O impacto prático é o fomento da produção descentralizada de eletricidade renovável, oferecendo novas oportunidades para proprietários gerarem rendimento e simplificando os processos administrativos para a instalação de sistemas de autoconsumo.

Criação da Prestação Social Única (PSU): A Assembleia da República aprovou, a 25 de junho de 2026, a criação da Prestação Social Única (PSU), que agregará 13 prestações sociais não contributivas existentes. Anteriormente, estes apoios eram geridos e concedidos de forma separada. O acesso à PSU dependerá de condições de recursos, idade e situação profissional, incluindo a inscrição em centro de emprego e a disponibilidade para formação profissional ou atividades de solidariedade social para adultos em idade ativa. Para nacionais de Estados fora da União Europeia ou Espaço Económico Europeu, é exigido um período mínimo de residência legal de um ano. O valor de referência específico da PSU será definido posteriormente por decreto-lei. O impacto prático é a simplificação do sistema de apoios sociais, embora com a introdução de novos requisitos de elegibilidade e obrigações para os beneficiários.

Diferimento do Pagamento de Contribuições à Segurança Social para Operadores de Transporte de Mercadorias: A Portaria n.º 266/2026/1, de 18 de junho (com entrada em vigor a 19 de junho de 2026), fixa os procedimentos para a medida excecional e temporária de diferimento do pagamento das contribuições à Segurança Social devidas nos meses de maio, junho e julho de 2026. Esta medida destina-se a operadores de transporte de mercadorias por conta de outrem que utilizem combustíveis fósseis e possuam inspeção periódica válida. Anteriormente, as contribuições seriam pagas nos prazos habituais (entre os dias 1 e 25 do mês seguinte). O impacto prático é o alívio temporário da pressão financeira sobre o setor de transporte rodoviário de mercadorias, auxiliando na gestão de tesouraria face aos impactos dos custos com combustíveis.

Why it matters: As novas medidas visam simplificar processos burocráticos para empresas, apoiar a transição energética e reorganizar o sistema de apoios sociais, com um impacto direto nas finanças e obrigações de cidadãos e negócios em Portugal.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Directive on the Distance Marketing of Consumer Financial Services (revised): Previously, existing rules for distance selling of financial services had varying degrees of consumer protection. Now, this revised Directive, applying from June 19, 2026, imposes stricter requirements on financial service providers. They must offer more comprehensive pre-contractual information, clearly detailing service characteristics, total price, withdrawal rights, and provider details. Furthermore, consumers gain the right to human intervention when interacting through online tools, ensuring they can contact a person for explanations in the same language as the pre-contractual information, even after the agreement is concluded in justified cases. This significantly enhances consumer protection in online financial transactions across the EU, directly impacting Portuguese financial services businesses operating at a distance.

Directive (EU) 2023/2673 on the "Withdrawal Button": Previously, consumers often faced varied and sometimes complex processes to exercise their right of withdrawal for online purchases and subscriptions. Now, from June 19, 2026, businesses selling products or services online to EU consumers must provide a clear and easily accessible digital withdrawal function directly on their website or app, ensuring that cancelling a contract is as straightforward as entering into one. This obligation applies to all online purchases and subscription-based services where EU withdrawal rights apply, streamlining the cancellation process for consumers and requiring all online businesses, including those in Portugal, to update their digital interfaces.

EU-US Trade Deal Legislation: Previously, certain US industrial goods faced import duties when entering the EU, and specific US seafood and agricultural products did not have preferential market access. Now, EU governments formally adopted legislation on June 25, 2026, to remove import duties on all US industrial goods and grant preferential market access for a wide range of US seafood and agricultural goods, including extending duty-free imports for US lobster. This legislation, which will take effect upon its publication in the Official Journal, directly benefits EU importers and exporters, including those in Portugal, by reducing costs and administrative burdens associated with transatlantic trade.

Why it matters: This week saw the entry into force of critical EU consumer protection measures for online transactions and financial services, alongside the formal adoption of a significant trade agreement with the US, streamlining cross-border commerce.

Executive Order "Advancing Regenerative Agriculture and Strengthening American Farm Resilience" and USDA Final Rule: On June 25, 2026, President Trump signed an Executive Order to advance regenerative agriculture, reinforcing the administration's commitment to supporting American farmers. Simultaneously, U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) Secretary Brooke L. Rollins announced a final Regenerative Feedstock Rule. The Executive Order directs HHS, USDA, and EPA to bolster research, innovation, and public-private partnerships for regenerative agriculture, and to develop a research framework for cumulative chemical exposures in the food supply. The USDA rule is designed to enable farmers to gain new value from regenerative agricultural practices through biofuel markets, establishing standards for feedstock crops, carbon intensity quantification, chain-of-custody, auditing, and regenerative practice standards. Previously, such a comprehensive federal framework for supporting regenerative agriculture through biofuel markets was not in place. This action signals a federal commitment to market-driven environmental practices in agriculture, potentially boosting the biofuel sector and creating new revenue streams for farmers adopting regenerative methods.

Treasury Sanctions on Illicit Mineral Trade Network and Cuban Entities: On June 25, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) imposed sanctions on a network, including Rwanda's Gasabo Gold Refinery Ltd., for its role in illegally smuggling minerals from eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) to Rwanda in coordination with the March 23 Movement (M23). These sanctions block all property and interests of designated individuals and entities under U.S. jurisdiction and prohibit transactions with them. Separately, on June 25, 2026, the U.S. announced new sanctions targeting five key Cuban state-owned companies, including financial, logistics, and mining firms (such as GeoMinera), and an individual connected to the Castro family, Annalie Lilliam Rueda Cardero, under Executive Order 14404. Previously, these specific entities and individual were not directly subjected to these U.S. sanctions. These actions intensify U.S. foreign policy pressure on actors fueling conflict in the DRC and supporting the Cuban government, potentially impacting commodity markets (gold, minerals) and financial transactions related to Cuba.

OFAC General License No. 60 for Venezuela Earthquake Relief: On June 25, 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) issued General License No. 60 (GL 60), authorizing transactions related to earthquake relief efforts in Venezuela that would otherwise be prohibited under the Venezuela Sanctions Regulations (31 CFR Part 591). This license is effective until October 23, 2026. Prior to GL 60, such transactions involving the Government of Venezuela (GOV) and Specially Designated Nationals (SDNs) under the Venezuela Sanctions Regulations were generally prohibited. GL 60 permits U.S. persons to engage in these transactions and allows U.S. financial institutions to process related payments from third countries. This action represents a targeted humanitarian easing of sanctions, potentially facilitating aid flow to Venezuela and signaling a willingness for conditional engagement in humanitarian crises despite broader sanctions.

US-Australia Customs Mutual Assistance Agreement: On June 25, 2026, U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), on behalf of the United States, signed a Customs Mutual Assistance Agreement (CMAA) with Australia in Brussels, Belgium. Once in force, this bilateral agreement will enable both countries to exchange information and provide mutual assistance on customs offenses, including duty evasion, trafficking, proliferation, money laundering, and terrorism-related activities, and to support judicial proceedings. Previously, formal mutual assistance on customs matters between the two nations was not codified under such a specific bilateral agreement. This agreement strengthens international trade security and law enforcement cooperation, potentially reducing illicit trade and improving the efficiency of legitimate trade flows between the two countries.

Why it matters: The U.S. government has taken several concrete steps this week to simultaneously advance domestic agricultural policy through environmental incentives, exert targeted foreign policy pressure through sanctions, and facilitate international humanitarian aid and trade security.
