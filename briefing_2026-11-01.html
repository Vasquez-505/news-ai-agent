Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2391 on Registration of Cellulose Ether Imports: This regulation requires businesses importing certain cellulose ethers from the People's Republic of China and the Republic of Korea to register these imports with customs authorities. Previously, these specific imports did not require such registration. Now, this measure facilitates the potential future imposition of anti-dumping duties, allowing any such duties to be levied retroactively from the date of registration. Affected businesses, particularly those in the chemical and manufacturing sectors, must comply with the registration requirements as of October 29, 2026. This allows the EU to track imports that may be subject to future trade defence measures.

Council Regulation (EU) 2026/2446 Amending Fishery Tariff Quotas: This regulation modifies the existing autonomous Union tariff quotas for specific fishery products for the period 2024-2026. Previously, Regulation (EU) 2023/2720 set the volumes for duty-free or reduced-duty imports of certain fishery products. This amendment adjusts those established quotas, impacting the quantities of various fishery products that can be imported into the EU at preferential tariffs. Businesses involved in the import of fishery products into the EU are affected by these updated quotas, which apply retroactively from January 1, 2024, to the 2024-2026 period, despite formal adoption on October 26, 2026.

Updated EU Rules on Social Security Coordination: This Regulation, formally adopted by the European Parliament on July 7, 2026, and the Council of the EU on September 28, 2026, entered into force today, November 1, 2026. It aims to make social security coordination clearer and fairer across Member States. Previously, existing rules on social security coordination were in place; the new rules introduce a coherent regime for long-term care benefits, new family benefit rules, a clearer definition of fraud in social security, and specific conditions for posting workers, including a requirement for at least three months of affiliation in the home country before posting and a two-month break after 24 months of posting before another. Most of the updated rules will apply from November 1, 2028, to allow Member States to adapt national procedures. This affects Portuguese businesses that post workers to other EU countries and Portuguese citizens working or residing in other EU Member States, ensuring stronger protection of their social security rights.

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2152 on Union Handling Fee for Distance Sales: This regulation, published on October 22, 2026, introduces a new €2 handling fee per item on all packages entering the EU market through distance sales, effective today, November 1, 2026. This is in addition to the €3 duty on small parcels (under €150) that came into force in July. Previously, only the temporary €3 duty applied to small parcels. Now, platforms, sellers, or carriers are responsible for paying this €2 fee for every item, regardless of its value, to cover customs control costs. This change will likely increase the cost of goods purchased by Portuguese consumers from non-EU online platforms and impact Portuguese businesses importing products from outside the EU for distance sales.

Why it matters: The EU is implementing new measures to address the costs and complexities of cross-border trade and labor mobility, impacting businesses importing goods and those employing workers across EU borders, and introducing new fees for e-commerce imports.
