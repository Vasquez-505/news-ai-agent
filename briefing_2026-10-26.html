These standards, which replace fragmented national AML rules, will become applicable from July 10, 2027, under the broader Anti-Money Laundering Regulation (AMLR). Previously, CASPs navigated a patchwork of national anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) regulations and the Transfer of Funds Regulation; now, they must prepare to comply with a unified, directly applicable EU rulebook. This mandates all CASPs, including those in Portugal, to implement robust customer due diligence processes.

Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act) – Imminent Transparency and Prohibitions: Following the entry into force of the AI Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) on July 27, 2026, specific transparency obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act will apply to *legacy* AI systems already on the market starting December 2, 2026. On this same date, new categories of prohibited AI uses, including non-consensual intimate imagery and AI-generated child sexual abuse material, will become fully enforceable. Previously, these precise transparency requirements did not extend to existing legacy systems, and these specific prohibited uses were not explicitly defined within the Act's initial scope. Companies developing or deploying AI in the EU, including Portuguese businesses, must ensure their existing AI systems meet transparency rules and immediately cease any use of AI for the newly prohibited activities to avoid penalties up to EUR 35 million or 7% of global revenue.

Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) – Revised Timelines and Scope: The European Commission's "Omnibus I simplification package," finalized in 2026, officially extended the deadline for EU Member States to transpose the CSDDD into national law from July 26, 2026, to July 26, 2028. Consequently, the company-level applicability for the largest firms (those with over 5,000 employees and €1.5 billion in turnover, or non-EU companies generating that turnover within the EU) will now begin in July 2029, a delay from earlier proposed dates. Additionally, the scope has been narrowed, excluding most mid-sized businesses previously anticipated to be covered. Previously, Member States faced a July 2026 transposition deadline, with a broader scope and earlier application dates for companies. This provides EU Member States, including Portugal, and affected companies with additional time to prepare for the directive's requirements to identify and address human rights and environmental impacts across their value chains.

These new measures will establish binding sustainability and performance requirements, including those related to emissions reduction, energy and water efficiency, and circularity. Previously, the Ecodesign Directive (2009/125/EC) primarily addressed energy-related products, lacking specific EU-wide ecodesign standards for intermediate goods like iron and steel. Manufacturers, importers, and distributors of iron and steel in the EU, including Portuguese firms, must now prepare for these detailed ecodesign requirements, which will reshape product design and supply chain operations towards greater sustainability.
