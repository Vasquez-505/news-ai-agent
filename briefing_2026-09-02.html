🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Alterations to the Regime for the Entry, Stay, Exit, and Removal of Foreigners from National Territory and Asylum Concession (Lei de Estrangeiros): This new regime, promulgated on August 31, 2026, and entering into force upon publication in Diário da República (anticipated September 1, 2026), significantly restricts avenues for foreign citizens to obtain residence permits without a prior visa. Previously, individuals could seek regularization by enrolling in certain studies (including professional training) or by having minor foreign children residing in Portugal (even if the children were also foreign). Now, these specific pathways for obtaining residency without a prior visa are eliminated, though the exception for parents of *Portuguese national minors* remains. Practical impact: Foreign citizens seeking residency in Portugal will generally be required to obtain an appropriate visa in their country of origin before entering Portugal, limiting post-arrival regularization options.

Lei n.º 58/2026, de 24 de agosto, on the Prohibition of Face Concealment in Public Places: This law, published on August 24, 2026, and entering into force 30 days after its publication (around September 23, 2026), prohibits the concealment of the face in public places. Previously, there was no general national prohibition on face concealment. The new law specifies exceptions for reasons of security, climatic conditions, or in places like aircraft, diplomatic premises, or places of worship. Violations are subject to fines ranging from €150 to €750 for negligence and €400 to €3000 for intent, with forcing someone to conceal their face due to gender carrying a prison sentence of up to 2 years or a fine of up to 240 days. Practical impact: From late September 2026, citizens will generally be required to show their face in public, affecting individuals who previously concealed their face for non-exempt reasons.

Regime Simplificado de Arrendamento Acessível (RSAA) and Contratos de Investimento para Arrendamento (CIA) (Housing Package): These new housing regimes, which entered into force on September 1, 2026, replace the previous Programa de Apoio ao Arrendamento with enhanced incentives to increase affordable housing supply. Under the RSAA, landlords (individuals or companies) renting properties under the accessible renting regime now benefit from total exemption from IRS and IRC on rental income. The CIA regime, aimed at larger investors constructing, rehabilitating, or acquiring properties for rent, offers exemptions from IMT, Stamp Duty, IMI (for up to 8 years, then a 50% reduction for up to 10 years), and reduced IVA on construction, provided rents do not exceed €2,300/month. Additionally, tenants can now deduct up to €900 in IRS for rents paid in 2026, increasing to €1000 from 2027. Practical impact: Landlords receive significant tax breaks for providing housing at moderate rents, aiming to increase the supply of affordable rental properties, while tenants benefit from increased tax deductions on rental payments.

Why it matters: The most significant change for Portuguese citizens and businesses is the set of new housing policies that came into effect on September 1, 2026, providing substantial tax incentives for landlords to offer affordable housing and increased tax deductions for tenants.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1119 on ESG Rating Providers: This Regulation specifies the detailed information ESG rating providers must include in their applications for authorisation to operate in the EU or for recognition as third-country providers. Previously, the overarching Regulation (EU) 2024/3005 established the framework for ESG rating providers, but the specific technical standards for application content were not yet defined. Now, providers must submit granular details regarding their methodologies, data sources, internal controls, governance structures, and measures to manage conflicts of interest, enabling the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) to assess compliance and integrity. This directly affects ESG rating providers operating or seeking to operate in the EU, requiring immediate compilation of comprehensive documentation. This enhanced transparency and reliability of ESG ratings benefits all investors, including Portuguese investors, by providing a clearer picture of sustainability performance.

EU AI Act - Transparency Obligations (Article 50): Providers of Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems that interact directly with natural persons (e.g., chatbots, voice assistants) must inform users they are engaging with an AI, unless it is already obvious. Furthermore, providers of AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic audio, image, video, or text content must ensure these outputs are machine-readable and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals, and deployers of deepfakes and AI-generated text on public-interest matters must disclose the artificial generation, unless substantive human editorial review has taken place. Previously, there were no specific EU-wide transparency obligations for AI content. These obligations became applicable on August 2, 2026. A limited transitional period allows generative AI systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, until December 2, 2026, to implement the machine-readable marking and detection obligations. This requires tech companies and businesses deploying AI in the EU to immediately reassess their product design and user interfaces to ensure compliance, with non-compliance potentially leading to fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover.

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40): This Regulation harmonises packaging rules across all 27 EU Member States. Previously, the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (94/62/EC) allowed Member States discretion in implementation, leading to diverse national rules. Now, from August 12, 2026, companies must ensure that all packaging placed on the EU market complies with a single set of rules, including holding documentation such as the EU Declaration of Conformity and Technical Documentation, and adhering to strict limits on substances like PFAS and heavy metals. Importers from outside the EU must appoint an authorised representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State to which they export. This significant change impacts manufacturing, import, and distribution companies across all sectors, requiring potential overhauls in packaging design, material sourcing, and compliance procedures.

Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) - Vulnerability and Incident Reporting Obligations (Article 14): Manufacturers of products with digital elements (PDEs) will be required to report actively exploited cybersecurity vulnerabilities and severe security incidents affecting their products. Previously, there was no mandatory EU-wide reporting regime for such cybersecurity events for PDEs. From September 11, 2026, manufacturers must submit an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of an event, followed by a detailed notification within 72 hours. A final report is due within 14 days for vulnerabilities (once a corrective measure is available) or one month for severe incidents, to be submitted via the CRA's Single Reporting Platform. This mandates robust internal processes for identifying, assessing, and reporting cybersecurity risks throughout the product lifecycle for all affected businesses.
