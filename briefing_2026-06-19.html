This progress signifies accelerating commercial viability and substantial long-term growth potential for the industry.

Gold & Silver: Precious metals are declining after the US Federal Reserve hinted at potential interest rate hikes, outweighing any positive sentiment from a recent US-Iran peace agreement. The Fed's hawkish stance, which included removing language suggesting future policy easing and shifting the median FOMC projection to one rate hike before year-end, increases the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets. This macro shift, coupled with a surging US Dollar Index, creates significant headwinds for gold and silver.

Oil & Gas (XLE, XOP): Oil prices and energy ETFs are under pressure following decisions by seven OPEC+ countries to increase collective production by 188,000 barrels per day (bbl/d) starting in June 2026, unwinding previous voluntary cuts. Additionally, improving shipping conditions through the Strait of Hormuz after the US-Iran interim peace deal have eased concerns about supply disruptions. This combination of increased supply and reduced geopolitical risk is driving the sustained bearish trend in the sector.

🌍 World News

United States-Iran: The United States and Iran formally signed a 14-point interim Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on June 17, 2026, following President Donald Trump's meeting with French President Emmanuel Macron at the Palace of Versailles, with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian also signing the document. This agreement aims to establish a 60-day ceasefire to facilitate further negotiations, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and provide a framework for potential sanctions relief and a $300 billion reconstruction fund for Iran. The MoU calls for the immediate termination of military operations on all fronts, including in Lebanon, and grants Iran a temporary waiver to export oil during the next 60 days, leading to three Saudi-flagged supertankers with six million barrels of crude onboard sailing through the Strait of Hormuz hours after the signing. This development significantly impacts global energy markets by reopening a critical maritime chokepoint and could pave the way for a broader peace deal, reducing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, though it has drawn criticism from members of Trump's own party and neither Israel nor Hezbollah have signed on to its Lebanon provisions. (Reuters/AP)

Gaza Humanitarian Crisis: UN Under-Secretary-General for Humanitarian Affairs Tom Fletcher briefed the Security Council on June 18, 2026, on the dire humanitarian conditions in Gaza, reporting that 70% of the population requires proper shelter and nearly 1,000 Palestinians have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire. Despite reduced active fighting since UN Security Council resolution 2803, essential services are on the brink, and 1.1 million children face daily water uncertainty. While Gaza is no longer classified as being in famine (IPC Phase 5), it remains in a severe crisis (IPC Phase 4), with 36% of households still going to bed hungry, and over 1,000 incidents of settler violence have been recorded in the West Bank in 2026 so far. This persistent humanitarian crisis underscores the failure of international efforts to fully stabilize the region, threatens broader regional stability, and requires sustained global attention and aid for vulnerable populations. (UN News/ReliefWeb)

Sudan: UN Secretary-General António Guterres expressed deep concern on June 18, 2026, regarding the escalating conflict in and around El Obeid, North Kordofan State, Sudan, citing drone attacks impacting civilians and civilian infrastructure. He specifically highlighted reports of the deployment of substantial military reinforcements by the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) around El Obeid. This military buildup indicates a potential imminent ground offensive on the city, which would place El Obeid, a crucial hub for humanitarian response efforts, at grave risk of large-scale violence and further displacement. The potential for a major ground offensive in El Obeid risks a significant deterioration of Sudan's already catastrophic humanitarian crisis, increasing regional instability, and threatening a vital pathway for international aid. (UN News)

Eastern Africa Energy Investment: On June 18, 2026, the World Bank Group Board approved a $1.6 billion financing package for the 10-year Regional Energy Transmission, Trade & Decarbonization program for Eastern Africa (RETRADE-EA). This initiative aims to accelerate regional power integration, expand energy access, and unlock economic opportunity across Eastern Africa. The first phase of RETRADE-EA will support the Uganda-Tanzania interconnector and strengthen regional institutions, with projections for at least 452 gigawatt-hours of electricity to be traded annually between Uganda and Tanzania by 2031, and a reduction of 25.8 million metric tons of CO2 emissions across the wider Eastern Africa Power Pool. This substantial investment in sustainable energy infrastructure promises to boost economic growth, improve energy security, and contribute significantly to global decarbonization efforts across Eastern Africa, setting a precedent for regional development projects. (World Bank Group)

Diferimento de Contribuições à Segurança Social: A Portaria n.º 266/2026/1, publicada em 18 de junho, estabelece que operadores de transporte elegíveis podem diferir o pagamento das contribuições à segurança social referentes aos meses de maio, junho e julho de 2026, até 25 de outubro de 2026, sem juros de mora. Esta medida visa aliviar a pressão financeira sobre os transportadores afetados pelo aumento dos custos dos combustíveis e do AdBlue.

Regulamentação do Imposto Mínimo Global (RIMG): A Portaria n.º 255/2026/1, de 12 de junho, aprovou o modelo oficial para a declaração de informação sobre o imposto complementar no âmbito do Regime do Imposto Mínimo Global (RIMG), transpondo diretivas da UE. Grandes grupos multinacionais e nacionais devem agora submeter eletronicamente (em formato XML, Modelo 63 - GIR) estas informações à Autoridade Tributária e Aduaneira. Anteriormente, não existia um modelo oficial e padronizado para esta declaração específica em Portugal. Esta uniformização facilita o cumprimento das novas obrigações fiscais internacionais para as empresas abrangidas.

Novas Exigências para Vistos de Estudante (Entrada em vigor em Setembro): O Conselho de Ministros aprovou em 17 de junho novas regulamentações para vistos de estudante, que entrarão em vigor em setembro de 2026. As novas regras endurecem a fiscalização e aumentam as exigências financeiras, exigindo comprovação formal de matrícula em instituição reconhecida pelo Ministério da Educação português e apresentação obrigatória de seguro de saúde. Anteriormente, os requisitos para o visto estudantil poderiam ser menos rigorosos ou ter uma aplicação menos estrita. Esta alteração deverá ter um impacto direto em milhares de estudantes internacionais, exigindo maior preparação e capacidade financeira.

Pacote Fiscal para a Habitação (Benefícios em vigor em 2026): O Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026, de 20 de maio, embora publicado em maio, tem medidas com efeitos em 2026. A partir de 2026, o limite anual da dedução de rendas no IRS para inquilinos é aumentado para 900€ (e para 1.000€ em 2027), face a limites anteriores mais baixos. Além disso, a taxa de IVA reduzida de 6% aplica-se a empreitadas de construção e reabilitação de imóveis para habitação própria permanente ou arrendamento acessível (com limites de valor de venda até 660.982€ ou renda até 2.300€/mês), o que antes estava sujeito à taxa normal de IVA (23%). Há também isenção de IMT para a aquisição da primeira habitação própria e permanente cujo valor não exceda 330.539€. Estas medidas visam aliviar a carga fiscal de inquilinos e dinamizar o investimento na oferta de habitação acessível.

Entrada em vigor do Pacto da UE sobre Migração e Asilo: Em 12 de junho, entrou em vigor o novo Pacto da União Europeia sobre Migração e Asilo. Este pacto estabelece procedimentos de asilo mais rápidos e regras mais rigorosas para pedidos abusivos, bem como critérios mais claros sobre a responsabilidade dos Estados-Membros no processamento de pedidos de asilo. Anteriormente, existia uma menor harmonização e regras mais díspares entre os Estados-Membros. Portugal, como membro da UE, está agora sujeito a estas novas diretrizes, o que implica processos mais padronizados para requerentes de asilo e migrantes.

Why it matters: O pacote de medidas para a habitação, com benefícios fiscais como o IVA a 6% em obras e o aumento das deduções de IRS para rendas, e as alterações às regras de vistos de estudante, representam as mudanças mais significativas com impacto direto nos cidadãos e empresas portuguesas, moldando o acesso à habitação e o perfil da imigração.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

CBAM Verification Standard Decision (EU 2026/412): Importers of high-carbon goods must now utilize accredited third-party verifiers for all emissions reports. Previously: Self-reporting of estimated emissions was permitted during the transitional phase (2023–2025). Now: Mandatory verified reports are required for all imports of steel, aluminum, and cement exceeding 2,500 tonnes per annum, effective for the June 30, 2026 quarterly filing. This increases operational costs for Portuguese construction and manufacturing firms importing raw materials from non-EU countries. [Official Journal of the EU]

AI Act - High-Risk System Implementing Regulation (EU 2026/588): Providers of AI systems used in employment and worker management must implement a mandatory human-oversight interface and a detailed risk-logging system. Previously: No specific technical standards for HR-AI existed beyond general transparency obligations. Now: Mandatory "Human-in-the-loop" (HITL) verification is required for all AI-driven hiring decisions in companies with >250 employees, with a compliance deadline of August 2, 2026. This creates a new technical compliance burden for Portuguese HR-tech startups and large corporate recruiters. [European Commission Press Release]

MiCA Liquidity Reserve Amendment (EU 2026/701): Issuers of Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs) must increase their liquid reserve holdings to ensure redemption stability. Previously: A minimum liquidity reserve of 20% of the total reserve assets was required. Now: A minimum liquidity reserve of 30% is required for all ARTs with a market capitalization exceeding €100 million, effective June 30, 2026. This affects EU-based crypto-asset issuers and potentially reduces the yield available to Portuguese retail investors in stablecoins. [EUR-Lex]

Why it matters: The transition from reporting to strict enforcement for carbon imports and AI systems marks a shift toward high-cost, mandatory compliance for EU importers and tech providers.
