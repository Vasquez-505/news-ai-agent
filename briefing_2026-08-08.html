AI Act Transparency Obligations (Article 50): Providers of AI systems designed for direct interaction with natural persons must now inform users that they are interacting with an AI. Previously, no such explicit EU-wide transparency obligations were in force. Additionally, providers of generative AI systems that produce synthetic audio, image, video, or text content must ensure their outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and are clearly identifiable as artificially generated or manipulated. Deployers who distribute deepfakes or publish AI-generated texts for public interest must also disclose this fact. These obligations became applicable on August 2, 2026. For generative AI systems already on the market before August 2, 2026, a transitional period allows until December 2, 2026, to implement these technical labelling measures. Non-compliance can result in administrative fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. This significantly impacts companies developing or deploying AI systems that interact with users or generate content, including Portuguese tech businesses.

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40): From August 12, 2026, all packaging placed on the EU market must be recyclable and comply with new substance restrictions. Previously, packaging rules were based on Directive 94/62/EC, which allowed for varying national implementations. Now, companies must ensure their packaging has a total concentration of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium not exceeding 100 mg/kg, and adhere to limits for per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging. Furthermore, importers from outside the EU must appoint an authorized representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State to which they export. This regulation directly affects manufacturers, importers, and distributors across the EU, including those in Portugal, by standardizing compliance requirements for packaging.

e-Evidence Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1543): The core provisions of this regulation will become applicable on August 18, 2026. Previously, law enforcement authorities seeking electronic evidence from service providers in other Member States typically relied on traditional mutual legal assistance requests, such as European Investigation Orders. With the new regulation, judicial authorities will be able to directly request the transmission of electronic data from service providers in other Member States using a European Production Order (EPOC). This streamlines cross-border access to electronic evidence, impacting service providers operating across the EU.

Recovery and Resilience Facility Disbursement to Portugal: On August 7, 2026, the European Commission disbursed €2.32 billion (net of pre-financing) to Portugal as the ninth payment under the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF).
