🌍 World News

Ethiopia-Eritrea: Ethiopia officially closed its embassy in Eritrea's capital, Asmara, and ordered 10 Eritrean diplomats to leave Addis Ababa within 48 hours on Thursday, October 2, 2026, accusing Eritrea of "direct and indirect acts of hostility and belligerence" that threaten Ethiopia's national security. Eritrea responded by calling the move "absurd" and vowed to reciprocate, asserting it had "no option but to reciprocate in kind and sever all diplomatic ties." This rupture formalizes the breakdown of a "cold peace" between the two Horn of Africa nations, which were allies in the 2020-22 Tigray war but have seen tensions rise over various issues, including sea access. This diplomatic breakdown raises fears that the conflict in Ethiopia, particularly with the recent fighting in northern Ethiopia, could escalate into a direct regional war involving Eritrea, potentially destabilizing the wider Horn of Africa and Red Sea region. [Reuters, AP, Arab News, Anadolu Agency]

Yemen Conflict: Yemeni government forces have launched strikes on Houthi-held Sanaa, with the Saudi-backed Yemeni army claiming to have killed approximately 700 Houthi fighters in the last 24 hours while closing a key crossing connecting Houthi-held al-Houban to government-held Taiz city. This escalation follows an earlier claim by Houthis of striking a Saudi Aramco site in Riyadh. The intensified fighting has led to a new wave of displacement, with more than 132,000 people already having fled since hostilities reignited in July, and families fleeing toward Djibouti in overcrowded boats. The escalating conflict not only deepens a severe humanitarian crisis in Yemen but also threatens the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a strategic chokepoint for global oil shipping, which could further destabilize global energy markets if shipping routes are disrupted. [Al Jazeera, Deutsche Welle]

G7 Nations: The Group of Seven (G7) nations agreed to coordinate the release of 100 million barrels of diesel and crude oil from emergency stockpiles over the next four months, following pressure from US President Donald Trump. Trump had threatened to cut off diesel exports to Europe if they did not release reserves to help bring down surging fuel prices. This decision aims to alleviate global diesel shortages and high fuel prices, which have been exacerbated by global conflicts and strained refining capacity, with one fuel price watcher suggesting prices could drop by as much as 18 to 20 cents per liter. The coordinated release is a significant international economic intervention designed to stabilize global energy markets, directly impacting inflation pressures and economic activity worldwide. [Global News]

DR Congo Ebola: A new drug trial has enrolled over 250 participants in the Democratic Republic of Congo for a treatment specifically targeting the Bundibugyo virus, which is responsible for the country's deadliest Ebola outbreak. This outbreak, declared in mid-May in eastern Congo, has already resulted in at least 8,300 confirmed cases and 4,000 deaths. The trial represents a critical step in the fight against this fast-moving and virulent strain of Ebola, for which no approved vaccine or treatment currently exists, offering a potential new tool for control and prevention. This scientific breakthrough is crucial for containing a major humanitarian crisis and mitigating the risk of international spread of a severe infectious disease. [AP News, PBS News]

Taiwan: Taiwan received the first two of 66 new F-16V Block 70 fighter jets from the United States on Friday, October 2, 2026, which landed at Taitung's Chihhang Air Base after months of delays. The delivery strengthens Taiwan's air force, which frequently scrambles to monitor China's military activity. The arrival of these advanced aircraft bolsters Taiwan's air defense capabilities against the rising military threat from Beijing, which claims the democratically governed island as its own territory. This arms delivery is a significant development in the delicate geopolitical balance of the Indo-Pacific, impacting US-China relations and the security outlook for a critical global trade route. [Reuters, Defense News]

AI developers can now objectively and consistently verify the operational reliability of their agents, ensuring, for example, that an agent designed to manage inventory accurately updates stock levels in a database every time, preventing silent failures.

Developers and knowledge workers can now streamline their coding workflows by utilizing DeepSeek's AI models for autonomous code generation and task execution within a single, integrated platform without incurring additional software subscription fees.

OpenAI Dots: OpenAI introduced "Dots," always-on AI agents designed to operate persistently in the background, autonomously handling a wide array of tasks. This product signifies OpenAI's strategic shift towards embedding AI directly into daily workflows, aiming to control the entire AI workflow rather than just providing access to models. Directive (EU) 2024/825 on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition: Previously, businesses could use vague or unsubstantiated environmental claims and sustainability labels. This Directive entered into application on September 27, 2026. Compliance is mandatory for all companies selling products to consumers in the EU, including those established outside the EU, with infringements potentially leading to fines of at least 4% of annual turnover. This significantly impacts businesses across all sectors by requiring stricter adherence to truthfulness in environmental marketing and transparent consumer information, necessitating that Portuguese businesses immediately review and update all their environmental claims and consumer information.

Now, the Council has formally adopted revised rules focusing on unemployment benefits, long-term care benefits, access to welfare benefits for economically inactive persons, family benefits, and applicable legislation for mobile workers. Crucially, for posting situations, A1 applications must be filed *before* work in the destination country begins, shifting from retrospective to prospective compliance. This Regulation entered into force upon its publication in the Official Journal on September 28, 2026, with the revised coordination rules for applicable legislation expected to apply in approximately two years. This impacts employers with mobile workforces, including Portuguese companies with staff working in other EU Member States, by introducing stricter procedural requirements for social security coordination.
