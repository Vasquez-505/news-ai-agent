🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 amending Air Passenger Rights (EC No 261/2004): Previously, passengers faced less clarity on rights and compensation, particularly regarding long delays, extraordinary circumstances, and specific clauses like "no-show." Now, this Regulation clarifies that passengers whose flight is delayed, whether in the airport terminal or already on board the aircraft, are entitled to assistance and compensation. It provides a clearer definition of "extraordinary circumstances" to enhance legal certainty for both airlines and passengers. Airlines must inform passengers of their rights at the time of booking, especially concerning missed connecting flights, and provide plain-language explanations for flight disruptions within seven days. The new rules introduce a 9-month window for passengers to file compensation claims with the airline, prohibit the "no-show clause" (where missing an outbound flight automatically cancels the return leg), and ban fees for correcting reasonable booking errors (e.g., misspelled names) or for printed boarding passes if passengers have already checked in digitally. Furthermore, it guarantees one free personal item (such as a handbag, laptop bag, or small backpack, up to 40x30x15cm) in the cabin, and mandates that children under 14 must be seated next to an accompanying adult at no extra charge. This significantly strengthens consumer protection and clarity for air travelers across the EU.

Council Regulation revising EU Social Security Coordination Rules: Previously, the process for proving social security coverage for workers posted to another EU country (via A1 forms) could be initiated retrospectively. Now, companies with mobile workforces must submit an A1 application *before* work commences in the destination country to establish social security coverage and secure exemption from local contributions, marking a critical shift to prospective compliance. The regulation also introduces a three-month prior affiliation condition, which may affect eligibility for postings for recently hired or transferred employees. It revises rules across five key areas: unemployment benefits, long-term care benefits, access to welfare benefits for economically inactive persons, family benefits, and the applicable legislation for mobile workers. This will require Portuguese businesses with cross-border employees to integrate early social security assessments into their mobility and HR workflows to ensure compliance with the new pre-travel declaration requirements.

Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (EU (ECGT) Regulations 2026): Previously, consumers often encountered vague or misleading environmental claims ("greenwashing") and inconsistent information regarding product durability, making informed, sustainable purchasing decisions difficult. Now, this Directive formally bans unfair commercial practices related to greenwashing and mandates that businesses ensure product durability. From 27 September 2026, retailers across the EU, including those in Portugal, must display a new EU Harmonised Notice, both in-store and online, clearly explaining consumers' minimum 2-year legal guarantee rights. Additionally, a new EU GARAN label will be introduced to highlight products that offer voluntary durability guarantees exceeding two years, thereby encouraging the production of longer-lasting goods. This aims to empower consumers to make more informed and sustainable choices while holding businesses accountable for their environmental and durability claims.

Regulation (EU) 2026/2108 establishing the Union Customs Code: Previously, the EU customs framework (Regulation (EU) No 952/2013) struggled with outdated IT systems, insufficient handling of non-financial risks associated with goods, and challenges posed by the rapid growth of e-commerce transactions. Now, this Regulation repeals and replaces the old code, comprehensively revising, simplifying, and harmonizing rules for goods entering and exiting the EU. It mandates a significant digitalization of customs processes, including leveraging artificial intelligence for real-time risk analysis, and establishes a new European Union Customs Authority to ensure coordinated governance and uniform implementation across all Member States. This modernization aims to improve the protection of the Union and its citizens against non-financial risks and to more effectively manage the increasing volume of goods, particularly from e-commerce, thereby significantly impacting Portuguese businesses involved in international trade by streamlining import/export procedures.
