🇪🇺 EU Policy

Directive revising the lists of pollutants in surface and groundwaters: Member States must adapt their national laws to incorporate updated lists of water pollutants and stricter controls. Previously, existing directives (Water Framework Directive, Environmental Quality Standards Directive, and Groundwater Directive) contained older lists of pollutants. Now, these directives are adapted to include newly identified substances harmful to the environment and human health, such as certain PFAS 'forever chemicals' (e.g., TFA), pesticides, pharmaceuticals, microplastics, and indicators of antimicrobial resistance. The revised law also introduces a requirement for "effect-based monitoring" to assess the combined risk to water quality and allows for more flexible application of the principle of non-deterioration of water status to facilitate important economic activities, provided environmental and health safeguards are ensured. This will require businesses, particularly those in industrial, agricultural, and waste management sectors across the EU, including Portugal, to review and potentially adapt their operational practices and waste treatment processes to comply with stricter pollution limits and new monitoring requirements.

Regulation (EU) 2026/808 (Crisis Management and Deposit Insurance Reform Package): This Regulation tightens the rules on which deposits can absorb losses in bank resolution as part of the broader Crisis Management and Deposit Insurance (CMDI) reform package. Previously, certain deposits with a right of early withdrawal could qualify towards banks' Minimum Requirement for Own Funds and Eligible Liabilities (MREL). Now, deposits granting a right of early withdrawal no longer qualify for MREL, even if subject to a contractual penalty. Banks must clearly disclose in their contractual documentation that such deposits count toward MREL and are therefore not protected by a deposit guarantee scheme up to €100,000 if a bank fails. A transitional provision allows deposits taken before 12 May 2028 that meet previous conditions to remain eligible until 11 May 2029. This significantly impacts EU banks, including those in Portugal, by requiring a reassessment of their resolution strategies, MREL-eligible liabilities, and client disclosures, enhancing the overall resilience of the EU banking sector.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/905 on spot FX benchmarks: This Regulation establishes a list of specific spot foreign exchange (FX) benchmarks that are exempt from the requirements of the EU Benchmark Regulation (BMR). Previously, all spot FX benchmarks were subject to the full regulatory obligations of the BMR. Now, by exempting these widely used benchmarks, the EU aims to ensure that banks, investment funds, and businesses within the Union can continue to access critical hedging tools without facing potential market disruption due to administrators lacking incentives to comply with the BMR. This provides regulatory clarity and reduces compliance burdens for financial market participants and businesses engaging in international trade.

Why it matters: The entry into force of updated water protection rules introduces stricter environmental obligations for businesses, while new banking regulations enhance financial stability by reforming deposit loss-absorption mechanisms.
