🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Alterações ao Pagamento do Imposto Único de Circulação (IUC): O Decreto-Lei n.º 161/2026, publicado a 4 de agosto de 2026, altera o regime de pagamento do IUC. Anteriormente, o imposto era pago anualmente no mês do aniversário da matrícula de cada veículo. A partir de 1 de janeiro de 2027, o IUC será liquidado centralizadamente por sujeito passivo para a totalidade dos seus veículos, com a possibilidade de pagamento numa única prestação em abril. Para valores superiores a €100, o pagamento pode ser faseado (e.g., em abril e outubro para valores entre €100 e €500; em abril, julho e outubro para valores superiores a €500). Um regime transitório para 2027 permite um pagamento único em outubro para valores até €500, ou em duas prestações (julho e outubro) para montantes superiores.

Practical impact for citizens or businesses: Esta alteração simplifica o cumprimento das obrigações fiscais para os proprietários de veículos, especialmente para quem possui múltiplos automóveis, ao unificar a liquidação e fixar datas de pagamento, reduzindo o risco de incumprimento involuntário.

Criação da Prestação Social Única (PSU): O Presidente da República promulgou, a 7 de agosto de 2026, o decreto-lei que institui a Prestação Social Única (PSU). Esta medida visa consolidar treze apoios sociais existentes num único benefício, com o objetivo de simplificar e tornar mais transparente o sistema de proteção social.

Practical impact for citizens or businesses: Os cidadãos beneficiários de apoios sociais poderão experimentar um processo de acesso mais simplificado e transparente aos seus direitos, potencialmente reduzindo a burocracia e facilitando a gestão dos seus benefícios.

Reforma do Sistema de Justiça Penal (Lei n.º 34/2026): A Lei n.º 34/2026, de 27 de julho, que entra em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026, introduz alterações ao Código Penal, Código de Processo Penal e Regulamento das Custas Processuais. Anteriormente, os processos penais eram frequentemente alvo de longos atrasos. Agora, a reforma atribui aos magistrados um papel mais ativo na gestão dos processos, reforça os poderes de direção processual, simplifica regimes processuais e cria mecanismos para sancionar demoras abusivas. Nos processos considerados de especial complexidade, os prazos processuais podem ser duplicados, e o juiz pode alargar os prazos para interposição de recursos e respetivas respostas até um máximo de 30 dias.

Practical impact for citizens or businesses: Esta lei visa acelerar significativamente a tramitação dos processos penais, resultando em resoluções mais céleres para indivíduos e empresas envolvidos em litígios criminais, mas exigindo também maior agilidade por parte dos profissionais do direito.

Regime de Contratos de Investimento para Arrendamento (CIA): O Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026, de 20 de maio, estabelece o regime dos Contratos de Investimento para Arrendamento (CIA), que entra em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026. Anteriormente, não existia um regime específico com incentivos fiscais tão abrangentes para o investimento na habitação para arrendamento a rendas moderadas. Este novo regime permite que investidores que celebrem CIAs com o IHRU, I.P., para projetos onde pelo menos 70% da área de construção se destine a arrendamento habitacional com rendas mensais não superiores a €2.300 (renda moderada), beneficiem de um conjunto de incentivos fiscais. Estes incluem isenções de IMT, IS e IMI (até 8 anos, seguindo-se uma redução de 50% até 10 anos), aplicação da taxa reduzida de IVA em empreitadas de construção e restituição de 50% do IVA suportado em serviços de construção.

Practical impact for citizens or businesses: Esta medida cria um forte incentivo para o aumento da oferta de habitação para arrendamento a preços moderados, o que poderá aliviar a pressão no mercado habitacional para cidadãos e empresas.

Why it matters: As medidas aprovadas e que entram em vigor em breve demonstram um foco governamental na simplificação de processos e no estímulo à oferta de habitação a preços acessíveis, com destaque para a criação de um regime robusto de incentivos fiscais para o investimento em habitação para arrendamento moderado.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on Packaging and Packaging Waste (PPWR): From August 12, 2026, manufacturers placing packaging on the EU market must undertake conformity assessments, prepare technical documentation, and issue an EU Declaration of Conformity, retaining this documentation for 5-10 years. Previously, the Directive 94/62/EC allowed Member States discretion, leading to fragmented national rules. Now, the PPWR directly establishes a single, harmonized set of rules across all 27 EU Member States. Additionally, strict substance controls are imposed, limiting heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium) and restricting per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging above specified thresholds. Importers from outside the EU are required to appoint an authorized representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State they export to. This fundamentally changes compliance for manufacturers, importers, and distributors across all sectors operating in the EU, including Portugal, as packaging compliance becomes a mandatory precondition for placing products on the market.

Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation 2024/1689) – Transparency Obligations (Article 50): As of August 2, 2026, providers and deployers of AI systems interacting with natural persons must clearly inform users they are dealing with an AI (e.g., a chatbot), not a human. Previously, there were no specific EU-wide legal mandates for such AI transparency. For AI systems generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text content, outputs must be marked in a machine-readable format and be recognizable as artificially generated or manipulated. Deployers distributing deepfakes or publishing AI-generated texts for public interest purposes are also subject to clear disclosure obligations, to be made at the latest at the time of first interaction or perception. A grace period until December 2, 2026, applies for generative AI systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, to comply with the marking obligation. This profoundly impacts tech companies developing or deploying generative AI and interactive AI systems, fostering trust by empowering citizens to make informed decisions about AI-generated content and interactions.

New EU Gas Market Rules: The new gas market rules apply from August 5, 2026, aiming to accelerate the transition from fossil gas to renewable and low-carbon gases, particularly hydrogen. They require Member States to strengthen consumer rights, ensuring free choice of supplier, clearer information on offers, and robust protection in case of disputes, alongside safeguarding vulnerable consumers from supply disconnection. Previously, gas market rules were less explicitly focused on decarbonisation and offered varying levels of consumer protection across Member States. This drives significant investment shifts in the energy sector towards greener alternatives and enhances consumer protections across the EU, including for energy companies and households in Portugal.

Revised MiFID/MiFIR Framework – Reasonable Commercial Basis (RCB) requirements for market data: From August 2026, the new Reasonable Commercial Basis (RCB) requirements for market data will fully apply, obliging trading venues, intermediaries, and data users to ensure market data is priced and made available on a reasonable commercial basis. Previously, the framework for market data pricing was less harmonized and explicit, leading to potential divergences in data access and costs. This ensures greater transparency and fairness in the market for financial data.

Why it matters: This week marks a pivotal moment for EU businesses, with major new compliance obligations taking effect in digital services and environmental sustainability, alongside significant advancements in energy market reform and financial data transparency.

🇺🇸 US Policy

Executive Order on Childhood Vaccine Recommendations: President Donald Trump signed an Executive Order on August 10, 2026, titled "Delivering Gold Standard Childhood Vaccine Recommendations for Americans," which directs federal health officials to recommend fewer routine childhood vaccines and to space out immunizations across separate medical visits. The order specifically calls for reducing the list of routinely recommended vaccines from 18 to 11 diseases, moving the remaining 7 to a "shared clinical decision-making" category, and promotes single-dose administration, notably recommending the separation of the combined measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine into three individual shots, though standalone versions are not currently available in the United States. This action aims to realign U.S. childhood vaccine guidance with the President's long-held beliefs on vaccine administration and parental choice, potentially shifting public health policy and vaccine procurement strategies.

DHS Final Rule on H-1B and L-1 Visa Biometric Fee: The Department of Homeland Security (DHS), through U.S. Customs and Border Protection, published a final rule in the Federal Register on August 11, 2026, effective September 9, 2026, amending regulations concerning the 9-11 Response and Biometric Entry-Exit Fee for certain H-1B and L-1 Visas. This rule clarifies that covered employers must submit the 9-11 Biometric Fee for all extension of status petitions, irrespective of whether a related fraud prevention and detection fee applies, including extensions that do not involve a change of employer. This corrects DHS's previous interpretation of statutory language to align with congressional intent, potentially increasing compliance costs for businesses filing H-1B and L-1 visa extension petitions.

IRS Final Regulations on Backup Withholding for Third-Party Network Transactions: The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) and the Department of the Treasury published final regulations (Treasury Decision 10053) in the Federal Register on August 10, 2026, effective immediately and applicable to payments made in calendar years beginning after December 31, 2024. These regulations govern backup withholding on reportable payments in settlement of third-party network transactions, raising the backup withholding threshold from $600 to $20,000 and 200 transactions. This change significantly reduces the frequency with which third-party settlement organizations must perform backup withholding, aiming to alleviate administrative burdens on small businesses and streamline tax reporting requirements.

SBA Final Rule on 8(a) Program Social Disadvantage Presumption: The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) published a final rule in the Federal Register on August 11, 2026, effective 30 days after publication, which amends its 8(a) Business Development Program regulations. The rule removes the rebuttable presumption that individuals belonging to certain designated groups are socially disadvantaged for individually-owned firms, establishing revised standards for individuals to establish social disadvantage. This action brings the 8(a) program into alignment with constitutional requirements following a Department of Justice advisory that the prior presumption was unconstitutional, potentially altering the eligibility landscape for minority-owned businesses seeking federal contracts.

FTC Ends Disparate-Impact Discrimination Claims: The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) announced on August 7, 2026, a policy statement clarifying that the Commission will no longer pursue legal claims based on disparate-impact or "unfair discrimination" theories under Section 5 of the FTC Act. This policy shift, influenced by a 2025 executive order, means the FTC will now focus enforcement on intentional discrimination (disparate treatment) rather than outcomes that disproportionately affect certain groups without proof of discriminatory intent. This move signals a significant change in federal consumer protection and fair lending enforcement strategy, potentially impacting compliance requirements for various industries.

Why it matters: The U.S. government has enacted several regulatory changes this week, most notably recalibrating federal health recommendations on childhood vaccines, adjusting visa fee requirements, and altering the criteria for federal contracting programs and anti-discrimination enforcement, indicating a broader shift towards streamlined regulations and a revised approach to social and economic policy.
