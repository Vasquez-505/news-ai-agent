🇺🇸 US Policy

DOL Final Rule on Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM) Transparency: The U.S. Department of Labor's (DOL) Employee Benefit Security Administration (EBSA) issued a final rule requiring Pharmacy Benefit Managers (PBMs) to provide greater financial transparency and disclosures to large, self-insured health plans governed by the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA). Previously, PBMs had less stringent disclosure requirements; now, they must disclose direct and indirect compensation, including manufacturer rebates and formulary incentives, before a service agreement is executed and on an ongoing basis every six months, or quarterly if requested, and employers gain the right to use their chosen PBM auditor. The rule is effective 60 days after its publication on November 2, 2026, and applies to plan years beginning on or after July 1, 2027. This action aims to reduce healthcare costs by increasing visibility into PBM financial arrangements, potentially impacting the profitability of PBMs and pharmaceutical companies.

ONC HTI-5 Final Rule on Health IT Certification: The Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology (ONC) updated its Health IT Certification Program requirements, removing 14 certification criteria and revising seven, while 39 criteria remain unchanged. This means certified health IT developers previously adhered to a broader set of requirements; now, the revised standards, effective December 28, 2026, and January 1, 2027 for some provisions, streamline compliance by narrowing the decision support interventions (DSI) criterion and eliminating source attribute requirements for certain DSIs. This deregulation is intended to reduce compliance burdens and foster innovation in health technology, influencing investment trends in the healthcare IT sector.

NCUA Final Rule on Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) Loan Treatment: The National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) Board adopted a final rule to permanently maintain the zero percent risk weighting for Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) loans under its capital adequacy regulation. This makes permanent the temporary regulatory treatment that has been in place for PPP loans since April 27, 2020. This change, effective November 4, 2026, provides long-term certainty for credit unions holding these loans, maintaining their favorable capital treatment and potentially supporting ongoing small business lending.

FDIC Interim Final Rule on On-Site Examination Cycles: The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC), along with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) and the Federal Reserve Board, issued an interim final rule that raises the asset threshold for certain supervised institutions to qualify for an 18-month on-site examination cycle. Previously, institutions below a certain lower asset threshold received an 18-month cycle; now, institutions with less than $6 billion in total assets are eligible. Effective November 2, 2026, this rule aims to reduce the regulatory burden on a wider range of smaller financial institutions, potentially freeing up resources for growth and operations.

FNA Final Rule on SNAP Retailer Stocking Standards: The Food and Nutrition Administration (FNA) published a final rule updating retailer stocking standards for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). Previously, retailers operated under different guidelines; now, as of November 4, 2026, all SNAP-authorized retailers, excluding specialty food stores, are required to continuously stock at least seven varieties in each of the four staple food categories (dairy, grains, protein, and vegetables/fruits) and offer at least one perishable variety in three of these categories. This action aims to enhance the nutritional options available to SNAP participants and may require adjustments in inventory and supply chains for affected grocery retailers.
