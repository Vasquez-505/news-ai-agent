Prestação Social Única (Decreto-Lei n.º 166/2026): Este decreto-lei, que entrou em vigor a 14 de agosto de 2026, cria uma Prestação Social Única (PSU) no âmbito do subsistema de solidariedade, substituindo 13 prestações sociais existentes (como a pensão social de velhice, o subsídio social de desemprego e o rendimento social de inserção). Anteriormente, o sistema era fragmentado com múltiplas prestações e diferentes regras de acesso e cálculo. Agora, a PSU introduz uma lógica de atribuição baseada em regras uniformes de verificação de recursos e um modelo de cálculo integrado, visando maior equidade e transparência. O impacto prático para os cidadãos e agregados familiares em situação de carência económica é a simplificação do acesso aos apoios sociais, que se tornam mais coerentes e ajustados às necessidades mínimas.

O impacto prático é que mais projetos de reabilitação urbana beneficiarão da taxa reduzida de IVA, incentivando o investimento e a renovação de imóveis e espaços públicos.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on Packaging and Packaging Waste (PPWR) – Initial Application: This regulation introduces harmonized EU-wide rules for packaging, replacing the previous fragmented national requirements under Directive 94/62/EC. As of August 12, 2026, key obligations apply: food-contact packaging must not contain per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) above strict limits, and all packaging must meet a combined heavy metal limit (lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium) capped at 100 mg/kg. Previously, national laws based on the Directive allowed for varied implementations; now, all packaging placed on the EU market must be designed for recyclability and adhere to these substance restrictions, with manufacturers required to complete conformity assessments and technical documentation. This significantly impacts manufacturers, importers, exporters, and e-commerce sellers, including those in Portugal, by standardizing environmental and safety requirements for all packaged goods entering the EU market.

EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) – Transparency Obligations: Starting August 2, 2026, specific transparency obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act began to apply to providers and deployers of certain AI systems. Previously, no such harmonized, direct EU-wide transparency requirements existed for AI systems. Now, providers of AI systems interacting directly with individuals (e.g., chatbots) must disclose that users are engaging with AI, unless obvious. Providers of AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic media (audio, image, video, text) must embed machine-readable markings and provide a detection mechanism. A limited transitional period for generative AI systems already on the market extends this marking/detection compliance to December 2, 2026. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals, and those using deepfakes or AI-generated text on public-interest matters must disclose the content's artificial origin unless human editorial review occurred. Non-compliance can result in substantial fines, up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. This means Portuguese tech businesses and entities deploying AI solutions must immediately implement disclosure mechanisms and update their AI governance frameworks.
