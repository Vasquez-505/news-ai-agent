🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação (RJUE): O Decreto-Lei n.º 108/2026, de 29 de maio, que entrou em vigor a 1 de outubro de 2026, alterou profundamente as regras de licenciamento urbanístico em Portugal. Anteriormente, muitas operações urbanísticas exigiam um licenciamento prévio municipal com longos prazos de aprovação; agora, a "comunicação prévia" torna-se a regra para a maioria das construções, alterações ou reabilitações, permitindo o início das obras após a submissão e pagamento, com fiscalização municipal posterior. Adicionalmente, os contratos de compra e venda de imóveis passam a ter de indicar explicitamente se o imóvel possui título urbanístico, embora a sua apresentação física já não seja obrigatória na escritura. Este regime acelera significativamente os processos para cidadãos e empresas que pretendam construir ou reabilitar, ao mesmo tempo que aumenta a transparência nas transações imobiliárias.

Regime Simplificado de Arrendamento Acessível (RSAA): A Portaria n.º 454/2026/1, publicada a 13 de outubro de 2026, regulamenta os limites máximos de renda por tipologia aplicáveis no âmbito do Regime Simplificado de Arrendamento Acessível. Enquanto o Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026 já havia estabelecido o RSAA em maio, esta Portaria define agora os valores concretos das rendas que qualificam para os incentivos fiscais (isenção de IRS e IRC sobre rendimentos prediais, e isenções de IMT e IMI em investimentos). Esta medida visa estimular a oferta de habitação com rendas controladas, oferecendo benefícios fiscais substanciais a proprietários e promotores que adiram ao regime, e contribuindo para aumentar a disponibilidade de habitação acessível para os cidadãos.

Suplemento Extraordinário de Pensões: O Decreto-Lei n.º 201/2026, de 8 de outubro, estabeleceu a atribuição de um suplemento extraordinário de pensão, de concessão única, para o ano de 2026. Este suplemento destina-se a pensionistas de invalidez, velhice e sobrevivência do sistema de segurança social, bem como a pensionistas por aposentação, reforma e sobrevivência do regime de proteção social convergente e do setor bancário, cujo montante mensal global de pensões seja igual ou inferior a três vezes o valor do Indexante dos Apoios Sociais (IAS) em vigor. Esta medida proporciona um reforço pontual do poder de compra para os pensionistas com rendimentos mais baixos, ajudando a mitigar o impacto do aumento acumulado do custo de vida.

Prorrogação dos Limites Mínimos do Imposto sobre Produtos Petrolíferos e Energéticos (ISP): O Presidente da República promulgou, a 13 de outubro de 2026, o Decreto da Assembleia da República n.º 114/XVII, que prorroga a vigência da Lei n.º 12-B/2026, de 15 de abril. Esta lei altera temporária e excecionalmente os limites mínimos das taxas unitárias do imposto sobre os produtos petrolíferos e energéticos. A extensão desta medida permite ao Governo manter a flexibilidade na gestão da carga fiscal sobre os combustíveis, potencialmente evitando aumentos automáticos e aliviando a pressão dos preços nos consumidores e nas empresas de transportes.

Why it matters: As novas regras do Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e Edificação (RJUE) representam a mudança mais significativa para cidadãos e empresas, ao simplificarem e desburocratizarem os processos de licenciamento de obras, tornando a construção e a reabilitação mais céleres.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

New EU Migration Return Order: EU interior ministers agreed on stricter rules for returning migrants, allowing Member States to impose sanctions such as reduced benefits, fines, or criminal charges on individuals who do not cooperate with return procedures. Previously, return rates were low despite a significant number of orders to leave the EU. Now, detention periods can extend up to 24 months, or longer for security risks, with the possibility of indefinite entry bans, and the establishment of return hubs outside the EU. A new European Return Order will also permit Member States to recognise each other's return decisions. These rules, which exclude unaccompanied minors, will take effect in the coming weeks. This impacts citizens of non-EU countries without the right to stay, increasing obligations to cooperate with authorities and facing harsher penalties for non-compliance.

Council Decision on Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) Financial Contribution to EEA: The Council adopted a Decision establishing the EU's position within the EEA Joint Committee to amend Protocol 31 of the EEA Agreement to include a "CBAM financial contribution." Previously, specific provisions for CBAM financial contributions from EEA EFTA states were not formally integrated into Protocol 31. Now, a framework is being formalized for these states to contribute financially in alignment with the EU's CBAM, impacting businesses trading goods subject to CBAM with EEA EFTA countries.

EU Chemical Safety and Environmental Compliance Regulations: The European Council granted final legislative ratification to new environmental compliance frameworks that tighten existing chemical exposure exemptions. Previously, certain long-standing regulatory loopholes allowed for specific chemical exposure exemptions. Now, these regulations establish a significantly higher baseline of protection for public health and vulnerable ecosystems across the EU by closing those loopholes. This directly affects industrial manufacturing businesses and any sector involved in the production or use of chemicals, requiring adherence to stricter environmental and public health standards.

Renewal of Approval for Hydrochloric Acid and Cis-tricos-9-ene in Biocidal Products: The Commission adopted Implementing Regulations renewing the approval of hydrochloric acid for use in biocidal products of product-type 2 and cis-tricos-9-ene for product-type 19. Previously, temporary postponements of expiry dates for the approval of these substances were in effect. Now, following a review, these active substances have their approvals formally renewed, ensuring their continued legal use in specified biocidal products within the EU. This provides regulatory certainty for manufacturers and distributors of biocidal products containing these substances.

Why it matters: This week saw significant advancements in concrete EU regulatory frameworks, particularly enhancing environmental and public health protections through new chemical safety standards and streamlining the legal status of key industrial substances, alongside a notable tightening of external border management policies.

CFTC Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) on Crypto Asset Transactions and Markets: The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) published an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) in the Federal Register on October 13, 2026, titled "Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets." This action formally opens a public comment period, closing December 14, 2026, for two contemplated federal regulatory frameworks: Regulation CTX, addressing leveraged, margined, or financed retail crypto asset transactions under the CFTC's existing authority, and Regulation CAM, proposing a new category of crypto exchanges. This signifies a formal step by the CFTC to establish clearer federal oversight and potential registration pathways for certain crypto activities, impacting how digital asset exchanges and intermediaries operate.

Public Law No. 119-119 (Identity Theft Single Point of Contact): Congress enacted H.B. 5345 into Public Law No. 119-119 on October 7, 2026, amending Title VII of the Social Security Act. This law mandates the establishment of a single point of contact within the Social Security Administration (SSA) for individuals who are victims of identity theft. Previously, victims might navigate fragmented systems; now, the SSA is required to provide a centralized resource for assistance, aiming to streamline support for those affected by identity theft related to their Social Security information.

Public Law No. 119-120 (Annual Report on Over-Budget Projects): Congress enacted S.B. 766 into Public Law No. 119-120 on October 7, 2026. This legislation requires the federal government to produce an annual report detailing taxpayer-funded projects that are both over budget and behind schedule. Previously, there was no mandated comprehensive annual report of this nature; now, federal agencies must provide increased transparency and accountability regarding cost overruns and delays in government projects.

Why it matters: The US government is actively shaping regulatory frameworks for emerging financial technologies while also enhancing citizen protection and government accountability for spending.
