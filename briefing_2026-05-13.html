🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Pacote de Medidas de Desagravamento Fiscal na Habitação: Este diploma do Governo, promulgado em 12 de maio de 2026, introduz diversas alterações fiscais para estimular a oferta de habitação. Anteriormente, a tributação das rendas no IRS seguia as regras gerais, sem um incentivo específico. Agora, os rendimentos de arrendamento de contratos até 2.300 euros mensais beneficiam de uma taxa de IRS reduzida para 10%, face à taxa anterior que poderia ser de 25% ou superior, dependendo do escalão. Adicionalmente, o IVA sobre a construção de novos imóveis destinados à venda (com preço até 660.982 euros) ou arrendamento (com renda até 2.300 euros mensais) é reduzido de 23% para 6%. Previamente, as mais-valias sobre a venda de imóveis habitacionais eram geralmente tributadas; agora, são isentas se reinvestidas em novas propriedades destinadas a arrendamento a preços moderados. O limite da dedução no IRS para inquilinos pelos contratos de arrendamento foi aumentado de um valor anterior para um máximo de 1.000 euros mensais. O impacto prático destas medidas é a diminuição da carga fiscal para proprietários que disponibilizam imóveis para arrendamento a preços acessíveis e para o setor da construção, visando aumentar a oferta habitacional, além de aliviar a despesa com rendas para os inquilinos.

Nova Lei da Nacionalidade: A Lei da Nacionalidade revista foi promulgada em 3 de maio de 2026, com a entrada formal em vigor do novo quadro nessa mesma data. Anteriormente, era geralmente exigido um período de cinco anos de residência legal em Portugal para que um estrangeiro pudesse requerer a nacionalidade portuguesa. Com a nova lei, o requisito de residência legal para a maioria dos requerentes passa a ser de dez anos. Para garantir a estabilidade jurídica, os processos administrativos pendentes à data da entrada em vigor desta nova lei continuarão a ser regidos pela legislação anterior. Esta alteração tem como impacto prático um período mais longo de espera para a obtenção da nacionalidade portuguesa pela via da residência, visando uma maior integração dos imigrantes no país.

Proibição de Reprocessamento de Dispositivos Médicos de Uso Único: A Portaria n.º 206/2026/1, publicada em Diário da República em 5 de maio de 2026 e em vigor a partir de 6 de maio de 2026, estabelece a proibição do reprocessamento de determinados dispositivos médicos de uso único em todo o território nacional. Anteriormente, o reprocessamento de alguns destes dispositivos poderia ocorrer sem uma regulamentação explícita ou proibição. Agora, uma lista específica de dispositivos médicos (detalhada num anexo à portaria) é explicitamente proibida de ser reprocessada. O impacto prático é o reforço da segurança dos pacientes e a imposição de maior rigor nas práticas das instituições de saúde, evitando riscos associados a dispositivos reprocessados.

Regulamento do Concurso Nacional de Acesso e Ingresso no Ensino Superior Público 2026-2027: A Portaria n.º 219/2026/1, publicada em Diário da República em 12 de maio de 2026, aprova o regulamento que define as normas e procedimentos para o concurso nacional de acesso e ingresso no ensino superior público para o ano letivo de 2026-2027. Previamente, aplicavam-se as regras do ano letivo anterior. Este regulamento estabelece as condições de candidatura, os prazos, as provas de ingresso e os critérios de seriação para os estudantes que pretendam ingressar em instituições de ensino superior públicas. O impacto prático é direto para milhares de estudantes do ensino secundário e candidatos ao ensino superior, que deverão consultar e cumprir as novas diretrizes e prazos definidos para o próximo ano letivo.

Why it matters: As medidas de desagravamento fiscal na habitação visam aumentar a oferta de imóveis a preços acessíveis e dinamizar o mercado, enquanto a nova Lei da Nacionalidade estabelece critérios mais rigorosos para a aquisição da cidadania portuguesa.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU Directive on Environmental Quality Standards and Groundwater (Revising Water Framework, Environmental Quality Standards, and Groundwater Directives): This Directive updates the lists of water pollutants in surface and groundwaters, including previously unregulated substances like certain PFAS 'forever chemicals' (such as TFA), pesticides, pharmaceuticals, microplastics, indicators of antimicrobial resistance, and sensitive groundwater ecosystems. It introduces a requirement for "effect-based monitoring" to assess combined risk to water quality and allows for flexible application of the non-deterioration principle for important economic activities under strict safeguards. Previously, older lists of pollutants were in effect, with less comprehensive coverage of emerging contaminants and monitoring methods. Member States must transpose these amendments into national law by December 22, 2027. This will require businesses, particularly those involved in industrial discharge and agriculture, to comply with stricter environmental standards and monitoring, impacting operations across the EU, including Portugal.

EU Anti-Corruption Directive: This Directive introduces uniform definitions of public officials, tightens sanctioning standards for companies, and raises expectations for effective corporate compliance systems. It also criminalizes corruption in certain "three-party constellations," such as granting an undue advantage to an intermediary to influence a public official. Previously, anti-corruption frameworks were more fragmented across Member States, with key existing EU instruments like the 1997 Convention and the 2003 Council Framework Decision. This Directive was adopted in April 2026 and entered into force 20 days after its publication, with Member States required to transpose its provisions into national law by 2028. This will lead to a more harmonized and robust framework for combating corruption, requiring businesses, including those in Portugal, to strengthen their internal compliance programs.

EU's 20th Russia Sanctions Package: This package introduces new sectoral sanctions impacting Russia's energy, financial, maritime, and technological sectors. Specifically, it prohibits the provision of liquefied natural gas (LNG) terminal services to Russian entities and certain Russian-owned or -controlled EU entities. It also prohibits engaging in any transaction involving the digital ruble (Russia's central bank digital currency) or the cryptoasset "RUBx," effective May 24, 2026. Furthermore, it significantly expands the transaction ban to cover non-financial entities that facilitate international payments through netting and set-off arrangements, with this prohibition taking effect for listed entities on May 14, 2026. These measures build upon the previous 19 sanctions packages. Businesses, including Portuguese companies, involved in the affected sectors or international payments must review their operations and supply chains to ensure compliance.

New EU Product Liability Directive (Directive (EU) 2024/2853): This Directive extends the scope of product liability to explicitly include software as a product, regardless of whether it is embedded in hardware, controls a system, or operates as a cloud-based service. Previously, product liability law was primarily restricted to physical objects, and the application of liability to software was contentious. This new Directive shifts the boundaries of liability towards increased risk responsibility for plant manufacturers, system integrators, and importers of AI hardware. Member States must transpose this Directive into national law by December 9, 2026. This will significantly increase legal exposure for companies in sectors like logistics plant engineering and any business developing, manufacturing, or importing software or AI hardware across the EU, including Portugal, necessitating early compliance initiatives.

Digital Omnibus on AI (simplifying the AI Act): A provisional agreement was reached on May 7, 2026, to simplify certain requirements under the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). The agreement postpones compliance obligations for high-risk AI systems (specifically those designated under Article 6(2) and Annex III of the AI Act) to December 2, 2027, instead of the previously set date of August 2, 2026. Additionally, transparency obligations for watermarking AI-generated content will now apply from December 2, 2026, rather than August 2, 2026. The agreement also bans AI systems that generate non-consensual sexually explicit or intimate content ("nudification" apps) and extends simplified requirements, previously for SMEs, to small mid-cap companies. The European Parliament and Council are expected to formally adopt this Regulation before August 2, 2026. This measure aims to ease the administrative burden for EU businesses, particularly smaller ones, in implementing the AI Act, while reinforcing protections against harmful AI applications.

Rescission of Conservation and Landscape Health Rule (BLM Final Rule): The Bureau of Land Management (BLM) issued a final rule on May 11, 2026, effective June 11, 2026, to fully rescind the Conservation and Landscape Health Rule that was previously issued on May 9, 2024. This action restores balance to federal land management by reaffirming the principles of multiple use and sustained yield, aiming to reduce regulatory burdens that previously elevated conservation as a discrete use and impeded efficient decision-making on public lands.

Restoring Flexibility in the Child Care and Development Fund (HHS Final Rule): The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) published a final rule on May 12, 2026, effective July 13, 2026, amending Child Care and Development Fund (CCDF) regulations to reduce costs and burden for States and Territories administering the program. This rule rescinds previous requirements to limit family co-payments to 7 percent of family income, to provide some direct services through grants or contracts, and to pay providers prospectively and based on enrollment.

OFAC Sanctions on Iran-linked Individuals and Entities: On May 11, 2026, the U.S. government announced sanctions by the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) against three individuals and nine companies, including entities based in Hong Kong, the United Arab Emirates, and Oman, for facilitating Iran's shipment of oil to China. These actions aim to disrupt financial networks supporting Iran's military programs and its oil sales, with immediate effect.

Extension of Compliance Dates for Disability Accessibility (HHS Interim Final Rule): The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) issued an interim final rule on May 12, 2026, effective May 7, 2026, extending the compliance dates for web content and mobile application accessibility requirements under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act. Previously, recipients with 15 or more employees were required to comply by May 11, 2026, and those with fewer than 15 employees by May 10, 2027; these deadlines are now extended to May 11, 2027, and May 10, 2028, respectively, providing more time for compliance.
