It streamlines the process for highly skilled workers from outside the EU to find employment within the Union. Previously, individual Member States had their own, often disparate, systems for attracting international talent. EU Directive revising lists of pollutants in surface and groundwaters: This Directive, which entered into force on May 11, 2026, updates and expands the lists of hazardous substances to be monitored and controlled in EU waters. Previously, the lists were older and did not comprehensively cover emerging pollutants. Now, the revised Directive includes substances such as certain PFAS "forever chemicals," specific pesticides, pharmaceuticals, and addresses microplastics and indicators of antimicrobial resistance. Member States are required to incorporate these new substances into their River Basin Management Plans and utilize "effect-based monitoring" to assess water quality. This will lead to stricter environmental standards across the EU, impacting industries involved in water discharge, agriculture, and pharmaceutical manufacturing, and requiring investment in pollution control technologies.

This position states that the CFTC will not recommend enforcement action against designated contract markets (DCMs), derivatives clearing organizations (DCOs), or their participants for failing to report fully collateralized event contract transaction data to swap data repositories (SDRs) or to meet related recordkeeping duties under Parts 43 and 45 of CFTC regulations. Previously, such entities were generally subject to these swap data reporting and recordkeeping requirements for event contracts that technically qualify as swaps. The relief is limited to specific conditions outlined in the May 13 letter, and firms can request to be added to an appendix for similar treatment, thereby streamlining approvals for event contracts. This significantly reduces the regulatory burden and compliance costs for prediction market operators and clearing organizations dealing with fully collateralized event contracts, potentially fostering growth and clearer operational frameworks in this evolving market segment.

Department of Justice Final Order on Marijuana Rescheduling: On May 13, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) issued a final order that immediately rescheduled certain marijuana-related products from Schedule I to Schedule III under the Controlled Substances Act (CSA). This order specifically covers (1) "drug products containing marijuana that have been approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)" and (2) "marijuana subject to a state medical marijuana license." Previously, these products were classified as Schedule I, which designates substances with no currently accepted medical use and a high potential for abuse. Now, as Schedule III substances, they are recognized as having accepted medical uses and a moderate to low potential for abuse and dependence. The DOJ also announced an expedited hearing process, starting June 29, 2026, to review broader marijuana rescheduling. This federal reclassification legitimizes medical marijuana prescribed under state law and FDA-approved marijuana drugs, potentially reducing federal legal risks for businesses in the medical cannabis sector and increasing legal clarity for employers regarding medical marijuana use.
