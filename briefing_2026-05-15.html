EU Directive revising lists of pollutants in surface and groundwaters: Previously, existing directives monitored specific lists of water pollutants. Now, this Directive, which entered into force on May 11, 2026, adds newly identified substances, including certain PFAS 'forever chemicals' (e.g., TFA), pesticides, pharmaceuticals, microplastics, and indicators of antimicrobial resistance, to the lists of pollutants in surface and groundwaters. It also introduces 'effect-based monitoring' to assess the combined risk to water quality. Member States must transpose these amendments into national law by December 22, 2027. This strengthens water protection across the EU, requiring businesses in sectors such as agriculture, chemical, and pharmaceutical industries, and those with water discharge, to comply with stricter environmental standards, impacting Portugal through national transposition and heightened compliance obligations.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1028 on Short-Term Rental Data Collection and Sharing: Previously, rules for short-term rentals varied significantly by Member State, with inconsistent registration and data obligations. Now, this Regulation, which applies from May 20, 2026, mandates online short-term rental platforms to verify and display a valid registration number for every listing in jurisdictions that require one, suspending non-compliant listings. Large platforms must also transmit standardized monthly data (including property address, registration number, listing URL, nights rented, and guest numbers) to national Single Digital Entry Points, with smaller platforms reporting quarterly. Member States must adopt necessary legislative and administrative measures by May 20, 2026. This increases transparency and enables local authorities, including in Portugal which already utilizes RNAL/AL numbers, to better monitor and manage the short-term rental market.

Regulation (EU) 2026/1030 on Greenhouse Gas Emissions Accounting of Transport Services: Previously, there was no uniform EU-wide method for calculating and reporting greenhouse gas emissions from transport services. Now, this Regulation, published in the Official Journal on May 12, 2026, creates a harmonized framework requiring transport operators, transport service organisers, and hub operators involved in services starting or ending in the EU to calculate and disclose their greenhouse gas emissions using standardized methodologies (based on EN ISO 14083:2023). While the Regulation was published this week, its entry into force is typically 20 days post-publication. Large operators may be required to use primary data for domestic services, but SMEs are generally exempt from data verification. This harmonizes emissions reporting, driving more sustainable transport choices and imposing new reporting obligations on logistics and transport companies across the EU, including those in Portugal.

Regulation (EU) 2026/1047 establishing an EU Talent Pool: Previously, employers lacked a unified EU tool to recruit talent from outside the Union to address specific labor shortages. Now, this Regulation, published in the Official Journal on May 12, 2026, establishes an EU Talent Pool, a digital platform managed by the European Commission, where employers in participating Member States can post job offers and access profiles of pre-screened non-EU jobseekers with skills for shortage occupations.
