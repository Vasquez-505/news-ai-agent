🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation on a Common System for the Return of Third-Country Nationals: This regulation introduces a European Return Order, a standardized form for key elements of return decisions, which will facilitate mutual recognition of return decisions among Member States, although this remains voluntary for an initial three-year period. It also establishes stricter measures for non-EU nationals deemed security risks, permitting indefinite entry bans and detention periods exceeding 24 months where justified and proportionate. Furthermore, the rules allow Member States to establish "return hubs" in non-EU countries, provided agreements with these countries respect international human rights standards. Previously, Member States faced challenges in effectively returning third-country nationals with no legal right to stay in the EU, with approximately two out of three individuals ordered to leave not actually departing. Now, national authorities will have more harmonized tools and procedures to implement swift and effective returns. Some provisions apply immediately upon entry into force (expected in the coming weeks following its adoption on October 1, 2026), while others, such as those requiring preparatory work, will apply one year after entry into force. This policy aims to enhance the effectiveness of EU migration management and border security.

Regulation Revising EU Social Security Coordination Rules: This regulation formally revises the EU framework for coordinating national social security systems across five key areas: unemployment benefits, long-term care benefits, access to welfare benefits for economically inactive persons, family benefits, and applicable legislation for mobile workers. A significant change for businesses with mobile workforces, including those in Portugal, is the shift to prospective compliance for posting situations. Previously, businesses could in some cases apply for A1 Portable Documents retrospectively; now, the A1 application, which certifies the applicable social security legislation, must be submitted *before* the work in the destination country begins to establish coverage and ensure exemption from local social security contributions. The regulation was formally adopted on September 28, 2026, and will enter into force upon its publication in the Official Journal of the European Union. While entry into force is imminent, the revised coordination rules determining applicable legislation are subject to a deferred application date, expected to be approximately two years from publication. This impacts Portuguese businesses and investors employing cross-border staff, requiring them to reassess internal processes for A1 applications to ensure they are submitted proactively.

Regulation 2026/2202 on Enhanced Air Passenger Rights: This regulation guarantees new rights for air passengers, requiring airlines to permit passengers to carry one personal item into the cabin at no additional cost. This personal item must meet safety and security requirements and typically measure no more than 40 × 30 × 15 cm or fit under the seat in front. Furthermore, to improve transparency, airlines and intermediaries are now obliged to display fares that include a separate cabin bag allowance by default. Previously, policies regarding free carry-on baggage and the transparency of baggage-inclusive fares varied significantly among airlines, often leading to unforeseen costs for passengers. The regulation was signed on September 16, 2026, and published on October 2, 2026. It enters into force on October 22, 2026, and will apply from October 23, 2027. This directly affects all airlines operating in the EU and impacts air travelers, including Portuguese citizens, by standardizing and improving the clarity of baggage rules and pricing.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/551 and Steel Import Rules: This Implementing Regulation, which came into application on October 1, 2026, updates the lists of products of animal origin, animal by-products, and composite products that are subject to official controls upon entry into the European Union. These lists are crucial for products such as meat, dairy, and eggs, ensuring they meet health and veterinary standards. Concurrently, new rules regarding steel imports also began to apply on October 1, 2026, requiring importers of certain steel products to provide specific information and evidence concerning the country where the metal was actually melted and poured. Previously, the lists for animal product controls might have been less current, and origin requirements for steel imports could have been less stringent. Now, businesses involved in importing these goods, including those in Portugal, must adhere to updated control measures and enhanced traceability requirements to ensure compliance with EU health and safety standards and origin transparency.
