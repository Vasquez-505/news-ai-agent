Commission Infringement Procedures for Directive (EU) 2024/825 (Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition): The European Commission initiated infringement procedures on May 28-29, 2026, by sending letters of formal notice to 20 Member States, including Portugal, for failing to fully transpose Directive (EU) 2024/825 into national law. Previously, consumer protection rules regarding green claims and product information varied or were less stringent. This Directive, with a transposition deadline of March 27, 2026, requires Member States to ensure businesses provide more reliable and transparent information on environmental claims, sustainability labels, product durability, and repairability, as well as clearer legal guarantee rights. This action puts immediate pressure on Portuguese businesses to enhance the transparency and accuracy of their green claims and product information, while Portugal's government has two months to notify complete national transposition to avoid further infringement steps.

Right to Repair Directive (EU) 2024/1799: Although adopted in 2024, the Directive sets a critical transposition deadline of July 31, 2026, for Member States to integrate its requirements into national law, making its application imminent. Previously, manufacturers' repair obligations were less harmonized across the EU. Now, manufacturers must offer repair services for products covered by Annex II (specific categories of goods) at a reasonable price, within a reasonable time, and using, where possible, second-hand or 3D-printed spare parts. They are also obligated to supply spare parts to independent repairers and consumers for a defined period after the last unit is placed on the market. Portuguese businesses manufacturing or importing Annex II products must prepare to significantly adjust their after-sales services and spare parts supply chains to meet these new obligations by the impending deadline.
