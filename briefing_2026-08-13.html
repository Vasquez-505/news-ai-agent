Prestação Social Única: O Decreto-Lei que cria a Prestação Social Única (PSU) foi promulgado em 8 de agosto de 2026. Anteriormente, existiam treze apoios sociais separados. Agora, estes são consolidados numa única prestação, visando tornar o sistema mais simples, justo e transparente. O impacto prático é uma potencial simplificação do acesso a apoios sociais para os cidadãos, embora o Presidente da República tenha alertado que o processo não deve traduzir-se numa diminuição da proteção social.

Regulamento de Atribuição de Bolsas de Estudo a Estudantes do Ensino Superior: A Portaria n.º 331-B/2026/1, de 10 de agosto, aprova o novo regulamento de atribuição de bolsas de estudo, entrando em vigor no primeiro dia útil após a sua publicação (11 de agosto de 2026), com aplicação a partir do ano letivo de 2026-2027. Anteriormente, o modelo não refletia adequadamente os custos reais de frequência do ensino superior e tinha um grau de progressividade limitado. Agora, o sistema é reformado para se basear nos custos adicionais e numa maior progressividade do apoio. O impacto prático é que os estudantes do ensino superior beneficiarão de um sistema de bolsas mais justo e eficaz, com apoios que melhor se adequam aos custos reais de frequência, facilitando o acesso e a permanência.

Regime Jurídico dos Estudantes com Necessidades Educativas Específicas no Ensino Superior: A Lei n.º 44/2026, de 10 de agosto, aprova o regime jurídico dos estudantes com necessidades educativas específicas no ensino superior e altera o Decreto-Lei n.º 163/2006, de 8 de agosto, sobre acessibilidade a edifícios. Anteriormente, havia um regime menos abrangente para garantir a inclusão destes estudantes. Agora, são estabelecidas novas disposições para assegurar a acessibilidade e o apoio necessário a estes estudantes. O impacto prático é a melhoria das condições para que estudantes com necessidades educativas especiais possam aceder e progredir no ensino superior, promovendo a inclusão.

Alteração ao Regime de Apoios Financeiros do Estado às Artes: O Decreto-Lei n.º 164-A/2026, de 12 de agosto, altera o Decreto-Lei n.º 103/2017, de 24 de agosto, que estabelece o regime de atribuição de apoios financeiros do Estado às artes visuais e performativas. O regime anterior não valorizava explicitamente práticas de igualdade de género, segurança e bem-estar no trabalho como o novo diploma. As alterações introduzem a valorização de práticas que promovam a igualdade de género, a segurança, a saúde física e mental e o bem-estar no trabalho, além de reforçar o apoio a novos criadores. O impacto prático é o incentivo a um ambiente de trabalho mais justo e seguro no setor artístico e o fomento de novas oportunidades para artistas emergentes.

Simplificação dos Procedimentos de Custas Processuais: O Decreto-Lei n.º 164/2026, de 6 de agosto, simplifica os procedimentos das secretarias judiciais relativos às custas processuais. Anteriormente, as partes tinham frequentemente de solicitar guias de pagamento. Agora, as secretarias passam a emitir e notificar as guias de pagamento sem necessidade de requerimento ou despacho judicial prévio, e são reforçadas as medidas de transparência na distribuição de processos. O impacto prático é a redução da burocracia e a aceleração dos procedimentos para cidadãos e empresas que interagem com o sistema judicial.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on Packaging and Packaging Waste (PPWR): This regulation begins its general application by requiring all packaging placed on the EU market from August 12, 2026, to adhere to strict substance limits. Previously, the EU operated under a directive (PPWD) allowing national variations in implementation; now, harmonized standards apply across the EU. Specifically, the combined concentration of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium in packaging components must not exceed 100 mg/kg. Furthermore, a near-total ban on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging takes effect, limiting any individual PFAS to 25 parts per billion (ppb) and the sum of PFAS to 100 parts per million (ppm). This directly impacts all companies, including Portuguese businesses, involved in manufacturing, importing, exporting, or selling packaged goods in the EU, necessitating packaging redesigns and supply chain audits. [New August 2026 EU Packaging Law, European Commission, EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation Begins to Apply August 12, 2026]

Regulation (EU) 2023/1464 amending REACH Annex XVII (Formaldehyde Restriction): From August 6, 2026, harmonised formaldehyde emission limits apply to furniture, wood-based articles, and other articles intended for indoor use when placed on the EU/EEA market. Previously, various national limits or no specific EU-wide limits existed. Now, furniture and wood-based articles must not exceed 0.062 mg/m³ of formaldehyde emissions, while other indoor articles have a limit of 0.080 mg/m³. Road vehicle interiors are granted an extended compliance deadline of August 6, 2027. This regulation mandates manufacturers and suppliers to ensure their products meet these new emission standards, affecting a broad range of consumer goods in Portugal and across the EU. [EU formaldehyde restriction: companies should prepare now for the 6 August 2026 deadline]

Regulation (EU) 2026/1738 on Vehicle Design, Production, and Waste Management: This new regulation entered into force on August 13, 2026, replacing existing EU framework directives from 2000 and 2005 with a single, directly applicable law covering the entire automotive life cycle. Previously, there were no EU-wide binding recycled plastic quotas for automakers; now, newly type-approved vehicles must incorporate at least 15% recycled plastic by weight from September 1, 2032, increasing to 25% by September 1, 2036, with 20% of this originating from scrapped vehicles. Automakers must also provide detailed instructions for part removal and replacement and will be required to issue a "digital circularity vehicle passport." From September 2029, Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) is expanded to the automotive sector, obliging carmakers to fund the collection and treatment of write-offs. Furthermore, from August 2031, only "roadworthy" vehicles, certified through European technical inspections, may be exported outside the EU. This significantly impacts the automotive industry, vehicle manufacturers, and the recycling sector across the EU, including Portugal, aiming to enhance circularity and reduce reliance on virgin resources. [EU Vehicle Rules Tighten Scrap Exports From Aug. 13]

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI) amending the AI Act: While the Digital Omnibus entered into force on July 27, 2026, its amendments impact critical compliance deadlines for the EU AI Act, which had transparency requirements entering into force on August 2, 2026. The key change is the delay of enforcement for most obligations concerning high-risk AI systems (previously due August 2, 2026) to December 2, 2027, for stand-alone systems and August 2, 2028, for AI systems embedded in regulated products. New prohibited AI practices include using AI to generate child sexual abuse material or non-consensual intimate content, with compliance required by December 2, 2026. For generative AI systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, a watermarking obligation compliance is due by December 2, 2026, while for systems placed on or after August 2, 2026, it applies immediately. This gives businesses, including those in Portugal, more time to adapt to complex high-risk AI system requirements but introduces immediate obligations for AI transparency and new prohibitions on harmful AI uses. Previously, these specific tariffs and import price programs were not in place for polysilicon. The measures are set to take effect on December 4, 2026. This action signifies a continued focus on strengthening domestic supply chains and could impact industries reliant on polysilicon, such as solar and semiconductor manufacturing.

Rescission of Syria's State Sponsor of Terrorism Designation: The Trump Administration officially rescinded Syria's State Sponsor of Terrorism (SST) designation. This decision follows the removal or suspension of comprehensive U.S. sanctions on Syria in 2025. Previously, Syria carried the SST designation, which had been in place for decades and served as a deterrent to investment and carried significant diplomatic and economic restrictions. Now, this designation is lifted. This represents a fundamental reversal of long-standing counterterrorism policy and could signal a shift in U.S. foreign policy towards Syria, potentially opening pathways for future engagement or investment, albeit amid ongoing instability.

FinCEN Eliminates Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting for US Entities: The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued a final rule (RIN 1506-AB67) that permanently removes the requirement for U.S. companies and U.S. persons to report beneficial ownership information (BOI) under the Corporate Transparency Act. Previously, U.S. companies and persons were required to report BOI to FinCEN. Now, this reporting obligation is eliminated for U.S. entities, while foreign entities that are reporting companies will still be required to report BOI for foreign individuals. This significantly reduces the compliance burden for domestic businesses.

SEC Exempts Data-Center Bonds from Key Securitization Rules: The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has made it easier for data center owners to sell asset-backed securities by stating that a major subset of data-center securitizations are not subject to certain disclosure and investor protection requirements, including risk retention. Previously, these securitizations generally faced all standard securitization rules, including the costly risk retention requirement. This regulatory clarification, which took effect upon the letter's issuance late last month, is expected to facilitate capital raising for data center development, supporting growth in the technology and infrastructure sectors.

National Security Presidential Memorandum on Combating Transnational Cyber-Enabled Crime: President Donald J. Trump signed a National Security Presidential Memorandum (NSPM) that empowers U.S. Federal law enforcement to utilize cyber tools to disrupt transnational criminal organizations (TCOs) operating in foreign jurisdictions who target Americans. The NSPM also establishes a framework encouraging private sector companies to collaborate with federal, state, local, tribal, and territorial agencies to gather TCO threat information and propose cyber operations. Previously, the government primarily relied on existing law enforcement and intelligence capabilities to counter cybercrime. This expands the U.S. government's capabilities and formally integrates the private sector into cyber-defense and offensive strategies.
