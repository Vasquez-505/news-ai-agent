A lei também simplifica a inscrição de despesas de reconstrução e reabilitação nos orçamentos e permite a concessão de apoio a pessoas em situação de vulnerabilidade sem a necessidade de regulamento municipal ou parceria com a administração central. Esta medida proporciona maior agilidade e flexibilidade financeira às autarquias para responderem rapidamente aos danos causados pela calamidade e apoiarem as populações afetadas.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EU AI Act) - Transparency Obligations: From August 2, 2026, providers and deployers of certain AI systems must comply with new transparency obligations under Article 50. Previously, there were no specific EU-wide legal requirements for AI transparency. Now, AI systems that directly interact with individuals (e.g., chatbots, voice assistants) must explicitly disclose that users are engaging with AI, unless this is already obvious. For AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic audio, image, video, or text (deepfakes), providers must embed machine-readable markings and provide a detection mechanism; for systems already on the market before August 2, 2026, this specific marking deadline is extended to December 2, 2026. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals. This has an immediate operational impact on businesses, including those in Portugal, that develop or deploy AI systems interacting with users or generating content, requiring them to implement new disclosure and labeling procedures. Non-compliance can lead to fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI) - Postponement of High-Risk Obligations and New Prohibited Practices: This amending Regulation, which entered into force on July 27, 2026, postpones the application of most "high-risk" AI system obligations under Annex III of the AI Act. Previously, these obligations were set to apply from August 2, 2026. Now, high-risk obligations for standalone Annex III systems (e.g., in employment, education, critical infrastructure, law enforcement) are delayed until December 2, 2027. For high-risk AI systems embedded into regulated products (Annex I), the deadline is extended to August 2, 2028. Additionally, a new prohibited practice is introduced from December 2, 2026: AI systems intended to generate non-consensual sexual or intimate content and child sexual abuse material. This provides businesses, including Portuguese companies, with additional time to prepare for the complex compliance requirements related to high-risk AI systems, while also introducing a new, strict prohibition on certain harmful AI applications.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EU AI Act) - Enforcement Powers: As of August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office, along with national authorities, gained full enforcement powers under the AI Act. Previously, the full enforcement mechanism was not active. Now, the AI Office can investigate and sanction providers of general-purpose AI systems. The full sanctioning framework is operational, with potential fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover for non-compliance with applicable AI Act obligations (including transparency rules), and up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover for prohibited practices. This marks a critical shift from legislative planning to active regulatory oversight, subjecting businesses operating AI systems in the EU to immediate scrutiny and potential penalties for non-compliance with the provisions currently in force.

Why it matters: The partial entry into force of the EU AI Act, alongside amendments clarifying its implementation timeline, signals the beginning of concrete regulatory enforcement for many AI applications across the EU, profoundly impacting tech companies and investors in the digital sector.

Presidential Proclamation on Polysilicon Tariffs and Minimum Import Prices: On August 6, 2026, President Trump signed a proclamation imposing a 15% ad valorem tariff on polysilicon derivatives and establishing minimum import prices (MIPs) for polysilicon and its derivative products under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962. Specific MIPs include $21/kg for polysilicon, $100/kg for ingots/wafers, $0.22/W for solar cells, and $0.38/W for solar modules. This action also authorizes the Secretary of Commerce to create an incentive program for companies investing in domestic polysilicon production facilities. Previously, a narrower safeguard tariff on solar cells and modules expired in February 2026. Now, broader tariffs and MIPs aim to revitalize U.S. polysilicon production capacity essential for semiconductors and solar power supply chains, effective December 4, 2026. This signals a significant move to protect and re-shore critical manufacturing capabilities, likely increasing costs for importers but supporting domestic industries in solar and semiconductor sectors.

Executive Orders on Birthright Citizenship and Birth Tourism: On August 6, 2026, President Trump signed two executive orders: "Continuing to Protect the Meaning and Value of American Citizenship" and "Ending Birth Tourism." The first directs federal agencies to withhold recognition of U.S. citizenship for children born in the U.S. under "historical exceptions," such as to members of foreign terrorist groups or foreign government employees/ambassadors. The second directs the State Department and Department of Homeland Security to strengthen enforcement against "birth tourism," defined as travel to the U.S. solely for the purpose of giving birth to secure U.S. citizenship for the child, with agencies mandated to update related regulations and guidance within 30 days. Previously, birthright citizenship was broadly applied with narrow exceptions; these orders aim to expand restrictions and enforcement. These orders represent a renewed administrative effort to restrict birthright citizenship and deter perceived abuses of immigration laws, potentially leading to increased scrutiny and legal challenges.

DHS Final Rule on H-1B and L-1 Biometric Fee: The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) published a final rule on August 8, 2026, amending regulations for the 9-11 Response and Biometric Entry-Exit Fee for certain H-1B and L-1 Visas. This rule now requires covered employers to pay the 9-11 Biometric Fee for all extension of status petitions, including those filed for employees remaining with the same employer. Previously, this fee applied to initial petitions and some change-of-employer filings, but not consistently to same-employer extensions. The fee remains $4,000 for H-1B and $4,500 for L-1 petitions, authorized through September 30, 2027. The change, effective September 9, 2026, aims to align regulations with congressional intent and fund DHS's biometric entry-exit system. This will increase costs and administrative burden for U.S. companies employing H-1B and L-1 visa holders, particularly those with a significant number of extension filings.

Treasury Department Sanctions Against Iranian Crypto Exchange and Individuals: On August 7, 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated Shelbit, an unlicensed Dubai-based cryptocurrency exchange, and its founder Siavash Kayvanpour, alleging they processed millions of dollars in crypto transactions for Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and other Iranian state-linked groups. This action expands efforts to disrupt illicit financial networks supporting Iran. Previously, OFAC had imposed sanctions on other Iranian banks and entities. This action, effective immediately, intensifies pressure on Iran's ability to evade international sanctions through cryptocurrency and targets financial intermediaries supporting its state-affiliated groups, increasing risks for entities involved in crypto transactions with Iran.
