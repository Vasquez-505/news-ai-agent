Alteração ao Código das Expropriações (Competência Municipal): Concede às Assembleias Municipais a autonomia para decidir sobre a utilidade pública de expropriações no âmbito de iniciativas da administração local. Anteriormente, a decisão final para expropriações locais dependia da aprovação da administração central. Esta medida agiliza a execução de projetos de desenvolvimento urbano e infraestruturas a nível municipal.

Regulamentação do Código Técnico do Cartão de Cidadão: Regulamenta os mecanismos técnicos de acesso e leitura do código técnico do cartão de cidadão. Este novo código permite uma identificação simplificada para acesso a determinados serviços, assegurando um equilíbrio entre segurança, usabilidade e proteção de dados pessoais. Anteriormente, não existia uma regulamentação explícita para o uso generalizado deste código técnico. Os cidadãos terão um acesso digital mais cómodo e seguro a serviços públicos e privados.

Regime de Subvenções para Hidrogénio e Gases Renováveis (PRR): Estabelece um regime de subvenções para o hidrogénio renovável e outros gases de origem renovável, no âmbito do Plano de Recuperação e Resiliência (PRR). Anteriormente, não havia um mecanismo de subvenção tão específico e direcionado para estes gases no âmbito do PRR. Anteriormente, a aplicação da taxa reduzida de IVA poderia ter condições mais abrangentes. 🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation amending the EU AI Act: This regulation delays the application of key obligations for Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems. Previously, most obligations for "high-risk" AI systems were due to apply from August 2, 2026, and transparency obligations for AI-generated content were also set for August 2, 2026. Now, the application date for standalone high-risk AI systems is delayed until December 2, 2027, while for high-risk AI systems embedded in products (e.g., medical devices, toys), the new application date is August 2, 2028. Additionally, the deadline for providers to implement transparency solutions, such as watermarking AI-generated content, is shortened from six months to three months, effective December 2, 2026, for systems already on the market before August 2, 2026. A new prohibition on AI systems that generate non-consensual sexual and intimate content or child sexual abuse material (CSAM) also applies from December 2, 2026. This primarily impacts tech companies and developers of AI systems, providing them with more time to ensure compliance with complex high-risk AI rules.

Regulation on temporary derogation from ePrivacy Directive for combating online child sexual abuse: This regulation introduces a temporary measure allowing providers of number-independent interpersonal communications services to process personal and other data to combat online child sexual abuse. Previously, Directive 2002/58/EC (the ePrivacy Directive) ensured a strict right to privacy and confidentiality of communications. Now, providers of services such as messaging apps are temporarily permitted to detect and report online child sexual abuse material and remove it from their platforms, deviating from previous privacy rules, until a long-term legal framework is established. This impacts technology companies operating communication services within the EU.

New EU Steel Regulation: This regulation, which entered into application on July 1, 2026, establishes new protective measures for the EU steel sector against global overcapacity. Previously, the EU's steel safeguard expired, leaving the sector more exposed. Now, tariff-free quotas are set at 18.3 million tonnes per year for 26 categories of steel products, with an out-of-quota duty of 50% for imports exceeding these limits. Companies importing steel into the EU must now also provide traceability information regarding the "melt & pour" stage of the steel's production. Half of the annual import quota is reserved for preferential trading partners, with the remainder available to all. This directly affects businesses involved in steel imports and manufacturing within the EU, including those in Portugal, aiming to ensure the long-term viability of the European steel industry.

Temporary €3 Customs Duty on Low-Value E-commerce Parcels: This measure, applicable from July 1, 2026, introduces a fixed customs duty on small e-commerce parcels entering the EU. Previously, shipments valued at less than €150 sent directly to consumers were exempt from customs charges. Now, a fixed customs duty of €3 per item will be applied to parcels valued below €150, charged per category of goods, and will remain in place until the EU customs data hub becomes operational. Furthermore, e-commerce platforms and sellers sending goods into the EU via distance sales are now designated as the importer and are responsible for all customs formalities and payments, shifting the burden from the final EU consumer. This impacts Portuguese consumers and e-commerce businesses by increasing costs for low-value imports and changing customs responsibilities.

Energy Efficiency Directive (EU/2023/1791) – Enhanced Annual Savings Obligation: From 2026 onwards, the revised Energy Efficiency Directive introduces more ambitious annual energy savings obligations for EU countries. Previously, EU countries were required to achieve new annual savings of at least 1.3% of final energy consumption in 2024-2025. Now, this obligation increases to at least 1.5% of final energy consumption in 2026-2027, further rising to 1.9% in 2028-2030. Additionally, large energy-consuming organizations must implement certified energy management systems by 2027. This applies to EU countries and major energy users across sectors such as manufacturing, data centers, and utilities, compelling them to implement more aggressive energy-saving measures.

Why it matters: This week saw significant regulatory adjustments in key sectors, from AI and digital privacy to trade and energy efficiency, demonstrating the EU's ongoing efforts to balance innovation, consumer protection, and industrial competitiveness through targeted legislative changes and new obligations.

SEC Expands Abbreviated Debt Tender Offer Framework: Effective June 30, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) Division of Corporation Finance issued an Exemptive Order that significantly expands and simplifies the framework for abbreviated tender and exchange offers for non-convertible debt securities. Previously, these offers typically required a minimum of 20 business days to remain open, but now they can be held open for a minimum of five business days. The order also expands eligible participants to include Institutional Accredited Investors (IAIs) alongside Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIBs) and non-U.S. persons, and removes the requirement for a concurrent retail cash option. This change streamlines corporate liability management, offering companies a faster, more flexible mechanism for debt restructuring, potentially increasing market efficiency.

U.S. Declines Automatic USMCA Extension, Initiates Annual Reviews: On July 1, 2026, the United States formally declined to renew the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) in its current form at its six-year joint review, opting instead to initiate a process of annual reviews for up to ten years rather than an automatic 16-year extension. The U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) Jamieson Greer stated that ongoing bilateral negotiations with Canada and Mexico will occur to address "shortcomings and trade deficits," although the USMCA remains in force pending further resolution or termination. This introduces trade policy uncertainty for North American businesses, potentially leading to more restrictive trade conditions and prolonged negotiations.

DOJ National Security Division Issues First Declination Under New Corporate Enforcement Policy: On July 2, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice's (DOJ) National Security Division (NSD) issued its first declination of prosecution under its revised Corporate Enforcement Policy to Robert Bosch GmbH. This decision, which allows the company to avoid prosecution, was based on Bosch's voluntary self-disclosure, full cooperation, and extensive remediation efforts regarding alleged export control violations. Concurrently, the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) imposed a $36 million civil penalty on Bosch for exporting foreign-produced sensor products and software to Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., a China-based party on the Entity List. Supreme Court Limits FTC Commissioner Removal Protections: On June 29, 2026, in *Trump v. Slaughter*, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the President may remove Federal Trade Commission (FTC) commissioners at will, explicitly overturning the "for-cause" removal protections that had been in place since 1935. Previously, FTC commissioners could only be removed for "inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office." This decision fundamentally shifts the balance of power, making the FTC significantly more responsive to presidential directives and potentially altering its regulatory and antitrust enforcement priorities.
