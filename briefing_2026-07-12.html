🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Clarificação do Regime do Património Imobiliário Público: O Decreto-Lei n.º 139/2026, de 10 de julho, clarifica a interpretação do artigo 33.º do Decreto-Lei n.º 87-A/2025, estabelecendo que o despacho do Primeiro-Ministro para a alienação, permuta, oneração ou cedência de utilização de património imobiliário do Estado e do setor público é uma autorização administrativa prévia. Previamente, o despacho do Primeiro-Ministro era requerido, mas a sua natureza e articulação com o regime legal existente eram menos explícitas. A nova regra define-o como uma autorização administrativa a ser proferida antes de outras autorizações legais, visando uma visão estratégica do Governo para as políticas públicas, nomeadamente na habitação, e exclui contratos de arrendamento e cedências com valores anuais inferiores a 7.500 € e prazos até seis anos. O impacto prático é um controlo centralizado mais robusto sobre transações imobiliárias públicas.

Certificação de Sistemas de Informação Escolares: O Decreto-Lei n.º 138/2026, de 10 de julho, torna obrigatória a certificação prévia dos sistemas de informação utilizados em contexto escolar e pedagógico no ensino não superior (público, privado e cooperativo). Anteriormente, não existia um mandato nacional explícito para esta certificação. O objetivo é assegurar que estes sistemas respeitam os direitos fundamentais, em particular a proteção de dados de menores e a segurança da informação. Os sistemas existentes têm até 1 de janeiro de 2027 para obter a certificação. O impacto prático é a exigência de que as escolas invistam na segurança e proteção de dados dos seus sistemas digitais, garantindo maior privacidade e segurança para alunos e docentes.

Prorrogação de Apoios e Medidas Pós-Tempestade "Kristin": O Decreto-Lei n.º 140/2026, de 10 de julho, altera o Decreto-Lei n.º 40-A/2026, prorrogando medidas excecionais de simplificação administrativa para a reconstrução após a "Tempestade Kristin". Especificamente, estende a suspensão da exigência de autorização prévia para demolição ou remoção de amianto e certas disposições de gestão de resíduos até 30 de setembro de 2026. Previamente, esta suspensão havia terminado em 28 de abril de 2026. Adicionalmente, estabelece um regime de apoio financeiro aos operadores de radiodifusão afetados pela tempestade. O impacto prático é a continuidade da desburocratização dos esforços de recuperação nas zonas afetadas e o apoio a um setor específico da comunicação social.

Revisão das Taxas do Imposto sobre Produtos Petrolíferos e Energéticos (ISP): A Portaria n.º 286-A/2026/1, de 10 de julho, procede à revisão e fixação das taxas unitárias do Imposto sobre Produtos Petrolíferos e Energéticos (ISP). Previamente, as taxas de ISP eram as que estavam em vigor. As novas taxas agora fixadas alteram o regime de tributação sobre combustíveis e outros produtos energéticos. O impacto prático é uma alteração direta nos custos de combustíveis e energia para consumidores e empresas.

Novo Regime de IVA de Grupo: O novo regime de IVA de grupo entrou em vigor em Portugal em 1 de julho de 2026, após a publicação das instruções oficiais. Anteriormente, as empresas eram tratadas como entidades separadas para efeitos de IVA. Agora, empresas relacionadas que cumpram critérios financeiros, económicos e organizacionais específicos (por exemplo, a empresa-mãe deter pelo menos 75% do capital social e 50% dos direitos de voto das subsidiárias por mais de um ano) podem ser tratadas como uma única entidade sujeita a IVA. Isto permite compensar saldos de IVA entre membros do grupo e exclui transações internas de IVA. O impacto prático é a simplificação da conformidade fiscal e potencial melhoria do fluxo de caixa para grupos empresariais qualificados.

Why it matters: O novo regime de IVA de grupo representa uma mudança significativa na forma como as grandes empresas e os grupos económicos gerem as suas obrigações fiscais, potencialmente reduzindo a carga administrativa e otimizando o fluxo de caixa.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

New EU Road Safety Rules Mandating Advanced Vehicle Safety Features: From July 7, 2026, all new passenger cars and vans sold in the European Union must be equipped with five advanced safety features, including advanced emergency braking systems capable of detecting pedestrians and cyclists, advanced driver distraction warning systems, improved forward visibility for drivers, and new testing requirements for worn tyres. Previously, some of these features were optional or lacked standardized requirements. This will increase manufacturing costs but aims to significantly reduce road accidents and fatalities across the EU.

Capital Requirements Directive VI (CRD VI) – Critical Grandfathering Cutoff for Cross-border Banking Services: As of July 11, 2026, non-EU credit institutions providing "core banking services" (such as deposit-taking, guarantees, and lending) into the EU will find that contracts entered into on or after this date will no longer benefit from transitional protection. This means that, in anticipation of the full prohibition taking effect on January 11, 2027, these new contracts must be structured assuming the requirement of an authorised local presence (third-country branch or EU subsidiary). Previously, existing cross-border services often operated without such mandatory local EU presence. This has a significant impact on non-EU banks, including US banks, conducting wholesale cross-border activities in Europe, potentially affecting the availability and structure of financial services for Portuguese investors and businesses.

Directive Amending the Consumer Rights Directive – New Online Withdrawal Function: Entering into force on July 9, 2026, this directive requires businesses selling products, subscriptions, software, or services to consumers in the EU via an online interface to provide a simple, online mechanism for consumers to withdraw from contracts. Previously, while consumers had withdrawal rights (e.g., a 14-day cooling-off period), the method for exercising these rights online was not uniformly mandated. Now, if a contract can be entered into online, it must also be terminable online. This change impacts all businesses engaging in remote sales to EU consumers, necessitating updates to their online platforms and terms of service to combat "dark patterns" and enhance consumer protection.

EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) – Full Application of Requirements for High-Risk AI Systems: From August 2, 2026, the stringent requirements for AI systems classified as high-risk will become fully applicable. This mandates providers and deployers of such systems (e.g., AI used in medical devices, employment, or critical infrastructure) to implement comprehensive risk management systems, ensure high data quality, provide human oversight, maintain detailed technical documentation, and adhere to strict transparency and cybersecurity standards. Previously, the AI Act entered into force in August 2024, but most of the specific compliance obligations for high-risk systems were subject to phased implementation. This represents a pivotal shift to full legal application for companies developing, importing, or deploying high-risk AI in the EU, particularly impacting tech investors.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1554 on Urban Mobility Data: Adopted on July 9, 2026, this regulation sets out specific rules for the collection and submission of urban mobility data to the European Commission, detailing data requirements per urban node for sustainability, safety, and accessibility in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2024/1679. Previously, data collection and reporting for urban mobility varied significantly across Member States and urban areas. Now, local and regional authorities and private urban mobility operators across the EU, including in Portugal, must adhere to harmonised standards for data collection and reporting to the Commission.

Why it matters: This week highlights the EU's continued push to embed safety, transparency, and consumer protection across critical sectors, from automotive and financial services to digital platforms and urban planning, while preparing for the full compliance phase of its landmark AI legislation.

"21st Century Road to Housing Act" Becomes Law: This legislation was enacted on July 11, 2026, without the President's signature, after passing both chambers of Congress with broad bipartisan support. The law introduces over 40 provisions aimed at increasing housing affordability, including raising the cap on certain affordable housing conversions by 100,000 (previously 455,000) and formally writing the disaster recovery program into law for at least three years, enabling HUD to pre-allocate disaster funds. This action aims to stimulate homebuilding and make the housing market more accessible for individual buyers.

USCIS Interim Final Rule on Immigration Benefit Request Signatures: Effective July 10, 2026, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) implemented an Interim Final Rule amending 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(a)(7) regarding signatures on immigration benefit requests. This rule grants USCIS increased discretion to reject or deny requests determined to have an invalid signature even after initial acceptance, and allows the agency to retain associated filing fees. Previously, USCIS generally rejected such requests, providing an opportunity for correction. This change heightens the importance of precise signature compliance for all immigration filings.

Presidential Proclamation on Commercial Aircraft Imports: On July 9, 2026, President Trump signed a proclamation directing the Secretary of Commerce and the U.S. Trade Representative to initiate or continue trade negotiations with trading partners to adjust import levels of commercial aircraft, jet engines, and aircraft and engine parts. This action follows a Section 232 investigation which determined these imports threatened national security, but opts for diplomatic negotiation over immediate tariffs, with cabinet officials reporting on progress within six months. This signals a preference for trade diplomacy to address national security concerns over direct punitive tariffs in this sector.

Department of Veterans Affairs Streamlines Emergency Treatment Notification: The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) published a final rule in the Federal Register on July 10, 2026 (effective August 10, 2026), amending 38 CFR Part 17 (Docket No. VA-2025-VHA-0007). This rule adds a new, centralized web-based process for veterans, their representatives, and eligible entities or providers to notify the VA for the authorization of emergency treatment under the Veterans Community Care Program (VCCP). This streamlines the process, making it easier for veterans to secure authorization for their emergency care.

OFAC Revokes Iran Oil Sanctions Waiver: On July 10, 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) revoked a temporary sanctions waiver that permitted transactions related to the production, delivery, and sale of Iranian-origin crude, petroleum, and petrochemical products. This action tightens economic pressure on Iran by closing a specific loophole for its oil and petrochemical exports.
