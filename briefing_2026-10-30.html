🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Redução das Taxas de IRS (Autorização Legislativa): A Lei n.º 64-B/2026, de 22 de outubro, autoriza o Governo a reduzir as taxas gerais do Imposto sobre o Rendimento das Pessoas Singulares (IRS) aplicáveis aos primeiros seis escalões de rendimento coletável. Anteriormente, as taxas variavam por escalão. Agora, as taxas normais aplicáveis aos primeiros seis escalões são de 12,2 %, 15,2 %, 20,7 %, 23,6 %, 30,6 % e 34,6 %, respetivamente, com os correspondentes ajustamentos das taxas médias. O impacto prático é uma potencial diminuição da carga fiscal para os cidadãos com rendimentos nos escalões mais baixos, assim que o Governo exercer a autorização legislativa. [Diário da República]

Regulamentação da Nova Lei da Nacionalidade (Aprovado): O Conselho de Ministros aprovou, a 29 de outubro de 2026, um Decreto-Lei que regulamenta a nova Lei da Nacionalidade, tornando a concessão da cidadania portuguesa mais exigente. Anteriormente, os critérios podiam ser menos rigorosos em termos de ligação efetiva a Portugal. Agora, exige-se uma ligação mais efetiva, incluindo conhecimento da língua, história e cultura, adesão aos valores constitucionais, e uma declaração solene de aceitação desses valores. Os prazos para a atribuição de nacionalidade a estrangeiros foram alargados, subindo para sete anos para cidadãos da CPLP (anteriormente, podiam ser mais curtos) e para dez anos para os demais. O impacto prático é que o processo de obtenção da cidadania portuguesa será mais rigoroso e demorado para os requerentes. [Portugal.gov.pt / ECO]

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU Inc. Corporate Law Regime: Previously, companies wishing to operate across the EU had to navigate 27 distinct national corporate law systems. Now, following the Council's adoption of its position on October 23, 2026, companies, particularly startups and scaleups, will have the option to register under a new 28th "EU Inc." regime, allowing for online registration within two days at a maximum cost of €100. This new framework aims to simplify cross-border business and investment, enabling Portuguese entrepreneurs to establish and scale businesses more easily across the single market.

Omnibus V Defense Simplification Package: Previously, defense procurement faced varied national rules and lower thresholds for EU scrutiny. Formally approved by the Council on October 9, 2026, this package introduces new regulations and a directive. The regulations, taking effect in November 2026 with direct EU-wide application, raise thresholds for the Defence and Security Procurement Directive (up to €2 million for supply and service contracts, and €10 million for works contracts), mandate faster permitting for defense readiness projects, and introduce General Transfer Licences for moving defense equipment between certified suppliers under EU-funded programmes or intra-EU industrial partnerships. The accompanying directive will require national transposition. This significantly streamlines the defense sector, benefiting EU defense companies, including any in Portugal, by reducing administrative complexities and potentially boosting investment and cross-border cooperation.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2391 on Cellulose Ethers: Previously, imports of certain cellulose ethers from the People's Republic of China and the Republic of Korea were not subject to specific surveillance measures. Published on October 29, 2026, this new regulation now subjects imports of certain cellulose ethers originating in these countries to registration. This registration is a preliminary step to allow for the potential levy of anti-dumping duties, creating a new administrative and reporting obligation for importers. This directly impacts businesses importing these specific chemicals, potentially leading to increased costs and necessitating supply chain adjustments.

Energy Efficiency Directive (EU) 2023/1791 – Mandatory Energy Audits: Previously, mandatory energy audits largely applied to "large enterprises" based on headcount and turnover. From October 2026, companies with annual energy consumption exceeding 10 terajoules (TJ) (approximately 2.78 GWh) must conduct a regular, independent energy audit at least every four years, unless they already operate a certified energy management system. Furthermore, by October 2027, companies consuming over 85 TJ (approximately 23.6 GWh) must implement a certified energy management system. This directive, although adopted in 2023 and due for national transposition by October 2025, sees its most significant corporate compliance obligations coming into application this month, affecting a broad range of energy-intensive businesses across the EU, including those in Portugal.

Why it matters: This week marks a shift towards enabling more agile EU-wide business operations through the "EU Inc" regime, while simultaneously tightening environmental and trade compliance, and streamlining the defense industry.

Sanctions on Rwandan Ministry of Defence and Financial Institution: On October 28, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) imposed sanctions on the Rwandan Ministry of Defence and the Zigama Credit and Savings Society, a Rwandan financial institution. Previously, OFAC had sanctioned the Rwanda Defence Force; this action extends blocking duties to the Ministry of Defence and a key financial institution, aiming to compel Rwanda to cease support for M23 and withdraw its troops from eastern DRC. This is a significant move to enforce regional peace agreements and potentially stabilize mineral-rich areas.
