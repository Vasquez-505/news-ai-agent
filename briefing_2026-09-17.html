🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Reforma do Código dos Contratos Públicos (Decreto-Lei n.º 177/2026): Este diploma, que entra em vigor a 1 de outubro de 2026, altera significativamente o Código dos Contratos Públicos para simplificar e agilizar os procedimentos. Os limiares para ajuste direto em empreitadas sobem de €30.000 para €150.000, e para consulta prévia de €150.000 para €1.000.000. Em bens e serviços, o ajuste direto passa de €20.000 para €75.000 e a consulta prévia de €75.000 para €130.000. O impacto prático é uma redução de formalismos e burocracia, acelerando o investimento público e o acesso a fundos comunitários para empresas.

Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação (Adiamento de Entrada em Vigor): A entrada em vigor da revisão do Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação (RJUE), aprovada pelo Decreto-Lei n.º 108/2026, de 29 de maio, foi adiada de 3 de agosto de 2026 para 1 de outubro de 2026. Esta medida concede mais tempo aos municípios para adaptarem as suas plataformas informáticas e implementarem a regulamentação complementar, beneficiando o setor imobiliário e da construção com um período adicional de preparação.

Regime de Acolhimento de Estrangeiros e Proteção Internacional (Lei n.º 62/2026): Esta lei, aprovada a 10 de setembro de 2026, assegura a execução de vários Regulamentos da União Europeia e transpõe Diretivas (UE) de 2024, alterando o regime de acolhimento de estrangeiros ou apátridas em centros de instalação temporária e o regime jurídico de entrada, permanência, saída e afastamento de estrangeiros do território nacional. A instalação, quando determinada, mantém-se agora até à concessão de visto ou autorização de residência, ou até à execução da decisão de expulsão/reembarque, devendo ser judicialmente reapreciada a cada 30 dias. Isto clarifica o enquadramento legal e os prazos para indivíduos em busca de proteção internacional ou acolhimento temporário.

Regulamento da Transformação de Veículos (Decreto-Lei n.º 184/2026): Aprovado a 16 de setembro de 2026, este Decreto-Lei estabelece os princípios gerais para a transformação de veículos a motor e seus reboques, visando manter as condições de segurança rodoviária e proteção ambiental verificadas na homologação. Regula a atividade dos transformadores, exigindo o reconhecimento pelo Instituto da Mobilidade e dos Transportes, I. P., e define as "alterações de características" (e.g., motor, transmissão, chassis). Garante maior clareza e segurança no processo de modificação de veículos, com coimas para o incumprimento (20% para a entidade fiscalizadora, 20% para a instrutora/aplicadora da coima, 60% para o Estado).

Extinção da Carreira de Administrador Prisional (Decreto-Lei n.º 186/2026): Este diploma, de 16 de setembro de 2026, extingue a carreira não revista de regime especial de administrador prisional e integra os seus trabalhadores na carreira geral de técnico superior. Anteriormente, regiam-se pelo Decreto-Lei n.º 351/99, de 3 de setembro. Os trabalhadores são reposicionados na posição remuneratória imediatamente seguinte à que detinham a 30 de junho de 2025, sem perda remuneratória. Adicionalmente, quem possuir 10 ou mais pontos de avaliação de desempenho a 30 de junho de 2025, será reposicionado novamente a 1 de janeiro de 2026, e com 20 ou mais pontos, a 1 de janeiro de 2027. A medida valoriza e harmoniza a carreira destes profissionais na administração pública.

Why it matters: As alterações ao Código dos Contratos Públicos e o adiamento da entrada em vigor do RJUE são as mudanças mais significativas, visando desburocratizar e acelerar os processos de investimento público e privado no setor da construção e imobiliário.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) - Article 14 (Vulnerability and Incident Reporting) and Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) - Article 3(1) (Access-by-Design Obligations): The Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) now requires manufacturers of "products with digital elements" to report severe cybersecurity incidents and actively exploited vulnerabilities to national authorities within 24 hours of becoming aware of them. Concurrently, the Data Act's "access-by-design" rule mandates that connected products and related services placed on the market from this week must be designed to ensure users can easily and securely access the data they generate by default, in a usable, machine-readable format. Previously, the EU lacked an all-sector mandatory cybersecurity framework for hardware and software products, and users often lacked direct control or easy access to data generated by their connected devices. CRA Article 14 applies from September 11, 2026, and Data Act Article 3(1) applies from September 12, 2026. These obligations affect manufacturers of connected products and digital services, including those headquartered outside the EU with EU market activity, across sectors such as manufacturing, technology, health care, financial services, and agritech. These measures significantly increase cybersecurity accountability for producers and empower users with greater control over their device-generated data, directly impacting product design and incident response for businesses, including Portuguese tech firms.

Reform of the EU Customs Code (Approved by European Parliament): This reform introduces a new handling fee for each item bought from non-EU web shops and sent directly to EU consumers, with Member States commencing collection by November 1, 2026, at the latest. Additionally, sellers and platforms facilitating distance sales from non-EU countries to EU customers will now be treated as importers, obliging them to provide customs authorities with all required data, pay or guarantee any charges, and ensure goods comply with EU laws. To ensure accountability, these companies must be established in the EU or represented by an EU-based entity holding authorised economic operator (AEO) or trusted trader status. Companies repeatedly ignoring EU rules may face fines of at least 1% (and up to 6%) of the total value of goods imported into the EU in the previous 12 months. Previously, the framework for e-commerce imports was less stringent, often leading to fragmented responsibilities and challenges in compliance checks for the high volume of small parcels. This reform fundamentally shifts responsibility for customs compliance and product conformity, aiming to level the playing field for EU businesses and enhance consumer protection, affecting all e-commerce businesses, including those operating from or into Portugal.

Commission Guidelines on exclusionary conduct by dominant companies: These final Guidelines clarify the European Commission's approach to assessing exclusionary abuses under Article 102 TFEU, confirming that companies with market shares below 40% will usually not be considered dominant, thus introducing a "soft safe harbor." The Guidelines maintain a framework requiring two cumulative conditions for conduct to breach Article 102 and allow dominant companies to rebut presumptions by presenting sufficient evidence that their conduct does not distort effective competition. This approach represents a recalibration from the 2008 Guidance Paper, which focused more on an economic approach, and a softening from a more presumption-based draft in 2024 which had drawn significant stakeholder criticism. The final Guidelines will apply as of October 10, 2026, 30 days after their publication in the Official Journal on September 9, 2026. These guidelines offer increased clarity and predictability for large businesses and investors concerning competition law enforcement regarding market dominance, particularly for those with market shares potentially exceeding 40%.

Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (Directive 2024/825/EU): This Directive, entering into effect on September 27, 2026, prohibits vague or generic environmental claims (e.g., "eco-friendly," "green," "sustainable") unless they are substantiated with specific, recognised scientific evidence. It also bans claims of "carbon neutral" or "climate neutral" for products if based solely on purchased carbon credits or offset schemes, requiring such claims to reflect real emissions reductions within the company's value chain. Furthermore, third-party sustainability labels used in marketing must now originate from certification schemes officially approved under EU or national authority processes. Previously, businesses could employ broad, unsubstantiated environmental claims and use unverified sustainability labels, contributing to "greenwashing." The Directive applies to all businesses making environmental claims to consumers operating within the EU, covering advertising, product labeling, digital content, and social media communications. This significantly impacts marketing strategies and product communication across all sectors, requiring businesses, including Portuguese ones, to adopt greater transparency and provide verifiable evidence for their environmental claims.

Why it matters: This week marks a significant shift in EU regulatory enforcement, with new rules taking effect to enhance digital product security and data access, streamline e-commerce customs, clarify competition law for dominant firms, and combat greenwashing in consumer markets.
