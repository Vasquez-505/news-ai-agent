🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026 (New Portuguese Nationality Law): This law significantly alters the requirements for obtaining Portuguese nationality through naturalization. Previously, a universal period of 5 years of legal residence was required for all applicants. Under the new regime, nationals of CPLP (Community of Portuguese Language Countries) and EU Member States must now complete 7 years of legal residence, while other foreign nationals require 10 years. Furthermore, the qualifying period for residency is now counted from the date of the actual issuance of the first residence permit, rather than the date of application submission. Nationality applications submitted prior to May 19, 2026, remain subject to the previous 5-year rule. This change considerably extends the waiting period for many foreign residents seeking Portuguese citizenship and may prolong the overall process due to administrative delays in permit issuance. (Source: Diário da República)

Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026 (Housing Tax Amendments and New Incentive Regimes): Approved on May 20, 2026, this decree-law introduces several new housing-related tax measures aimed at increasing affordable housing supply. It establishes a reduced 6% VAT rate for qualifying residential construction and renovation projects designated for permanent housing or long-term rental schemes, a significant reduction from previous standard VAT rates (e.g., 23%). This reduced VAT rate applies to projects commenced between September 25, 2025, and the end of 2029, and will enter into force in July 2026. Additionally, the law introduces a reduced 10% IRS tax rate on qualifying rental income (down from standard progressive IRS rates) until the end of 2029, and offers capital gains exemptions when proceeds from property sales are reinvested into residential rental properties under specific conditions. These measures aim to lower construction and renovation costs for eligible housing and provide tax incentives for landlords, encouraging more properties to enter the long-term rental market. (Source: PwC Portugal Tax Flash, IMI Daily)

EU Regulation 2024/1028 (Short-Term Rental Data Infrastructure): This European Union regulation became fully applicable from May 20, 2026, and significantly impacts the operation of short-term rental properties in Portugal. Previously, online platforms were not systematically required to verify or publicly display the registration numbers (RNAL numbers in Portugal) provided by hosts. Under the new regulation, major online short-term rental platforms, such as Airbnb and Booking.com, are now mandated to collect and publicly display these RNAL numbers from hosts, and to conduct random checks on the validity of these registrations. This enforces stricter compliance for property owners operating short-term rentals, as unlicensed listings will face automatic removal from platforms. (Source: IMI Daily, Global Law Experts)
