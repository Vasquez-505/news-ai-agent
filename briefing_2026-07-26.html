Esta alteração impacta diretamente o preço dos combustíveis para consumidores e empresas.

Anteriormente, Portugal não possuía um enquadramento regulamentar específico para o lobby. A nova lei, que entra em vigor no dia 27 de julho de 2026, visa dar mais transparência ao processo de decisão pública, regulando a interação entre os lobistas e os decisores públicos. Esta medida pretende democratizar o acesso de todos os atores da economia aos decisores públicos.

Novo Regime de IVA de Grupo: Publicado em 5 de junho de 2026 pela Lei n.º 62/2025, o novo regime de IVA de grupo entrou em vigor em 1º de julho de 2026, com clarificações adicionais divulgadas pela Autoridade Tributária e Aduaneira em 23 de julho de 2026. Anteriormente, empresas relacionadas eram tratadas individualmente para efeitos de IVA. Agora, permite que empresas relacionadas sejam consideradas uma única entidade sujeita a IVA, possibilitando a compensação de saldos de IVA entre membros e desconsiderando transações internas para efeitos do imposto. As empresas-mãe devem deter pelo menos 75% do capital social das subsidiárias e controlar mais de 50% dos direitos de voto para elegibilidade. O regime simplifica o cumprimento fiscal e otimiza o fluxo de caixa para grupos empresariais.

O novo modelo visa promover a igualdade de oportunidades no acesso e frequência do ensino superior, reduzindo os obstáculos económicos através de apoios sociais diretos (bolsas de estudo) e indiretos (residências estudantis, apoio psicológico, adaptação de recursos para estudantes com necessidades educativas especiais). Esta medida visa mitigar a influência da origem social nos percursos educativos dos estudantes universitários.

Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) – Ban on Destruction of Unsold Consumer Products (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781): This regulation prohibits the destruction of specific unsold consumer products, starting with apparel, clothing accessories, and footwear, across the EU. Previously, there was no EU-wide ban, leading to varied national practices. Now, companies manufacturing, distributing, or retailing these products must comply, ensuring sustainable waste management. The regulation applies from July 19, 2026.

EU Electricity Market Reform: New rules designed to enhance the resilience of the electricity market, boost renewable energy integration, and strengthen consumer protection have come into effect. Previously, consumers faced less choice and weaker safeguards in the energy market. Now, consumers have more options for fixed-price and dynamic contracts, clearer information, stronger safeguards when changing suppliers, and new opportunities to generate and share renewable energy. Vulnerable consumers are also afforded increased protection from disconnection. These rules apply from July 17, 2026.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1747 on Imports of Aliphatic-Aromatic Copolyesters: This regulation subjects imports of certain aliphatic-aromatic copolyesters originating in the People's Republic of China to registration. Before this, these imports were not under a specific registration regime. Now, businesses importing these particular chemical products from China must register them, which allows for monitoring and potential future trade defense measures. This regulation was published on July 23, 2026.

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) (Regulation (EU) 2025/40): This regulation, entering into application on August 12, 2026, establishes a single, harmonised set of rules for packaging across all 27 EU Member States, replacing the previous fragmented national directives. From this date, companies must maintain documentation, including an EU Declaration of Conformity and Technical Documentation, proving packaging compliance with new limits on PFAS and heavy metals. Furthermore, importers from outside the EU are required to appoint an authorised representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State they export to. This will significantly impact Portuguese manufacturers, importers, and distributors, as packaging compliance becomes a mandatory precondition for placing products on the EU market.

EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act): The main obligations of the EU AI Act will begin to apply from August 2, 2026. This landmark regulation introduces a risk-based framework for AI systems; previously, there was no overarching EU-wide legislation for AI. High-risk AI systems, such as those used in employment decisions or biometric identification, will now face mandatory requirements for documentation, transparency, and human oversight. Providers, deployers, importers, and distributors of AI systems in the EU, including those in Portugal, will need to ensure compliance, with responsibility extending to businesses using third-party AI solutions.

Why it matters: This week highlights the EU's continued push towards comprehensive regulatory frameworks in critical sectors, particularly through the immediate application of new sustainability requirements for products and the imminent operationalisation of rules governing packaging and artificial intelligence, signalling a significant shift towards product-level and digital-era compliance.
