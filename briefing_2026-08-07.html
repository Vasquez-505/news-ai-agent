A taxa de esforço máxima recomendada diminuiu de 50% para 45% do rendimento mensal do cliente. Anteriormente, os prazos de maturidade variavam em três escalões; agora, para mutuários até 35 anos, o prazo máximo é de 40 anos, e para os com mais de 35 anos, é de 35 anos. O impacto prático é uma maior dificuldade para as famílias acederem a crédito, exigindo uma menor percentagem do rendimento comprometida com dívidas e ajustando os planos de pagamento a idades mais jovens.

Novo Modelo de Pagamento do Imposto Único de Circulação (IUC): Foi publicado o Decreto-Lei n.º 161/2026, que altera o regime de pagamento do IUC, embora com efeitos a partir de 1 de janeiro de 2027. Anteriormente, o imposto era liquidado no mês da matrícula do veículo; agora, passará a ser pago em datas fixas anuais. Para valores até 100 euros, o pagamento será único até final de abril. Se o valor for superior a 100 euros e igual ou inferior a 500 euros, será pago em duas prestações (abril e outubro). Para valores mais elevados, o pagamento ocorrerá em três prestações (abril, julho e outubro). O impacto para os proprietários de veículos será uma maior previsibilidade nas datas de pagamento, mas também uma potencial reestruturação orçamental para acomodar as novas mensalidades.

Criação da Estrutura Nacional SAFE: O Decreto-Lei n.º 163/2026 estabeleceu o modelo de governação do empréstimo atribuído a Portugal no âmbito do Instrumento de Ação para a Segurança da Europa (SAFE). Esta nova estrutura será responsável pela coordenação, acompanhamento e monitorização da participação de Portugal neste mecanismo europeu, que visa financiar investimentos estratégicos na área da defesa. O impacto para o país é a modernização das capacidades das Forças Armadas e o estímulo à participação da indústria nacional em projetos de cooperação europeia, gerindo um financiamento superior a 5,8 mil milhões de euros.

Simplificação de Procedimentos Relativos a Custas Processuais: O Decreto-Lei n.º 164/2026 foi aprovado para simplificar os procedimentos das secretarias judiciais relacionados com as custas processuais. Este diploma visa agilizar os trâmites administrativos no sistema judicial. O impacto prático é a redução da burocracia e um potencial aumento da eficiência na tramitação dos processos judiciais, beneficiando cidadãos e empresas que interagem com a justiça.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) – Transparency Obligations and High-Risk Systems: As of August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office, alongside national authorities, commenced enforcement of key provisions of the Artificial Intelligence Act. Previously, the AI Act had entered into force in phases, with prohibited AI practices applying from February 2025 and governance rules for General Purpose AI models from August 2025. Now, obligations for high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III, encompassing areas such as biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, and essential services, are fully applicable. Additionally, transparency rules now require any AI system interacting with humans, such as chatbots, to clearly inform users they are engaging with an AI, not a human. AI-generated or altered content, including deepfakes, must be labelled, and machine-readable marks are required for detection. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must also inform affected individuals. All providers, deployers, importers, and distributors of AI systems operating in or affecting the EU market must comply with these rules immediately from August 2, 2026, with a limited transitional period until December 2, 2026, for marking and detection obligations for existing generative AI systems. Non-compliance can lead to administrative fines of up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. This significantly impacts Portuguese businesses across various sectors utilizing or developing AI, requiring immediate audits and implementation of compliance procedures to avoid substantial penalties. [Source: European Commission, Cooley, Al Jazeera]

Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912 Extending Temporary Protection: On August 4, 2026, the Official Journal of the European Union published Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912, adopted on July 30, 2026, which extends the temporary protection initially introduced by Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/382. Previously, the temporary protection granted to individuals fleeing the war in Ukraine had a set expiry date. Now, this Decision ensures the continued legal status and rights of these individuals within the EU without a new end-date, extending the measures indefinitely. This decision directly impacts Portugal by ensuring continued support and integration pathways for beneficiaries of temporary protection residing in the country. [Source: EUR-Lex]

Commission Implementing Regulation Extending Suspension of EU Rebalancing Measures on US Exports: The European Commission adopted an Implementing Regulation on July 31, 2026, published on August 5, 2026, that extends the suspension of EU rebalancing measures on US exports to the European Union indefinitely. Previously, EU rebalancing measures, adopted on July 24, 2025 (via Commission Implementing Regulation 2025/1564) in response to potential US tariffs, had their suspension set to expire on August 6, 2026 (via Commission Implementing Regulation 2026/295). Now, this new Implementing Regulation ensures the seamless continuation of the suspension, providing greater certainty and stability in transatlantic trade for European businesses. This action prevents the re-application of tariffs on a package of EU imports from the US valued at EUR 93 billion, directly benefiting Portuguese importers and businesses engaged in transatlantic trade by maintaining predictable trade conditions. This measure establishes price floors and a 15% tariff, effective December 4, 2026, for imported polysilicon ($21/kg), polysilicon ingots and wafers ($100/kg), solar cells ($0.22/watt), and solar modules ($0.38/watt), while also authorizing the Commerce Department to create an incentive program for domestic production. This aims to safeguard U.S. polysilicon manufacturers from foreign competition, particularly from China, with substantial implications for global supply chains in the solar and semiconductor industries.

DOJ Agreement with Connecticut Children's to End Pediatric Gender-Affirming Care: The Department of Justice announced on August 5, 2026, a resolution with Connecticut Children's Medical Center, requiring the facility to cease providing "sex-rejecting procedures" to minors. This is the third such agreement secured by the DOJ in an ongoing national investigation into federal law violations related to pediatric gender-affirming care.

SEC Technical Amendments to Investment Company Governance Standards: Effective August 6, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) adopted technical amendments to rule 0-1(a)(7) under the Investment Company Act of 1940. These amendments update the Code of Federal Regulations to reflect a Federal court's vacatur, effective July 6, 2006, of certain prior amendments to the governance standards for registered investment companies and business development companies. This action aligns the regulatory text with existing legal precedents by reverting the fund governance standards to those in effect before the vacated requirements.
