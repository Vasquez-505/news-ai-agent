US-Iran Negotiations & Strait of Hormuz: High-level negotiations between the United States and Iran, mediated by Pakistan and Qatar, continued on June 21-22, 2026, in Switzerland, aimed at expanding a fragile interim agreement and permanently ending the "Iran war." Amidst these talks, Iran announced on June 21 that it had re-closed the Strait of Hormuz, linking the move to Israeli attacks in Lebanon and demanding economic relief and a full ceasefire in Lebanon, while US President Donald Trump issued threats of further attacks on Iran if it hinders the Strait's reopening. The closure created conflicting claims, with the US Central Command stating commercial vessels were still passing through, though reports indicated a significant drop in transiting ships from 26 to 5 on June 21. The renewed tensions have complicated the peace talks, with Iranian officials emphasizing that nuclear issues would not be discussed until other clauses of the Memorandum of Understanding, including economic relief and a complete cessation of hostilities in Lebanon, were met. The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint for global energy shipments, with its closure estimated to reduce global GDP by roughly $2.2 trillion annually and having already caused oil prices to surpass $100 per barrel earlier in 2026.

Cambodian Humanitarian Crisis: Cambodian authorities are continuing their crackdown on cyber scam compounds, leading to the liberation of approximately 200,000 trafficked workers from 35 countries. However, human rights groups are calling this a humanitarian crisis as thousands of these freed workers are now stranded in Cambodia with little to no money and no means to return to their home countries, facing visa overstay fines of $10 per day. Many workers are camping along riversides and in residential areas of Phnom Penh, unable to afford the fines or travel home, while the Cambodian government, through Information Minister Neth Pheaktra, acknowledges the problem but prioritizes the crackdown over direct aid for the stranded. This situation highlights the widespread issue of human trafficking and forced labor in cyber scam operations, creating a significant international humanitarian challenge that requires coordinated efforts to repatriate victims and address vulnerabilities across borders.

European Heatwave: A severe heatwave swept across parts of Europe on Sunday, June 21, with France experiencing temperatures reaching 40°C (104°F), prompting authorities to cancel trains, concerts, and sporting events. Public drinking was banned in "red alert" zones in France to mitigate health risks, and the Eiffel Tower and other venues set up misting stations to cool crowds. Spain, Italy, and Germany are also experiencing dangerously high temperatures, raising concerns about public health and infrastructure strain. This extreme weather event underscores the increasing frequency and intensity of heatwaves linked to climate change, posing significant risks to public health, economic activity, and infrastructure across densely populated regions.

Australia-Canada Defense Deal: Australia announced on Monday, June 22, a significant defense export agreement to sell advanced Over-the-Horizon Radar technology to Canada for A$2.5 billion (US$1.75 billion). This marks Australia's largest-ever defense export deal and its first overseas sale of the specialized radar, known as Jindalee Operational Radar Network, which is capable of detecting aircraft, ships, and missiles up to 3,000 km (1,864 miles) away. The technology will be used to bolster Canada's surveillance capabilities in its Arctic region, which accounts for about 40% of its landmass and faces much of Russia's Arctic territory. This deal strengthens defense cooperation between two key allies and significantly enhances Arctic security capabilities amidst rising geopolitical competition in the region, potentially influencing the balance of power and surveillance strategies in the High North.

🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Contratação de Médicos em Regime de Prestação de Serviço no SNS (Decreto-Lei n.º 115/2026): Disciplina a contratação de médicos (preferencialmente especialistas) em regime de prestação de serviço no Serviço Nacional de Saúde (SNS), estabelecendo requisitos de elegibilidade, forma e conteúdo dos contratos, e condições para a sua renovação ou cessação. Anteriormente, a contratação neste regime carecia de um enquadramento normativo próprio, o que podia levar a práticas menos transparentes. Agora, são introduzidas normas de incompatibilidade para prevenir a rotatividade estratégica ou a substituição de vínculos públicos por prestações externas mais onerosas, visando uma gestão mais racional e equitativa. O objetivo é assegurar maior transparência e eficácia na resposta a carências pontuais de recursos humanos no SNS.

Regime Extraordinário de Regularização de Ocupações de Casas de Função (Decreto-Lei n.º 116/2026): Estabelece um regime extraordinário para a regularização de situações de ocupação irregular de "casas de função" (habitações funcionais) pertencentes ao domínio privado do Estado e de institutos públicos. Previamente, o Regime Jurídico do Património Imobiliário Público impunha o dever de restituição da casa de função em caso de cessação de funções, falecimento ou transferência do funcionário, permitindo o despejo administrativo em caso de incumprimento. Este novo decreto-lei visa resolver as situações de ocupação irregular por ex-funcionários ou seus familiares, que impedem a boa gestão e utilização deste património imobiliário.

Diferimento do Pagamento de Contribuições à Segurança Social para Transportes de Mercadorias (Portaria n.º 266/2026/1): Fixa os procedimentos para uma medida excecional de diferimento do pagamento das contribuições à segurança social para os meses de referência de maio, junho e julho de 2026. Anteriormente, estas contribuições seriam devidas nos prazos regulares mensais. Agora, os beneficiários elegíveis (operadores de veículos que utilizam combustíveis fósseis, com inspeção periódica obrigatória válida e licenciados pelo IMT, I. P., para transporte de mercadorias por conta de outrem) podem pagar as suas obrigações até 25 de outubro de 2026, sem aplicação de juros de mora. Esta medida proporciona um alívio financeiro e de liquidez para as empresas de transporte de mercadorias.

Aumento da Capacidade de Recrutamento das Forças Armadas (Decreto-Lei aprovado a 18 de junho de 2026): O Governo aprovou um novo decreto-lei que eleva a capacidade máxima de recrutamento para as Forças Armadas para cerca de 31.000 militares. Anteriormente, o número de efetivos militares tinha vindo a diminuir progressivamente, de aproximadamente 29.000 em 2015 para perto de 23.000 em 2024. Esta medida visa inverter a tendência de quebra de efetivos, fortalecendo a capacidade operacional e de defesa nacional do país.

Novas Medidas Fiscais para a Habitação (Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026, publicado a 20 de maio, mas com entrada em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026): Introduz um conjunto abrangente de incentivos fiscais para dinamizar a oferta de habitação em Portugal. As principais alterações incluem: a aplicação de uma taxa reduzida de IVA de 6% na construção e reabilitação de habitação (anteriormente as taxas gerais de IVA eram superiores); os senhorios que pratiquem rendas moderadas passam a beneficiar de uma taxa de IRS de 10% (antes sujeita às taxas progressivas do IRS); e o reinvestimento em imóveis destinados ao arrendamento pode beneficiar de isenção de IRS. Além disso, é estabelecida uma taxa de IMT de 7,5% para a aquisição de imóveis urbanos destinados exclusivamente à habitação por não residentes, a menos que se tornem residentes fiscais ou destinem o imóvel a arrendamento de longa duração sob certas condições. Estas medidas entram em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026.

Why it matters: As novas medidas fiscais para a habitação, que entram em vigor em setembro, representam a mudança mais significativa para os cidadãos e empresas portuguesas, com o objetivo de impulsionar a construção, a reabilitação e a oferta de arrendamento a preços mais acessíveis, através de um pacote abrangente de incentivos e alterações fiscais.

Revised EU Foreign Investment Screening Framework: This new regulation requires all EU member states to maintain foreign investment screening regimes, expanding their common minimum scope to include sensitive sectors such as military equipment, dual-use items, critical technologies, and infrastructure. Previously, member states had varied or no screening mechanisms; now, the framework reinforces cooperation between national authorities and the European Commission in reviewing foreign direct investments. The regulation will enter into force 20 days after its publication in the Official Journal of the EU, with the new rules applying 18 months thereafter. This will impact investors and businesses, including Portuguese entities, involved in cross-border mergers, acquisitions, or expansions within sensitive EU sectors, necessitating enhanced due diligence and potentially longer approval processes.

Regulation on the Return of Illegally Staying Third Country Nationals: This regulation establishes a common European system for the return of non-EU nationals residing illegally in a Member State. Previously, return procedures were fragmented across national jurisdictions; now, the new rules streamline processes, increase possibilities for pre-removal detention (up to 24 months based on individual assessment for absconding or security risks), and mandate cooperation from non-EU nationals subject to a return decision. It also allows for the establishment of "return hubs" in third countries under strict human rights safeguards. The regulation was approved by the European Parliament on June 17, 2026, and will enter into force the day after its publication in the Official Journal of the EU, with some articles becoming applicable after a 12-month implementation period. This will affect Member States' migration management and potentially labor markets and social services in countries like Portugal, as national authorities implement the new, standardized return procedures.

EU-U.S. Tariff Deal: This legislative package, given final approval by MEPs on June 16, 2026, implements EU tariff commitments that eliminate tariffs on all US industrial goods and grant preferential market access for a wide range of US seafood and agricultural goods. Previously, these goods were subject to varying tariff rates. The agreement includes a sunset clause, meaning these preferences will expire on December 31, 2029, unless renewed, and allows the Commission to suspend EU tariff preferences if the US maintains tariffs above 15% on EU steel and aluminium derivatives beyond December 31, 2026. Businesses, including Portuguese exporters and importers involved in transatlantic trade of industrial goods, seafood, and agricultural products, will benefit from reduced costs and increased market access, while the safeguard mechanism offers protection for EU steel and aluminium industries.

EU Customs Reform (Elimination of €150 Duty-Free Threshold): This regulatory overhaul, set to take effect on July 1, 2026, eliminates the customs duty-free treatment for low-value imports (shipments valued at €150 or less) into the EU. Previously, such imports were exempt from customs duties.
