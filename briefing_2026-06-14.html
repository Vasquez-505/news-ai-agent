🇪🇺 EU Policy

Pay Transparency Directive (Directive (EU) 2023/970): This Directive, for which Member States were required to transpose national laws by June 7, 2026, mandates that employers must proactively disclose salary ranges to job applicants and are prohibited from inquiring about a candidate's pay history. Previously, pay transparency varied significantly across Member States; now, employees have a right to request information on average pay levels, broken down by gender, for colleagues performing the same or equivalent work, and companies with more than 250 employees are now obligated to report on gender pay gaps. This directly impacts businesses in Portugal, requiring them to adapt recruitment practices and potentially remuneration structures to comply with the new transparency and reporting standards.

New Pact on Migration and Asylum: Entering into effect on June 12, 2026, this pact overhauls the EU's approach to managing irregular migration. Previously, the system was characterized by fragmented national procedures, often placing a disproportionate burden on frontline Member States. Now, it establishes common procedures, including screening of foreigners at EU borders for up to seven days and faster asylum procedures (three months instead of six, down from six months or more) for those deemed a security risk or from "safe" countries. Furthermore, it introduces a "mandatory solidarity mechanism," where all EU countries must either physically host a share of asylum seekers (up to 30,000 per year) or provide financial or personnel support. This will significantly alter how Portugal, as an EU border nation and recipient of migratory flows, manages its borders and processes asylum claims, potentially requiring adjustments to national infrastructure and resources.

Regulation establishing a new framework to protect the EU steel market from global overcapacity: Formally adopted by the Council on June 8, 2026, and set to apply from July 1, 2026, this regulation replaces the previous EU steel safeguard measure which expires on June 30, 2026. Previously, the safeguard measure offered a general level of protection; now, the new framework introduces a revised tariff-rate quota (TRQ) system that will reduce import quotas and impose higher duties on imports exceeding these quotas. It also includes a "melt and pour" requirement to enhance transparency by identifying the steel's country of origin. This directly affects Portuguese steel producers and any businesses in Portugal that import steel, as they will face revised import tariffs and quotas, aimed at fostering fairer competition within the EU market.

Why it matters: The entry into force of the New Pact on Migration and Asylum represents the most significant EU-level change this week, fundamentally altering how the Union collectively manages migration and asylum across all Member States.
