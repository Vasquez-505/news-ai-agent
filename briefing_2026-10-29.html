🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Redução das Taxas de IRS (Lei n.º 64-B/2026): Esta lei, publicada a 22 de outubro de 2026 e em vigor a partir de 23 de outubro de 2026, autoriza o Governo a reduzir as taxas gerais de Imposto sobre o Rendimento das Pessoas Singulares (IRS) aplicáveis aos primeiros seis escalões de rendimento coletável. As novas taxas são fixadas em 12,2%, 15,2%, 20,7%, 23,6%, 30,6% e 34,6% respetivamente. A medida visa aliviar a carga fiscal sobre os rendimentos da maioria dos contribuintes portugueses.

Redução de 50% na Retenção na Fonte de IRS em Novembro (Despacho de 14 de outubro de 2026): Um despacho publicado a 14 de outubro de 2026 estabelece uma redução excecional de 50% na retenção na fonte de IRS para trabalhadores por conta de outrem e pensionistas, a aplicar nos rendimentos pagos em novembro de 2026, incluindo o subsídio de Natal se processado nesse mês. Esta medida temporária procura refletir de forma retroativa as descidas das taxas de IRS aprovadas para 2026, aumentando o rendimento líquido disponível no mês de novembro.

Novas Regras para Desbloquear Heranças Indivisas (Decreto-Lei aprovado a 22 de outubro de 2026): O Governo aprovou um decreto-lei que visa agilizar o processo de venda de imóveis integrados em heranças indivisas. Anteriormente, um único herdeiro podia bloquear a venda de um imóvel herdado; agora, um único herdeiro pode exigir a venda ao fim de dois anos sem acordo. A medida pretende colocar mais imóveis no mercado, contribuindo para aumentar a oferta habitacional e desbloquear património.

Valorização das Carreiras de Conservador e Oficial de Registos (Decreto-Lei n.º 206/2026): Este diploma, publicado a 27 de outubro de 2026 e em vigor a partir de 28 de outubro de 2026, aprova medidas de valorização para os trabalhadores das carreiras especiais de conservador de registos e de oficial de registos. O decreto-lei procede à revisão das carreiras existentes (conservador, notário, ajudante e escriturário dos registos e notariado) e altera o respetivo regime remuneratório. A medida visa reconhecer e melhorar as condições dos profissionais que asseguram o funcionamento dos serviços de registo e notariado.

Why it matters: As medidas aprovadas esta semana, em particular a autorização para a redução das taxas de IRS e a redução temporária da retenção na fonte, terão um impacto direto e positivo no rendimento disponível de grande parte dos cidadãos portugueses, enquanto as novas regras para heranças indivisas procuram dinamizar o mercado imobiliário.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Here is the EUROPEAN UNION POLICY section of this morning's briefing:

"Omnibus V" Defense Simplification Package (Regulations and Directive): This package, formally approved by the Council of the EU on October 9, 2026, introduces significant changes to defense procurement, permitting, and intra-EU transfers. Previously, defense procurement faced lower thresholds and more cumbersome national permitting and transfer processes. Now, it sets higher thresholds for defense contracts (up to EUR 2 million for supply/service, EUR 10 million for works) under the Defence and Security Procurement Directive, establishes a new EU Permit-Granting Regulation for faster permitting of "defense readiness projects" (requiring Member States to designate a Single Point of Contact), and introduces General Transfer Licences for moving defense equipment between certified suppliers within the EU. The Regulations will take effect in November 2026 with direct EU-wide application, while the Directive requires national transposition. This aims to reduce administrative burdens and stimulate investment in the EU's defense industrial base, particularly relevant for Portuguese businesses in the defense and related manufacturing sectors.

New EU Rules on Return for Third-Country Nationals: The European Council gave its final approval on October 28, 2026, to new rules designed to make returns of individuals with no legal right to stay in the EU more effective. Previously, roughly two out of three individuals ordered to leave the EU did not depart. The new framework introduces a European Return Order to facilitate mutual recognition of return decisions among Member States (initially voluntary), allows for stricter measures such as indefinite entry bans and longer detention periods (beyond 24 months) for non-EU nationals posing a security risk, and enables Member States to establish "return hubs" in non-EU countries, provided agreements respect international human rights standards. These rules will come into force in the coming weeks and aim to significantly enhance Member States' ability to manage migration flows more effectively.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2403 on Comprehensive Guarantees for Authorised Economic Operators: This Regulation, published and entering into force on October 28, 2026, amends Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447. Previously, Authorised Economic Operators (AEOs) faced certain comprehensive guarantee requirements for their customs operations, including those related to distance sales. The new rule significantly reduces the comprehensive guarantees required for AEOs specifically in the context of distance sales and adjusts certain data elements for the Union handling fee. This change is intended to ease financial burdens and streamline customs procedures for AEOs, particularly benefiting Portuguese e-commerce businesses engaged in international distance selling.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2390 on Imports of Acetylsalicylic Acid from China: Published and entering into force on October 28, 2026, this Regulation requires that imports of acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) originating in the People's Republic of China be made subject to registration. Previously, such imports were not under a specific registration regime for potential anti-dumping measures. The new rule mandates this registration to allow for the potential future levy of anti-dumping duties retroactively on these imports. This introduces increased scrutiny and potential financial implications for EU businesses importing this chemical from China.

Why it matters: The formal approval of the "Omnibus V" defense simplification package this week represents a strategic shift towards enhancing the EU's defense industrial capabilities and cross-border cooperation, directly impacting a key sector for investors.

🇺🇸 US Policy

Presidential Proclamation on Robotics and Industrial Machinery Imports: President Donald J. Trump signed a Proclamation under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 to address the threatened impairment of national security posed by imports of robotics, industrial machinery, and their parts and components. The Proclamation directs the U.S. Secretary of Commerce and U.S. Trade Representative to jointly negotiate agreements with trading partners to resolve this threat and reserves the President's authority to take further actions, such as imposing tariffs. Previously, specific actions under Section 232 primarily targeted other sectors like steel and aluminum; now, this expands the focus to advanced manufacturing components. This action signals a continued emphasis on protecting and strengthening domestic manufacturing critical for national security.

Treasury Department Sanctions Rwandan Ministry of Defence and North Korea-linked IT Schemes: The U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) imposed sanctions on the Rwandan Ministry of Defence and Zigama Credit and Savings Society for materially assisting the M23 armed group in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). Additionally, OFAC sanctioned 10 individuals and one entity, comprising Russian and DPRK nationals, for their involvement in North Korean government-run IT worker schemes that defraud U.S. businesses and generate revenue for the DPRK's weapons programs. As a result, all property and interests in property of these designated persons and entities subject to U.S. jurisdiction are blocked, and U.S. persons are generally prohibited from transactions with them. These actions aim to hold accountable those prolonging conflict in the DRC and to disrupt illicit funding for North Korea's WMD programs, building on previous sanctions against similar networks.

DHS Adds Chinese Genomics Companies to Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act Entity List: The Department of Homeland Security (DHS), on behalf of the Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force (FLETF), added three affiliated Chinese genomics companies to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) Entity List. Effective October 29, 2026, goods produced by these companies are now presumed to be prohibited from entering the United States unless importers can demonstrate, under the law's requirements, that the goods were not made with forced labor. This action expands the list of entities subject to import restrictions under the UFLPA, reinforcing efforts to combat forced labor in U.S. supply chains and protect American workers from unfair competition.

Federal Reserve Finalizes Stress Test Framework Changes: The Federal Reserve Board finalized two rules that modify its annual supervisory stress test framework. These changes are designed to enhance transparency, increase public accountability, and reduce the year-over-year volatility in stress test-related capital requirements by approximately 50%, without significantly altering aggregate capital levels for banks. Previously, the stress test framework had different levels of transparency and higher volatility in capital requirements. The new rules, effective immediately, allow for annual public input on stress test scenarios and material model changes and update the framework for hypothetical scenario design. This aims to provide greater clarity and stability in regulatory capital planning for large financial institutions.

CFTC Sues Utah to Defend Federal Oversight of Prediction Markets: The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) filed a federal lawsuit against the State of Utah and Attorney General Derek Brown in the U.S. District Court for the District of Utah. The lawsuit seeks to prevent Utah from using its state laws to restrict or close prediction markets that are registered with and supervised by the CFTC. This action directly responds to Utah's recent decision to pursue civil enforcement actions in state court against CFTC-regulated contract markets and businesses, and asserts the CFTC's exclusive federal jurisdiction over commodity derivatives, including event contracts. This move by the CFTC aims to maintain a unified federal regulatory framework for prediction markets, challenging state-level attempts to regulate federally supervised financial products.

Why it matters: This week's U.S. policy actions demonstrate a robust federal engagement across economic and national security fronts, including new trade measures to protect domestic industries, targeted sanctions to counter foreign destabilization and illicit finance, and significant regulatory enhancements aimed at ensuring market integrity and federal jurisdiction over emerging financial products.
