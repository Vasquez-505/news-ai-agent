🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Regulamento de Atribuição de Bolsas de Estudo a Estudantes do Ensino Superior (Portaria n.º 331-B/2026/1, de 10 de agosto): Aprova um novo regime de atribuição de bolsas de estudo no ensino superior, substituindo o modelo anterior por um sistema que adequa o valor das bolsas aos custos reais de frequência e reforça a progressividade dos apoios. Anteriormente, o modelo não refletia adequadamente os custos reais e tinha progressividade limitada; agora, a bolsa será equivalente ao custo adicional de frequentar o ensino superior, deduzida a capacidade contributiva familiar, para garantir maior justiça social e eficácia. Entrou em vigor no primeiro dia útil após a sua publicação (11 de agosto de 2026), aplicando-se a partir do ano letivo de 2026-2027. O impacto prático é que os estudantes de famílias com menores rendimentos receberão um apoio financeiro mais ajustado aos seus custos reais de estudo.

Regime Jurídico dos Estudantes com Necessidades Educativas Específicas no Ensino Superior (Lei n.º 44/2026, de 10 de agosto): Estabelece um regime jurídico específico para estudantes com necessidades educativas especiais no ensino superior, visando a não discriminação e a igualdade de oportunidades. Anteriormente, não existia um quadro legal tão abrangente e específico; agora, estes estudantes beneficiam de prioridade na inscrição, escolha de turmas e horários, podem optar por regimes de tempo parcial ou híbrido, e as instituições de ensino superior são obrigadas a aprovar planos anuais de melhoria das condições de acessibilidade com orçamentos e prazos definidos. A lei entrou em vigor no primeiro dia útil após a sua publicação (11 de agosto de 2026). O impacto prático é uma maior inclusão e apoio académico para estudantes com deficiência ou condições de saúde específicas, tornando o ensino superior mais acessível.

Regime de Reafectação de Financiamento PRR para Portugal 2030 (Decreto-Lei n.º 165/2026, de 13 de agosto): Altera os decretos-leis n.º 5/2023 e n.º 20-A/2023, criando um regime específico no Portugal 2030 para operações de entidades públicas e do setor social com financiamento do Plano de Recuperação e Resiliência (PRR) revogado ou cessado. Anteriormente, estas operações poderiam perder o apoio e ser interrompidas; agora, podem ser integradas no Portugal 2030 sob certas condições (ex: execução comprovada, pelo menos um pagamento PRR efetuado). 🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on Packaging and Packaging Waste (PPWR): This regulation introduces harmonized sustainability and labelling requirements for packaging across the EU. Previously, Member States had more flexibility under the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (94/62/EC), leading to varied national approaches. As of August 12, 2026, companies placing packaged goods on the EU market must comply with new substance restrictions, including a cap of 100 mg/kg for the combined sum of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium in packaging materials, and a near-total ban on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging (no more than 25 ppb for individual PFAS, or 250 ppb for the sum of targeted PFAS). Furthermore, companies must provide a mandatory EU Declaration of Conformity and register in national packaging registers where packaging is placed on the market. This directly impacts Portuguese manufacturers, importers, and e-commerce businesses by requiring significant changes to packaging design, material sourcing, and compliance documentation. [Source: EUR-Lex, European Commission]

EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) - Article 50 Transparency Obligations: These obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems entered into force on August 2, 2026, and are applicable this week. Previously, there were no specific EU-wide transparency obligations for AI systems. Now, providers of AI systems that directly interact with individuals (e.g., chatbots) must disclose that users are engaging with AI, unless this is already obvious. Providers of AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic audio, image, video, or text must embed machine-readable markings and provide a detection mechanism, with a compliance deadline of December 2, 2026, for systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, and immediate compliance for new systems. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals, and those using deepfakes or AI-generated text on public-interest matters must disclose the artificial nature of the content unless it has undergone substantial human editorial review. Non-compliance can result in fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. This affects Portuguese tech companies and businesses deploying AI systems by imposing immediate transparency requirements to enhance user awareness and trust. [Source: Cooley, CertScope, Morgan Lewis]

This new act specifically details the *procedure* to conduct the listing of high-risk plants, plant products, or other objects. [Source: EUR-Lex]

Decision (EU) 2026/1942 of the European Central Bank (ECB/2026/18): Published on August 14, 2026, this Decision establishes specific rules for the processing of personal data within the context of the prudential supervision of credit institutions by the European Central Bank. Executive Order 14203 - Sanctions Against International Criminal Court Officials: President Donald Trump issued Executive Order 14203 on August 16, 2026, imposing sanctions on officials, judges, and other individuals and entities associated with the International Criminal Court (ICC). These sanctions include the blocking of property and assets and the suspension of entry into the United States for those responsible for the ICC's investigations involving the U.S. and its allies, such as Israel. Previously, such broad sanctions against ICC personnel for these activities were not in effect; now, individuals involved face tangible and significant consequences, including potential asset freezes, U.S. entry bans, and criminal penalties of up to $1 million in fines and 20 years of imprisonment for willful violations under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). This action signals a strong U.S. stance against perceived ICC overreach into U.S. and allied sovereign matters.

U.S. Court of International Trade Upholds Removal of De Minimis Tariff Exemption: On August 15, 2026, the U.S. Court of International Trade issued a summary judgment in favor of the Trump administration, affirming the presidential suspension of the "de minimus exemption" for imports valued under $800. This ruling eliminates a loophole that previously allowed individual importers to avoid paying duties for goods under $800, a practice expanded by Congress in 2015 from a $200 threshold. The court held that the President's action, invoked under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), was a permissible exercise of executive authority. This action means that a broader range of lower-value imports will now be subject to tariffs.

Tariffs Imposed on Drones and Components: On August 16, 2026, President Donald Trump issued a proclamation to implement a "100% ad valorem tariff" on imports of certain drones and their components. This new tariff specifically targets drones of a particular size or with capabilities deemed sensitive for national security purposes, including their docking stations and critical components. The tariffs are set to take effect on September 18, 2026, with smaller drones and those from certain allied countries receiving lesser tariffs. This move reflects a focus on strengthening national security and the domestic industrial base by increasing the cost of foreign-manufactured drones.

FinCEN Narrows Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting Requirements: A final rule issued by the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) became effective on August 14, 2026, modifying beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting requirements under the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA). The rule exempts U.S. person beneficial owners from having to report their BOI and exempts reporting companies from submitting information about their U.S. person company applicants to FinCEN. Additionally, all U.S. persons are now exempt from updating information already provided to FinCEN in connection with obtaining a FinCEN identifier. Previously, reporting companies were required to report identifying information about themselves, their beneficial owners, and, for companies created on or after January 1, 2024, their company applicants; now, the scope of required reporting for U.S. persons has been significantly reduced.

FTC Shifts Stance on Disparate Impact and "Unfair Discrimination" Claims: The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) announced a new policy statement on August 7, 2026, and published on August 14, 2026, clarifying that it will no longer pursue claims based on disparate-impact or "unfair discrimination" theories under the FTC Act or the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA). This policy repudiates the FTC's previous position that Section 5 of the FTC Act could independently authorize the Commission to pursue discrimination claims based on "unfairness." Previously, the FTC had asserted claims of "unfair discrimination" and disparate impact in its enforcement actions. The new policy signals a move away from outcome-based discrimination theories toward a focus on intentional discrimination.
