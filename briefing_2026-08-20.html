🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40): This regulation, applicable in substance from August 12, 2026, replaces the previous Packaging Directive (94/62/EC), which allowed Member States discretion in implementation, leading to varied national rules. The new regulation establishes direct, harmonised obligations across the EU for companies placing packaged goods on the market. Specifically, it bans the intentional use of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging above 25 ppb for individual PFAS or 250 ppb for the sum of targeted PFAS. Businesses must also provide a mandatory EU Declaration of Conformity for their packaging and register as producers in the national packaging registers of every Member State where they place packaged goods on the market. While formally applicable, the European Commission has advised Member States against immediate strict enforcement of certain provisions, such as the cross-border registration requirement, to allow operators a reasonable period for compliance without penalties. Member States have until February 12, 2027, to establish their specific enforcement rules. This directly impacts manufacturers, importers, and distributors, including those in Portugal, by standardising sustainability requirements for packaging and introducing new administrative duties, particularly for businesses engaged in cross-border trade.

End-of-Life Vehicles (ELV) Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2026/1738): This regulation entered into force on August 13, 2026, replacing the earlier ELV Directive (2000/53/EC) and the 3R type-approval Directive (2005/64/EC). Previously, there were no binding EU-wide recycled content quotas for plastics in new vehicles. The new law introduces a requirement that newly type-approved vehicles must incorporate at least 15% recycled plastic by weight starting September 1, 2032, increasing to 25% by September 1, 2036. A "closed-loop" rule mandates that at least 20% of this recycled plastic must originate from scrapped vehicles. Additionally, manufacturers must provide detailed instructions for removing and replacing vehicle components and issue a "digital circularity vehicle passport" for each vehicle, detailing its material composition. The Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) framework expands to the automotive sector starting September 2029, obliging carmakers to fund the collection and treatment of end-of-life vehicles. This represents a significant shift for the automotive industry, including any manufacturers or parts suppliers in Portugal, by mandating circular economy practices and increasing producer responsibility throughout a vehicle's lifecycle.

EU AI Act Transparency Obligations (Article 50 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689): These specific transparency provisions became applicable from August 2, 2026. Prior to this, no explicit, harmonised EU-wide legal framework for AI transparency existed beyond general data protection principles. Now, providers of AI systems that directly interact with individuals, such as chatbots or voice assistants, must clearly disclose that users are engaging with an AI system, unless the context makes this obvious. For providers of AI systems that generate or manipulate synthetic audio, image, video, or text, there is an obligation to embed machine-readable markings to identify the content as artificially generated or manipulated, along with providing a detection mechanism. A transitional period for these marking and detection obligations applies until December 2, 2026, for generative AI systems already on the market before August 2, 2026. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals that such processing is taking place. Non-compliance can result in administrative fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. This is highly relevant for tech companies and online service providers in Portugal and across the EU that develop or deploy AI, necessitating immediate review and adjustment of their systems to meet these disclosure and marking requirements.

EU e-Evidence Regulation: This regulation came into effect on August 18, 2026, to streamline the process for law enforcement authorities to obtain electronic evidence across the European Union. Previously, obtaining electronic evidence from service providers located in other Member States often relied on fragmented and lengthy national legal assistance procedures. The new regulation introduces a European Production Order, which allows judicial or law enforcement authorities to directly request data from service providers in other EU Member States. Organisations offering certain online services in an EU Member State must now register an 'addressee' to receive and promptly respond to these requests for e-Evidence. The deadline for service providers to register an addressee was August 18, 2026, or within six months of their establishment if later. This creates new binding legal obligations for online service providers, including those operating in Portugal, requiring them to establish a designated contact point and processes for handling cross-border electronic evidence requests.

Why it matters: This week saw the entry into force of several critical EU regulations, fundamentally reshaping obligations for businesses in the digital, automotive, and consumer goods sectors, aiming to enhance transparency, sustainability, and cross-border legal cooperation.
