🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Interpretação Autêntica da Taxa Reduzida de IVA para Reabilitação Urbana: A Lei n.º 48/2026, de 17 de agosto, clarifica que as empreitadas de reabilitação urbana beneficiam da taxa reduzida de IVA (6%, em vez da taxa normal de 23%) quando realizadas em imóveis ou espaços públicos localizados em áreas de reabilitação urbana legalmente delimitadas, independentemente da aprovação de uma operação de reabilitação urbana específica. Anteriormente, existia ambiguidade sobre a necessidade de uma operação de reabilitação urbana formalmente aprovada para a aplicação da taxa reduzida. Esta medida visa simplificar os processos e incentivar a regeneração urbana.

Alteração Excecional do Prazo de Pagamento de Contribuições da Segurança Social: A Segurança Social decidiu alargar excecionalmente o prazo para o pagamento das contribuições mensais referentes a agosto de 2026 em cinco dias. Anteriormente, o pagamento deveria ser efetuado até ao dia 25 de agosto. Agora, pode ser realizado até 31 de agosto, inclusive. Esta alteração temporária visa facilitar o cumprimento das obrigações contributivas durante o período de férias.

Novo Regime de Autoconsumo de Energia: O Decreto-Lei n.º 130/2026, de 29 de junho, com entrada em vigor a 28 de agosto de 2026, aumenta o limite de potência isento de controlo prévio para 800 watts (anteriormente um limite inferior não especificado) para unidades de autoconsumo que não injetam excedentes na rede. Adicionalmente, introduz novas regras para o autoconsumo coletivo e para a proximidade entre a produção e o consumo de energia. O impacto prático é a simplificação dos procedimentos para cidadãos e condomínios que pretendam produzir e partilhar eletricidade renovável, especialmente para instalações de pequena escala.

Embora os medicamentos continuem a ser gratuitos, a nova regra promove uma maior utilização de genéricos e biossimilares. O objetivo é assegurar maior racionalidade e equidade na utilização dos recursos públicos, sem prejudicar o princípio da gratuitidade.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on Packaging and Packaging Waste (PPWR): Previously, packaging rules were primarily governed by Directive 94/62/EC, leading to varied national implementation. Now, as of August 12, 2026, companies placing packaged goods on the EU market must comply with a near-total ban on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging and ensure all packaging adheres to a strict combined limit of 100 mg/kg for heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury, hexavalent chromium). Manufacturers are required to complete conformity assessments and technical documentation for these new chemical safety and heavy metal limits. The regulation also sets binding targets for full recyclability of all packaging by 2030 and increasing recycled content in plastic packaging by 2030 and 2040, alongside bans on certain single-use packaging from 2030. This regulation imposes direct and harmonized obligations, significantly impacting product design, supply chain documentation, and market access for all businesses, including those in Portugal, operating within the EU.

EU AI Act - Article 50 Transparency Obligations: Previously, there were no harmonised EU-wide specific transparency obligations for Artificial Intelligence systems. Now, from August 2, 2026, providers of AI systems that directly interact with individuals (e.g., chatbots) must clearly disclose that users are interacting with AI, unless this fact is contextually obvious. Furthermore, providers of AI systems generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text content are required to embed machine-readable markings to identify it as AI-generated and provide technical detection mechanisms. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation systems must inform affected individuals, and those deploying deepfakes or AI-generated text on public-interest matters must disclose their artificial origin, unless the content has undergone substantial human editorial review. This creates new and immediate compliance requirements for tech companies and businesses leveraging AI within the EU, or whose AI outputs affect EU citizens, aiming to enhance user trust and combat misinformation. Non-compliance can result in fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover.

EU e-Evidence Regulation: Previously, obtaining cross-border electronic evidence for criminal investigations within the EU often involved lengthy judicial assistance procedures or voluntary cooperation between authorities and service providers. Now, as of August 18, 2026, organizations that offer specific online services in an EU Member State must formally register an 'addressee' to receive and promptly respond to legally binding requests for electronic evidence directly from competent authorities. This streamlines the process for law enforcement to acquire data across EU borders, but imposes a new mandatory administrative and legal compliance burden on online service providers, including those based in or serving Portugal, to establish the designated contact point and appropriate internal procedures.

European Commission Communication C/2026/4514 on Budgetary Flexibility for Energy Security: Previously, the flexibility under the national escape clause for budgetary purposes was predominantly reserved for defence spending. Now, as of August 21, 2026, the European Commission has formally adopted guidance that allows Member States to extend this budgetary flexibility to finance measures aimed at strengthening energy security and accelerating the transition away from fossil fuels during the 2026–2028 period. Eligible investments include critical areas such as renewables, batteries and energy storage, heat pumps, electric vehicle charging infrastructure, and building renovations, provided they meet strict criteria for additionality, effectiveness, and the sustainability of public finances. This offers Member States, including Portugal, a clearer framework and potential national funding avenues to accelerate green investments, bolster energy independence, and mitigate energy price volatility, directly benefiting national energy strategies and related industries.

Why it matters: This week marks a significant shift in EU regulatory enforcement, with new and clarified obligations coming into play for both environmental sustainability in product manufacturing and transparency in digital services, particularly concerning the widespread application of AI.
