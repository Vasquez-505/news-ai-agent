🇪🇺 EU Policy

Council Regulations on EU-US Tariff Commitments: These two new regulations, adopted by the Council on June 25, 2026, implement the EU's tariff commitments under the August 21, 2025, EU-US Joint Statement. The "Main Regulation" eliminates import duties on most US industrial goods, including chemicals, pharmaceuticals, plastics, textiles, metals, machinery, vehicles, and aircraft, and reduces duties on certain fresh produce, while also establishing 20 duty-free or reduced-duty import quotas for US agricultural and seafood products. The "Lobster Regulation" reinstates and expands the 0% import duty regime on US lobster imports, including prepared or preserved lobster. Previously, various US industrial and agricultural goods were subject to standard EU tariffs or retaliatory tariffs imposed in 2025 (e.g., 25% on steel and aluminum, 10% baseline, 25% on automobiles, 50% on copper). The Main Regulation enters into force the day after its publication in the Official Journal (expected in the coming weeks) and applies until December 31, 2029. The Lobster Regulation also enters into force upon publication (expected in the coming weeks) and applies retroactively from August 1, 2025, until July 31, 2030. This significantly lowers import costs for EU businesses sourcing from the US, fostering improved transatlantic trade for all EU member states, including Portugal.

Regulation (EU) 2026/1384 on EU Steel Market Protection: This Regulation, published in the Official Journal on June 24, 2026, and entering into force on June 25, 2026, applies from July 1, 2026. It replaces the existing EU steel safeguard measures, which expire on June 30, 2026. The new regime introduces a more comprehensive tariff-rate quota system, setting the total annual tariff quota volume at 18,345,922 tonnes, roughly half of previous levels, distributed quarterly across product categories. Previously, the out-of-quota duty was 25%; now, for steel imports exceeding these quotas, the duty is raised to 50% ad valorem. Furthermore, from October 1, 2026, importers must provide evidence of the country where the steel was originally melted and poured ("melt-and-pour" traceability requirement). This substantially tightens import management in the EU steel market, increasing costs for importers whose shipments fall outside the new quota limits and imposing new compliance burdens related to traceability for all businesses dealing with steel imports, including in Portugal.

Amendments to the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Omnibus VII package): The Council formally adopted new rules on June 29, 2026, to streamline and simplify certain provisions of the EU AI Act. Previously, key obligations for standalone high-risk AI systems were slated to apply from August 2, 2026, and for high-risk AI systems embedded in regulated products from August 2, 2027. These application dates are now delayed to December 2, 2027, for standalone high-risk AI systems (a 16-month extension) and August 2, 2028, for high-risk AI systems embedded in products (a 12-month extension). Additionally, the new law introduces a prohibition on AI practices generating non-consensual sexual and intimate content or child sexual abuse material, effective from December 2, 2026. The grace period for providers to implement transparency solutions for artificially generated content is shortened from six months to three months, with the new deadline set for December 2, 2026. These changes offer crucial additional time for businesses and tech companies across the EU, including those in Portugal, to prepare for complex AI compliance, while also reinforcing ethical safeguards against AI misuse.

Why it matters: The Council's final approval of the EU AI Act amendments provides significant relief and clarity for tech companies and investors by delaying key compliance deadlines for high-risk AI systems, demonstrating the EU's responsiveness to implementation challenges while upholding strong ethical standards.

SEC Expands Five-Business-Day Relief for Non-Convertible Debt Tender and Exchange Offers: The Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) Division of Corporation Finance issued an exemptive order, effective June 30, 2026, permitting issuer self-tender and exchange offers for non-convertible debt securities to remain open for an abbreviated five-business-day offering period. Previously, such offers were primarily guided by a January 23, 2015 no-action letter, typically requiring a 20-business-day minimum offering period and imposing conditions like a guaranteed delivery mechanism and a prohibition on financing with "Senior Indebtedness," while restricting partial offers and concurrent consent solicitations. Now, the new order permits partial tender and exchange offers (with specific announcement requirements for changes over 2%), allows offers in connection with consent solicitations for simple majority amendments, eliminates the guaranteed delivery requirement and the prohibition on "Senior Indebtedness" financing, and removes the Form 8-K filing requirement for launch press releases or consideration changes. This order provides significant flexibility and efficiency for companies undertaking liability management transactions, potentially accelerating debt restructuring and reducing market risk exposure.

Department of Education Final Rule on Federal Student Loan Programs: The Department of Education issued a final rule, effective July 1, 2026, amending regulations for Federal student loan programs to implement statutory changes from Public Law 119-21, the "Working Families Tax Cuts Act" (signed July 4, 2025). Previously, the programs operated under existing loan limits, the Graduate PLUS (Grad PLUS) Program, and multiple Income-Contingent Repayment (ICR) plans, and borrowers in default who had previously rehabilitated a defaulted loan could not do so again. Now, the rule establishes new loan limits for graduate students, professional students, and parents, phases out the Grad PLUS Program, and simplifies repayment plans by phasing out existing ICR plans, creating a new Tiered Standard repayment plan, and establishing a new income-driven "Repayment Assistance Plan." This overhaul significantly restructures federal student loan availability and repayment options, impacting future and current borrowers' financial planning and the overall student loan market.

Supreme Court Rules on FTC Commissioner Removal and Federal Reserve Independence: On June 29, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court, in *Trump v. Slaughter*, held that the President may fire Federal Trade Commission (FTC) commissioners at will, overruling the 1935 decision *Humphrey's Executor v. United States*. In a companion decision, *Trump v. Cook*, the Court upheld for-cause removal protections for Federal Reserve governors. Previously, FTC commissioners could only be removed for "inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance in office." Now, FTC commissioners are subject to at-will removal by the President, allowing for direct alignment with presidential policy, while the Federal Reserve's "distinct historical tradition of central bank independence" was reinforced, retaining for-cause removal protections for its governors. This ruling significantly alters the independence of the FTC from presidential influence while upholding the independence of the Federal Reserve.
