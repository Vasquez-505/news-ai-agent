Apoios Extraordinários a Setores Afetados pelos Custos dos Combustíveis e Nova Regra de Transparência do ISP: Um novo decreto-lei estabelece apoios financeiros extraordinários e temporários para mitigar o aumento dos custos de exploração associados aos combustíveis. Operadores de transporte rodoviário de mercadorias por conta de outrem, transporte público rodoviário de passageiros, empresas de táxis, entidades do setor social, associações humanitárias de bombeiros, cooperativas agrícolas, organizações de produtores e empresas de máquinas industriais de terraplanagem e indústria extrativa (com peso operacional superior a 9 toneladas) são elegíveis para um apoio único. Anteriormente, não havia um regime de apoio extraordinário com estas características e abrangência específica. Agora, o valor do apoio varia por setor e tipo de veículo; por exemplo, veículos de transporte de mercadorias com peso bruto igual ou superior a 35 toneladas recebem até 457,80€ por veículo (420€ para combustível e 37,80€ para AdBlue), enquanto entidades do setor social recebem um montante fixo de 600€ por beneficiário. Adicionalmente, todos os postos de abastecimento de combustíveis são agora obrigados a afixar, de forma visível, a diferença entre o Imposto sobre os Produtos Petrolíferos e Energéticos (ISP) que seria cobrado à taxa de referência e o imposto efetivamente cobrado à taxa em vigor, acrescido de IVA. Anteriormente, esta informação apenas tinha de constar das faturas emitidas desde outubro de 2022. Esta nova obrigação entra em vigor a 17 de outubro de 2026. O impacto prático é a redução de custos operacionais para as empresas e entidades abrangidas, e uma maior transparência para os consumidores no preço dos combustíveis.

Esta medida garante que as operações aeroportuárias continuem sem interrupções nos principais aeroportos do país.

Novas Regras para Licenciamento Urbanístico e Edificação (Em vigor desde 1 de outubro): Um decreto-lei que revê o regime aplicável ao licenciamento de operações urbanísticas e altera o Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação (RJUE) e o Regime Jurídico da Reabilitação Urbana, entrou em vigor no dia 1 de outubro de 2026. O impacto prático é a simplificação e aceleração dos processos de licenciamento para construção e reabilitação, beneficiando o setor imobiliário e os cidadãos.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

New Rules on Return for Third-Country Nationals: Previously, around two-thirds of individuals ordered to leave the EU did not actually depart. Now, non-EU nationals who lack a legal right to stay in the EU are obliged to leave the Member State concerned and cooperate with authorities. Failure to comply can result in sanctions, including reduced benefits, refusal of voluntary return incentives, financial penalties, or criminal sanctions where national law allows. Member States gain the ability to establish "return hubs" in non-EU countries through agreements, although unaccompanied minors are excluded from such arrangements. Stricter measures are introduced for those posing security risks, including the possibility of indefinite entry bans and detention periods exceeding 24 months. A "European Return Order" is also established to facilitate the mutual recognition of return decisions among Member States, initially on a voluntary basis, with a review in three years for potential mandatory application. These rules will be published in the Official Journal in the coming weeks and enter into force the day after publication, with some provisions, such as those on return hubs, applying immediately, while others will apply one year after entry into force. This regulation significantly strengthens the EU's migration management framework, directly impacting third-country nationals without legal status and the operational capabilities of Member States, including Portugal, in managing returns.

Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 on Air Passenger Rights: Previously, airlines had more discretion over cabin baggage charges and less stringent requirements for transparency in baggage pricing and passenger information. Now, every air passenger is guaranteed the right to carry one small personal item (measuring no more than 40 × 30 × 15 cm) in the cabin free of charge. Airlines must provide clearer baggage pricing information and are prohibited from mandating the use of mobile applications for accessing boarding passes. Passenger protections are significantly strengthened for flight delays (with compensation for delays exceeding three hours), cancellations, missed connections, and overbooking. Passengers are also offered a refund option after a five-hour departure delay, and all refunds must be processed within seven calendar days. Furthermore, airlines cannot compel passengers to accept vouchers instead of monetary refunds. The regulation also introduces additional safeguards for families and passengers with disabilities or reduced mobility. This regulation was adopted on September 16, 2026, published in the Official Journal on October 2, 2026, and enters into force on October 22, 2026; however, most of its provisions will apply from October 23, 2027. This directly benefits EU air travelers, including Portuguese citizens, by establishing clearer, stronger, and more consistent rights and obligations across the airline industry.

Targeted Reform of the EU Economic Governance Framework: Previously, the EU's economic governance framework imposed a higher administrative burden on Member States due to duplicate reporting requirements, and sanctions for non-compliance outside the Stability and Growth Pact's 'corrective arm' were often severe but less automatic. Now, the European Parliament approved an agreement with the Council on October 6, 2026, for a targeted legislative revision that simplifies the EU economic governance framework, aligning it more closely with the 2024 reform of the Stability and Growth Pact. This reform reduces Member States' administrative burden by eliminating redundant reporting requirements. Additionally, sanctions for non-compliance outside the Pact's 'corrective arm' will become graduated, less severe, but more automatic. This aims to create a more streamlined and effective system for overseeing national fiscal policies, impacting all EU Member States, including Portugal, by modifying compliance procedures and enforcement mechanisms.

New State Aid Rules for Sustainable Transport: Previously, the EU's State aid framework for land and multimodal transport was less explicitly structured to prioritize and facilitate investments in environmentally friendly transport solutions. Now, the European Commission adopted new State aid Land and Multimodal Transport Guidelines and a Transport Block Exemption Regulation on October 1, 2026. These legal instruments are designed to support and facilitate public investments in more sustainable transport modes for both passengers and freight, effectively updating and modernizing the EU's State aid framework in this sector. This regulatory update is set to encourage Member States and businesses, including those in Portugal, to accelerate investments in greener transport infrastructure and services by providing a clearer and more supportive legal framework for state aid.

Why it matters: This week marks a significant step towards a more unified and stringent EU approach to external border management and internal market regulation, particularly impacting migration and consumer protection in air travel.
