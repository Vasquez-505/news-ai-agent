🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Lei n.º 58/2026 – Proibição de Ocultar o Rosto em Espaços Públicos: Esta lei proíbe o uso de vestuário que oculte ou obstaculize a exibição do rosto em espaços públicos. Anteriormente, não existia uma lei nacional específica sobre o tema; agora, a violação desta proibição é punível com coimas entre 150€ e 750€ por negligência, ou 400€ e 3000€ em caso de dolo. A lei também criminaliza quem forçar a ocultação do rosto, com penas de prisão até 2 anos ou multa até 240 dias, agravadas para menores. Esta medida entra em vigor a 23 de setembro de 2026. Os cidadãos devem garantir que o rosto está visível em locais públicos, e empresas devem assegurar a conformidade nas suas instalações.

Lei n.º 59/2026 – Novo Regime Jurídico do TVDE: Altera o regime legal para o transporte remunerado de passageiros em veículos descaracterizados a partir de plataforma eletrónica (TVDE). Previamente, a Lei n.º 45/2018 regulava a atividade de TVDE, separada dos táxis, com dísticos amovíveis e sem formação mínima obrigatória de 50 horas. Agora, o novo "Regime TVDE" integra o setor dos táxis, permitindo que estes se registem para atividade TVDE; impõe uma formação inicial mínima de 50 horas (teórica e prática) com exame final para novos motoristas; e torna obrigatória a utilização de dísticos identificadores inamovíveis, visíveis do exterior e com elementos antifraude, associados ao registo do veículo. Esta lei entra em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026. Motoristas e operadores TVDE enfrentam requisitos mais rigorosos de formação e identificação veicular, enquanto o setor do táxi ganha a opção de operar no mercado TVDE, aumentando a potencial concorrência.

Decreto-Lei n.º 171/2026 – Revisão do Código dos Valores Mobiliários (CVM): Este decreto-lei introduz uma revisão profunda ao CVM para tornar os mercados de capitais nacionais mais atrativos. Anteriormente, o requisito mínimo de dispersão acionista (free float) para admissão de ações à negociação em mercado regulamentado era de 25%. Agora, esse requisito é reduzido para 10%, podendo ser ainda menor mediante avaliação casuística. O diploma também cria a figura de sistemas de negociação multilateral e segmentos de "PME em crescimento" com regras de admissão mais flexíveis. O limiar para o "squeeze-out" (aquisição potestativa) após uma oferta pública de aquisição geral aumenta para 90% do capital social. O limite de capitalização bolsista de 1.000 milhões de euros que condicionava a agregação de pagamentos entre serviços de execução e de pesquisa é eliminado. Este decreto-lei entra em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026. As empresas, em particular as PME, terão acesso facilitado ao mercado de capitais, e os investidores podem beneficiar de maior diversidade de listagens, com novas dinâmicas nas ofertas públicas e na remuneração de intermediários financeiros.

Portaria n.º 394/2026/1 – Normas de Execução da Prestação Social Única (PSU): Estabelece os procedimentos detalhados para a aplicação da Prestação Social Única (PSU), criada pelo Decreto-Lei n.º 166/2026, de 13 de agosto. Previamente, existia um sistema de prestações sociais mais fragmentado. Agora, esta Portaria define o processo de requerimento, os critérios de apuramento de recursos económicos (incluindo bens móveis sujeitos a registo), a elaboração de um plano de inserção por agregado familiar e as obrigações dos membros não empregados do agregado familiar em relação a atividades de solidariedade social. Os cidadãos em situação de vulnerabilidade acederão a apoios sociais através de um esquema unificado, mas estarão sujeitos a novas obrigações de integração e de atividades para manterem o direito à prestação.

Portaria n.º 395/2026/1 – Apoio Extraordinário a Setores Agrícolas e da Pesca: Fixa os procedimentos para a aplicação de um apoio financeiro extraordinário e temporário, de natureza não reembolsável, por veículo, destinado a mitigar os efeitos do aumento dos preços dos combustíveis e do AdBlue verificado em abril de 2026. Este apoio abrange cooperativas agrícolas, organizações de produtores, empresas de comercialização e transformação de pescado e empresas de produção de aquacultura que efetuem transporte por conta própria. Os pedidos de apoio são apresentados por formulário eletrónico no sítio do IFAP, I. P., com prazo a definir por anúncio. As empresas dos setores agrícola e das pescas que realizam transporte próprio receberão apoio financeiro para compensar os custos adicionais de combustível, contribuindo para a sua estabilidade económica.

Why it matters: A combinação da nova legislação sobre TVDE, que integra táxis e impõe novas regras para motoristas, com as alterações ao Código dos Valores Mobiliários, que facilitam o acesso das empresas à bolsa, representa as mudanças mais significativas para as empresas e cidadãos portugueses esta semana.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU AI Act - Transparency Obligations and Enforcement for General-Purpose AI: As of August 2, 2026, providers and deployers of AI systems must explicitly inform users when they are interacting with an AI, such as chatbots. This includes mandatory labelling for AI-generated or manipulated content, like deepfakes. Previously, many of these transparency and enforcement mechanisms for specific AI types were not fully active. The European Commission's AI Office and national authorities now possess full enforcement and sanctioning powers over general-purpose AI (GPAI) models. While the broad rollout of high-risk AI obligations originally set for this date has been largely postponed by the "Digital Omnibus" to December 2, 2027 (for stand-alone high-risk systems) and August 2, 2028 (for embedded high-risk AI), the immediate application of transparency rules and GPAI enforcement is critical. This significantly increases accountability for AI developers and users, particularly in ensuring public awareness of AI interaction and content authenticity. This is highly relevant for tech businesses and investors in the AI sector, including those in Portugal, affecting product design and compliance strategies.

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on Packaging and Packaging Waste (PPWR): This Regulation directly applies across all 27 EU Member States from August 12, 2026, replacing the previous Packaging Directive (94/62/EC). Previously, the Directive allowed Member States discretion, leading to fragmented national rules. Now, companies placing packaging on the EU market must comply with harmonized requirements, including ensuring all packaging is recyclable by 2030, meeting mandatory recycled content targets for plastic packaging (with increasing targets for 2030 and 2040), and strictly restricting harmful "forever chemicals" (PFAS) and heavy metals (e.g., a limit of 100 mg/kg for the sum total of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium). It also mandates clear labelling, reduction of unnecessary packaging and empty space, and the promotion of reuse/refill options. Importers from outside the EU must appoint an authorised representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State. This creates a unified EU market for packaging rules, fundamentally changing product design, manufacturing, and supply chain operations for businesses across all sectors, including Portuguese manufacturers and distributors, by imposing strict sustainability and recyclability obligations.

EU End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation (ELVR): This Regulation entered into force on August 13, 2026, establishing a new framework to strengthen circularity in the automotive sector, moving beyond previously varied national rules. It mandates tougher requirements for vehicle design to improve recyclability and sets specific targets for dismantling and recycling. The Regulation introduces digital vehicle passports and sets recycled-content targets for vehicle manufacturing. Furthermore, the export of used vehicles to non-EU countries is now conditional on roadworthiness, aiming to limit the outflow of older, more polluting cars. This significantly affects the automotive industry, from manufacturers to recyclers, by driving innovation in sustainable design and material use, and impacts the used car market both within and outside the EU.

Commission Decision No 1/2026 of the Committee on Investment Facilitation (EU-Angola Sustainable Investment Agreement): Adopted on March 3, 2026, and formally published in the Official Journal on August 26, 2026, this Decision adopts the Rules of Procedure for the Committee on Investment Facilitation established by the Sustainable Investment Facilitation Agreement between the European Union and the Republic of Angola. Previously, this committee operated without a formally established set of operational rules. Now, these rules govern the committee's functioning, providing a clear procedural framework for facilitating and overseeing investment cooperation. This formalises the operational framework for EU investments in Angola, offering clarity and a structured mechanism for addressing investment-related issues, which is relevant for European investors, including Portuguese ones, looking at opportunities in the Angolan market.

Why it matters: This week marks a significant shift as key provisions of the AI Act begin enforcement, alongside the broad application of new packaging and end-of-life vehicle regulations, pushing European industries towards greater sustainability, circularity, and transparency, while also formalising investment frameworks with international partners.

🇺🇸 US Policy

Trade and Economic Actions: The US government has enacted several significant trade and economic policy changes. President Trump's Proclamation of August 13, 2026, became effective, imposing Section 232 tariffs on imports of uncrewed aircraft systems (drones) and their parts and components. Previously, these specific tariffs on drones for national security reasons did not exist. Now, a 100% ad valorem tariff applies to drones over 25 kilograms or with thermal imaging capabilities and their critical components (effective September 3, 2026, and February 9, 2027, for components), a 25% tariff on smaller drones and their components, and reduced rates (10-15%) for imports from allied countries that meet certification requirements. The Proclamation also directs the Secretary of Commerce to establish an onshoring program for drone production. Additionally, a Proclamation signed on August 26, 2026, temporarily expands the quantity of lean beef trimmings subject to a lower in-quota tariff rate. Previously, the in-quota amount was lower. Now, it is temporarily increased by 300,000 metric tons for 90 days, beginning September 1, 2026, to ensure affordable ground beef for American consumers. Furthermore, an Executive Order signed on August 27, 2026, officially renamed Lake Ontario to "Lake America." Previously, the body of water was named Lake Ontario. Now, it is to be officially known as "Lake America," with federal government references mandated to reflect this change.

National Security and Sanctions Policy: The US has implemented broad actions in foreign policy and counter-terrorism. The Treasury Department launched "Operation Economic Outcast" on August 24, 2026, a comprehensive economic campaign against Iran, with actions widely reported and effective within the last 48 hours. Previously, Iran was subject to various sanctions. Now, this operation expands the sanctions architecture with five new sectoral determinations under Executive Order 13902 (targeting digital assets, technology, gold, aviation, and shipping sectors), designates nearly 60 additional entities, individuals, and vessels, and suspends Iran General Licences F and G while issuing General Licences AA and BB. Concurrently, on August 26, 2026, the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated UK-based Palestine Action, the Masar Badil network, and Italy-based Autistici Inventati as Specially Designated Global Terrorists (SDGTs) under Executive Order 13224, along with two leaders of Masar Badil. Previously, these groups and individuals were not subject to these specific US sanctions. Now, their property and interests in the US or under US person control are blocked, and transactions with them are generally prohibited. Separately, on August 24, 2026, the Secretary of State officially rescinded Syria's designation as a State Sponsor of Terrorism (SST) and revoked the designation of Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) as a Specially Designated Global Terrorist. Previously, Syria was an SST, and HTS was an SDGT, leading to various prohibitions and restrictions. Now, the SST designation is removed, ending prohibitions under the Terrorism List Governments Sanctions Regulations and 22 USC 7205(a)(1), and HTS is removed from the SDN List, facilitating private sector investment in Syria. The Secretary of State also waived prohibitions under the Syria Accountability Act and sanctions under the Chemical and Biological Weapons Control and Warfare Elimination Act of 1991.

Domestic Regulatory and Enforcement Actions: Significant domestic regulatory and enforcement actions have been taken. An Executive Order signed on August 26, 2026, declared a national emergency to secure the United States Bulk-Power System from foreign supply threats. Previously, no national emergency was declared for this specific threat. Now, it authorizes the Secretary of Energy to prohibit or condition the purchase and installation of foreign-produced bulk-power system electric equipment that poses an unacceptable national security risk. On August 27, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) finalized orders requiring Cox Media Group, MindSift, and 1010 Digital Works to pay a total of $930,000 to settle charges of deceiving customers. Previously, these companies allegedly falsely claimed to offer an AI-powered marketing service that targeted ads based on consumer conversations. Now, they must pay settlements and are prohibited from misrepresenting their advertising services, voice data collection, or geographic targeting capabilities. Lastly, on August 25, 2026, the US Department of Justice (DOJ) announced a $21.5 million settlement with Deloitte to resolve False Claims Act allegations. Previously, certain Deloitte diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) initiatives were alleged to have violated federal anti-discrimination requirements for government contractors. Now, Deloitte has agreed to this settlement without admitting liability, indicating continued DOJ scrutiny of DEI practices in federal contracting.

Why it matters: The US government has enacted comprehensive policy shifts across trade, national security, and domestic regulatory frameworks this week, including new tariffs on critical technologies, expanded international sanctions, and intensified enforcement of corporate compliance.
