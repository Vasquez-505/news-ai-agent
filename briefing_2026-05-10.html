EU Anti-Corruption Directive: Adopted in April 2026 and entering into force, this Directive requires Member States to criminalise corruption, introduces uniform definitions of public officials, and tightens sanctioning standards for companies. Legal entities can face fines of up to 5% of their total worldwide annual turnover or fixed maximum fines of up to €40 million for serious offenses. Member States must transpose the Directive into national law by 2028, significantly impacting corporate compliance systems across the EU, including Portuguese businesses.

Revised Package Travel Directive: This Directive formally entered into force on May 5, 2026, with full practical application from March 5, 2029. It aims to strengthen consumer protection in the travel sector, clarify definitions of package travel, and ensure a more balanced distribution of responsibility among all participants in the travel chain. Key changes include clearer rules for returning funds in cases of insolvency and removing strict restrictions on advance payments, which will affect travel agencies and tour operators throughout the EU, including Portugal.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/977: Published on May 4, 2026, this regulation lays down uniform quality management and procedural requirements for conformity assessment activities performed by notified bodies. It specifically applies to bodies designated under Regulations (EU) 2017/745 (medical devices) and (EU) 2017/746 (in vitro diagnostic medical devices). This ensures a consistent and high standard for the certification of medical devices entering the EU market, impacting manufacturers and conformity assessment bodies.
