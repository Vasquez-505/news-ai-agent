Oil & Gas: Crude oil prices surged this week, with WTI climbing to a five-week high, driven by renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Escalating conflict between the U.S. and Iran, including retaliatory strikes and a U.S. naval blockade against Iranian ports, has raised concerns about supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil flows. This geopolitical risk premium has outweighed an OPEC+ decision to nominally increase output by 188,000 barrels per day from August, which appears theoretical given current disruptions. This situation suggests continued volatility and potential upside for energy prices due to persistent supply concerns.

Equities — Apple: Apple significantly outperformed the broader tech sector, briefly unseating Nvidia as the world's most valuable company on July 17. This shift reflects a broader sector rotation as investors favor Apple's disciplined, low-capital-expenditure approach to AI integration, utilizing on-device processing, over the heavy infrastructure spending of AI hyperscalers like Nvidia. HSBC upgraded Apple to a "Buy" rating with a substantially raised price target of $366, citing its strong product pipeline and revamped Apple Intelligence integration. This indicates a change in investor sentiment towards more cash-generative and asset-light AI strategies.

Equities — Broader Tech/AI (Nvidia, Alphabet, Meta, Tesla): The broader tech sector, including Nvidia and several other Magnificent 7 stocks, experienced declines this week as investors rotated away from high-capital-expenditure AI hyperscalers. The market is showing signs of "AI-trade angst," moving out of companies heavily investing in AI infrastructure, and a general "risk-off" sentiment has impacted more speculative tech names. This suggests a re-evaluation of valuation models for companies with significant AI build-out costs.

Gold & Silver: Gold and silver prices continued their significant decline, reaching new 8-month lows. This downward pressure stems from stronger-than-expected U.S. economic data, which has reinforced expectations of a more hawkish stance from the Federal Reserve, including potential future interest rate hikes. Rising real yields and a stronger U.S. Dollar have increased the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding precious metals, pushing investors towards interest-bearing assets. This indicates a challenging environment for precious metals as long as real yields remain elevated and the dollar is strong.

Quantum Computing (QTUM ETF): The quantum computing sector experienced a significant sell-off this week, with pure-play stocks like IonQ, D-Wave, Rigetti, and Quantum Computing dropping sharply. Why it matters: Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East are directly driving energy prices higher, while simultaneously contributing to a risk-off sentiment that is rotating capital within the tech sector and pressuring non-yielding assets like gold and silver through hawkish interest rate expectations.

O diploma aguarda promulgação presidencial e publicação em Diário da República.

Proibição de Ocultação do Rosto em Espaços Públicos: A Assembleia da República aprovou uma lei que proíbe a utilização, em espaços públicos, de vestuário que oculte ou impeça a exibição do rosto. Anteriormente, não existia uma proibição nacional geral sobre o uso de tais vestimentas em espaços públicos. Esta medida visa a segurança e a identificação em locais públicos. O diploma aguarda promulgação presidencial e publicação em Diário da República.

Revisão das Taxas do Imposto sobre Produtos Petrolíferos e Energéticos (ISP): A Portaria n.º 301-A/2026/1 procede à revisão e fixação das taxas unitárias do imposto sobre os produtos petrolíferos e energéticos. Embora os valores exatos não sejam detalhados, esta revisão implica uma alteração nos custos de combustível e energia. Os cidadãos e empresas podem esperar ajustamentos nos preços na bomba.

Anteriormente, a instituição operava como Instituto Politécnico do Porto. Esta mudança visa reforçar e qualificar a rede pública de ensino superior no Norte do país. 🇪🇺 EU Policy

Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) – Ban on destruction of unsold apparel, clothing accessories and footwear: Large companies are now prohibited from destroying unsold apparel, clothing accessories, and footwear, effective July 19, 2026. Previously, an estimated 4-9% of unsold textiles were destroyed annually in Europe. Medium-sized companies will be subject to this ban from July 2030, and all companies must disclose information on unsold consumer products they discard. This regulation aims to foster a circular economy by promoting reuse and recycling, reducing environmental damage, and creating a level playing field for businesses across the EU.

EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) – Commission Implementing Measures: On July 13, 2026, the European Commission adopted implementing measures that update the product scope and refine the functioning of the EUDR Information System. The previous scope included certain products; now, cattle hides, skins, leather, re-treaded tyres, soybeans for sowing, articles of vulcanised rubber, conveyor and transmission belts, and aircraft and motor vehicle seats are *removed* from the Regulation's scope. Conversely, soluble coffee, certain palm oil derivatives, and frozen cattle tongues are *added* to the list of regulated products. New products added to the scope will become subject to the Regulation from December 30, 2027. This provides greater legal certainty and predictability for businesses and Member States, particularly impacting those involved in the trade and production of the listed commodities, including Portuguese businesses in these sectors.

EU AI Act – Digital Omnibus on AI (Application Deadline Deferrals): The Digital Omnibus on AI, which entered into force in July 2026, significantly defers application deadlines for key obligations under the EU AI Act. Previously, the general application date for standalone high-risk AI systems (listed in Annex III, covering areas like employment, education, critical infrastructure, and law enforcement) was August 2, 2026. These obligations are now deferred to December 2, 2027. For AI systems embedded in products already covered by EU product safety legislation (Annex I, such as medical devices or machinery), the deadline shifts further to August 2, 2028. Additionally, transparency obligations for providers to mark AI-generated content, for systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, now benefit from a three-month grace period. This provides businesses, including Portuguese tech and AI companies, a crucial extended period to ensure compliance with complex AI governance requirements.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1734 on the protection of animals at the time of killing: Adopted on July 16, 2026, this regulation amends Annex I to Council Regulation (EC) No 1099/2009 by formally authorizing the use of non-penetrative captive bolt stunning and nitrogen high expansion foam stunning for animals at the time of killing. Previously, these specific methods were not explicitly permitted or were under different regulatory conditions. Now, food businesses and slaughterhouses across the EU, including in Portugal, can utilize these new stunning techniques, provided they comply with associated technical specifications, potentially impacting operational practices and animal welfare standards in the meat industry.

Why it matters: The entry into force of the ban on destroying unsold goods and the significant deferral of key AI Act compliance deadlines provide both immediate operational changes for businesses and crucial breathing room for the tech sector, respectively, while the EU solidifies its position on circular economy and AI governance.

US Finalizes 25% Tariffs on Brazil: The U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) announced on July 15, 2026, final 25% tariffs on certain goods from Brazil under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974. These tariffs, effective July 22, 2026, target Brazilian measures impacting digital trade, electronic payment services, unfair preferential tariffs, anti-corruption interference, intellectual property, ethanol market access, and illegal deforestation. Previously, these specific goods were not subject to these additional duties. This action signals increased protectionist trade policy and could impact supply chains and the cost of Brazilian imports.

CFTC Ends Routine Reporting for Physical Commodity Swaps: On July 17, 2026, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) issued a final order terminating the routine daily and event-based Part 20 position-reporting requirements for physical commodity swaps for clearing organizations, clearing members, and swap dealers. Previously, these entities were required to file such reports. This action streamlines regulatory compliance for derivatives market participants by eliminating duplicative and costly reporting, though obligations for recordkeeping and compliance with special-call provisions remain.

CFTC Amends Margin Requirements for Uncleared Swaps: On July 14, 2026, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) approved a final rule amending margin requirements for uncleared swaps for swap dealers and major swap participants not subject to prudential regulator margin rules. The rule revises the definition of "margin affiliate" for certain collective investment vehicles (seeded funds), providing relief from posting and collecting initial margin for up to three years after the asset manager begins investing on behalf of the fund. Additionally, it expands eligible collateral to include money market funds that engage in reverse repo, repo, and securities lending activities, which were previously disqualified. This expands liquidity and reduces operational burdens for certain derivatives transactions, with an effective date 30 days after its publication in the Federal Register.

Treasury Sanctions Ransomware Facilitators: On July 13, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated two individuals, Dmytro Rashevskyi and Yegeniy Vladimirovich Silayev, and one entity, First VPN Service (1VPNS), for their roles in enabling ransomware attacks against American businesses and critical infrastructure. This action, taken under Executive Order 14390, blocks all property and interests in property of these designated persons that are within U.S. jurisdiction or in the possession or control of U.S. persons. These sanctions aim to disrupt the cybercriminal ecosystem by targeting those who provide essential services to ransomware groups.

Treasury Sanctions Iran's IRGC Weapons Procurement Network: On July 15, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) sanctioned seven individuals and entities involved in an international network supporting weapons procurement efforts on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). This action, taken pursuant to Executive Order 13382, seeks to disrupt the overseas procurement and financial networks sustaining Iran's weapons production and proliferation efforts, which followed Iranian attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz.
