🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Novas Regras para Crédito à Habitação e Consumo: As novas regras macroprudenciais do Banco de Portugal, em vigor desde 1 de agosto de 2026, reduziram a taxa de esforço máxima recomendada aos bancos de 50% para 45% do rendimento líquido mensal do agregado familiar. Anteriormente, os bancos tinham maior margem para exceções e podiam conceder empréstimos a 100% para imóveis da sua propriedade; agora, estas margens foram reduzidas, e a possibilidade de financiar a 100% para imóveis próprios do banco foi eliminada. Isto significa que as famílias e empresas terão um acesso mais rigoroso ao crédito, podendo ser-lhes aprovados montantes de financiamento mais baixos.

Ajuste Temporário ao Imposto Sobre Produtos Petrolíferos (ISP): A Portaria n.º 331-A/2026/1, de 7 de agosto, que entra em vigor hoje, 10 de agosto de 2026, ajusta o desconto extraordinário e temporário no ISP. Os novos descontos são de 59,16 € por 1000 litros para gasóleo rodoviário e de 35,39 € por 1000 litros para gasolina sem chumbo, face aos valores anteriormente aplicados (que resultavam da Portaria n.º 320-D/2026/1, de 31 de julho). Esta medida ajusta os preços dos combustíveis rodoviários, potencialmente resultando numa ligeira descida para consumidores e empresas.

Reforço da Proteção Animal na Proteção Civil: A Lei n.º 38/2026, de 3 de agosto, integra expressamente a busca, salvamento e socorro animal no âmbito da proteção civil, alterando a Lei de Bases da Proteção Civil. Anteriormente, a proteção civil não tinha um enquadramento legal explícito para estas atividades; agora, a busca e salvamento de animais em situações de emergência (como incêndios ou inundações) passa a ser uma competência formal e coordenada da proteção civil. Esta mudança melhora a resposta a emergências que afetam animais, beneficiando proprietários de animais e organizações de bem-estar animal.

Alteração ao Regime de Compensações por Ataques de Lobo Ibérico: O Governo alterou o regime de compensações aos produtores agropecuários por ataques de lobo ibérico, comunicado a 9 de agosto de 2026. Anteriormente, o valor das indemnizações era reduzido progressivamente consoante o número de ataques anuais (por exemplo, 90% a partir do 3.º ataque, 70% a partir do 8.º e 50% entre o 12.º e 15.º ataque), e havia exclusão automática para animais com menos de um mês de idade ou em caso de desaparecimento; agora, a redução progressiva foi eliminada, o desaparecimento de animais passa a ser elegível para indemnização com base em avaliação técnica, e a exclusão automática de animais com idade inferior a um mês foi removida. Estas alterações reforçam o apoio financeiro aos produtores em territórios com presença de lobo, tornando o sistema de compensações mais justo e adequado.

Why it matters: As novas regras de acesso ao crédito terão um impacto significativo na capacidade de endividamento das famílias e empresas, enquanto as medidas sobre combustíveis e compensações a produtores demonstram ajustes governamentais face a dinâmicas económicas e ambientais específicas.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

AI Act Transparency Obligations (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744): From August 2, 2026, providers of AI systems designed for direct interaction with natural persons must ensure users are informed they are interacting with AI. Previously, the AI Act's transparency obligations were set to apply generally from this date without specific transitional relief. Now, providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video, or text content must ensure outputs are machine-readable and identifiable as artificially generated. For generative AI systems already on the market before August 2, 2026, a compliance deadline for the machine-readable marking obligation (Article 50(2)) is extended to December 2, 2026, while new systems must comply immediately. This requires businesses, including Portuguese companies, that develop or deploy AI systems to review their design and disclosure mechanisms to avoid fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover.

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40): From August 12, 2026, this regulation directly applies across the EU, replacing the previous Packaging Directive (94/62/EC). Companies, including manufacturers, producers, distributors, online platforms, and importers of packaged goods, must ensure packaging complies with new design, sustainability, and labelling requirements. This includes limits of 100 mg/kg for the sum of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium in packaging, and specific limits on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging. All packaging must be designed for material recycling, and importers from outside the EU must hold an EU Declaration of Conformity and Technical Documentation for packaged goods and appoint an authorised representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State they export to. This directly impacts Portuguese businesses involved in packaging or importing packaged goods, requiring immediate review of their packaging materials and supply chains.

Extension of Suspension of EU Rebalancing Measures Against the United States: The European Union has extended indefinitely the suspension of its rebalancing measures against the United States. Previously, these measures, adopted in response to potential high US tariffs on EU exports, were suspended under Commission Implementing Regulation 2026/295 and set to expire on August 6, 2026. Now, via a new Commission Implementing Regulation, the suspension continues without an end-date, with the Commission maintaining the ability to reactivate measures if deemed necessary. This decision provides continued certainty and stability for EU exporters and importers, including Portuguese businesses, involved in transatlantic trade, keeping a potential trade-risk package off the immediate agenda.

Council Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1940 and Council Decision (CFSP) 2026/1939 on Restrictive Measures in respect of Ukraine: On August 7, 2026, the Council adopted Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1940 and Decision (CFSP) 2026/1939, further amending and implementing restrictive measures concerning actions undermining or threatening Ukraine's territorial integrity, sovereignty, and independence. These acts add five additional individuals holding senior positions in Russian defense and military technology companies to the sanctions list, subjecting them to asset freezes and travel bans, building upon previous measures under Regulation (EU) No 269/2014 and Decision 2014/145/CFSP. Businesses and financial institutions across the EU, including in Portugal, must continue to rigorously screen counterparties and ensure compliance with expanded asset freezes and travel bans, particularly those with existing ties to Russian defense and military technology sectors.

🇺🇸 US Policy

FTC Policy on Disparate Impact Claims: The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) announced on August 7, 2026, a policy statement clarifying that it will no longer pursue consumer-protection cases based solely on disparate-impact or "unfair discrimination" theories. Previously, the FTC brought cases where business practices, even if facially neutral, disproportionately affected protected groups without requiring proof of intent. Now, the FTC will require proof of intent to discriminate for such claims, such as those brought under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act. This signals a shift in the FTC's enforcement strategy, potentially narrowing the scope of certain consumer protection actions.

Adjusting Imports of Polysilicon and its Derivatives: President Trump signed a Proclamation on August 6, 2026, imposing a minimum import price program on polysilicon and a 15% ad valorem tariff on polysilicon derivative products to protect the domestic industry and supply chain from imports. These remedies will take effect 120 days after signing, around December 4, 2026. Previously, temporary safeguard tariffs on solar cells and modules were in place, which expired in February 2026. This action aims to secure the domestic supply chain for semiconductors and solar power and encourage onshoring of these industries.

Executive Orders on Birthright Citizenship and Birth Tourism: President Trump signed two executive orders on August 6, 2026, aiming to restrict who automatically becomes a U.S. citizen at birth, specifically targeting "birth tourism" and children born to certain categories of foreign nationals, including foreign government employees, alien enemies, or members of terrorist groups. These orders seek to deny visas or entry to parents for the purpose of obtaining U.S. citizenship for their children. Previously, children born in the U.S. and subject to its jurisdiction were generally considered citizens under the 14th Amendment, a principle the Supreme Court recently affirmed for children born to undocumented immigrants and temporary visitors. These actions could lead to further legal challenges and changes in immigration policy.

New Section 301 Duties on Most U.S. Imports: New Section 301 duties, replacing a temporary Section 122 tariff, took effect on July 24, 2026, and were widely reported on August 8, 2026. These duties impose an additional 10% or 12.5% tariff on most goods from 60 trading partners, covering approximately 99.4% of U.S. imports (with some exemptions for steel, autos, and USMCA-qualifying goods from Canada and Mexico). Previously, a temporary 10% Section 122 tariff was in effect since February 24, 2026. This action, justified by concerns over forced labor, significantly broadens the scope of U.S. tariffs and is designed for the long term.

OFAC Sanctions Designations: On August 6, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) published new names on its Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List (SDN List), based on determinations made on April 23, 2026. This action blocks property and interests in property subject to U.S. jurisdiction of these designated persons and prohibits U.S. persons from engaging in transactions with them, targeting illicit drug trade and terrorism financing. This expands the scope of individuals and entities subject to U.S. financial restrictions.

Why it matters: The U.S. government has enacted significant new trade tariffs and adjusted enforcement policies, alongside new executive actions impacting immigration, signalling a sustained focus on economic nationalism and border security.
