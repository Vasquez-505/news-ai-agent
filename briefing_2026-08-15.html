Prestação Social Única (PSU): O Decreto-Lei n.º 166/2026, de 13 de agosto, criou a Prestação Social Única, que entrou em vigor a 14 de agosto de 2026, com o objetivo de simplificar e racionalizar o subsistema de solidariedade não contributiva. Esta medida unifica diversas prestações sociais, como o Rendimento Social de Inserção (RSI) e várias pensões não contributivas (velhice, viuvez, orfandade e invalidez), sendo o seu valor de referência fixado em 50% do Indexante de Apoios Sociais (IAS). Cerca de 94% dos futuros beneficiários manterão ou verão o seu apoio aumentado face ao que recebem atualmente, embora o novo apoio só esteja plenamente operacional a 31 de dezembro de 2026 devido a adaptações do sistema da Segurança Social.

As instituições de ensino superior são agora obrigadas a aprovar regulamentos internos em conformidade com este novo regime para o ano letivo de 2027/2028. Esta lei assegura melhores condições de apoio e acessibilidade para estudantes com necessidades educativas específicas no ensino superior.

Regime Específico no Portugal 2030 para Operações com Financiamento PRR Revogado ou Cessado: O Decreto-Lei n.º 165/2026, de 13 de agosto, em vigor a 14 de agosto de 2026, alterou os Decretos-Leis n.º 5/2023 e n.º 20-A/2023, estabelecendo um regime específico no âmbito do Portugal 2030. Este regime é aplicável a operações promovidas por entidades públicas e do setor social e solidário cujas candidaturas aprovadas anteriormente no Plano de Recuperação e Resiliência (PRR) tiveram o financiamento revogado ou cessado. Permite a integração destas operações no Portugal 2030, salvaguardando a continuidade de investimentos de interesse público e a execução dos fundos europeus.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40): Previously, EU packaging rules were based on a 1994 directive, leading to fragmented national requirements and compliance challenges. Now, this new Regulation, applying directly across all 27 EU Member States from August 12, 2026, mandates that food-contact packaging must not contain per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) above strict limits. All packaging must also comply with a combined limit on heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium capped at 100 mg/kg). Businesses are required to hold an EU Declaration of Conformity and Technical Documentation for their packaging. This creates a harmonised framework for businesses, enhancing legal certainty and efficiency, while protecting consumer health and the environment. Portuguese businesses, particularly those in food, retail, and manufacturing, must update their packaging materials and documentation to ensure market access and avoid potential product removal.

Transparency Obligations under Article 50 of the Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act): Previously, there were no specific EU-wide transparency obligations for AI-generated content. From August 2, 2026, providers of AI systems designed for direct interaction with natural persons must inform users that they are interacting with an AI. For AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video, or text content, providers must ensure that the outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and are clearly identifiable as artificially generated or manipulated. This enhances user trust and accountability in AI, affecting technology companies and developers of AI-powered services across the EU, including those in Portugal.

e-Evidence Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1543): Previously, obtaining electronic evidence across borders within the EU could be a slow and complex process due to varying national rules. The core provisions of this Regulation, applicable from August 18, 2026, establish a harmonized framework for law enforcement and judicial authorities to request and obtain electronic evidence directly from service providers located in other Member States, significantly streamlining cross-border investigations. This will improve the speed and efficiency of criminal investigations across the EU by facilitating faster access to electronic data, affecting online service providers (including those operating in Portugal) who will be subject to direct requests from authorities in other Member States.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1872 amending Implementing Regulation (EU) 2016/2070: Previously, financial institutions adhered to reporting templates and instructions under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2016/2070, based on Directive 2013/36/EU. This new Implementing Regulation, published on August 12, 2026, amends and updates the benchmark portfolios, reporting templates, and reporting instructions to be applied by credit institutions and investment firms for their reporting obligations under Article 78(2) of Directive 2013/36/EU (Capital Requirements Directive). This ensures financial reporting standards remain current and harmonized across the EU, impacting financial institutions and investors in Portugal by updating their compliance requirements for supervisory reporting and enhancing transparency in financial markets.

🇺🇸 US Policy

Adjusting Imports of Unmanned Aircraft Systems and Components: President Donald J. Trump signed a Proclamation on August 13, 2026, imposing a 100% ad valorem tariff on certain sensitive drones (those with a maximum takeoff weight over 25 kilograms or thermal imaging capabilities) and their critical components. Additionally, a 25% ad valorem tariff is placed on other drones and components, and preferential tariffs (15% or 10%) are applied to imports from specific allied nations (European Union, Japan, Liechtenstein, Republic of Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan, and the United Kingdom) provided substantial hardware, software, and technology originate from those countries and the United States. These tariffs become effective 21 days after signing for most items, with a 180-day delay for certain components. Previously, these imports were not subject to these specific tariffs under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962. This action aims to bolster national security by strengthening America's domestic drone industry and supply chain, potentially increasing costs for imported drones and benefiting U.S. drone manufacturers.

Rebuilding the United States Navy and America's Shipbuilding Industrial Base: On August 13, 2026, President Donald J. Trump signed a National Security Presidential Memorandum directing the Secretary of War to establish a fifth Naval shipyard, the first in over 80 years, to expand the nation's submarine and aircraft carrier repair capabilities. The memorandum also mandates the creation of a Component Repair Center for critical submarine components, a comprehensive review, reform, and reorganization of Naval Sea Systems Command, and the replacement of Advanced Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch Systems and Advanced Weapons Elevators with traditional steam and hydraulic systems on the CVN-81 aircraft carrier during its construction. Previously, these specific directives for expanding and reforming the naval shipbuilding industrial base were not in effect. Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting Requirement Revision: The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued a final rule, effective August 14, 2026, that adopts with limited changes an interim final rule from March 26, 2025. This rule narrows the beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting requirements under the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA). Specifically, it continues to exempt U.S. person beneficial owners from having to report their BOI and from providing BOI to reporting companies. It also exempts reporting companies from submitting information about U.S. person company applicants to FinCEN and exempts U.S. persons from the requirement to update information provided to obtain a FinCEN identifier. Previously, the initial Reporting Rule effective January 1, 2024, generally required reporting of such information. This rule reduces compliance burdens for certain U.S. companies and individuals, potentially easing administrative costs for small and medium-sized entities.

SEC Staff Discontinuation of Shareholder Proposal No-Action Relief: On August 14, 2026, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) Division of Corporation Finance announced it will entirely discontinue its practice of responding to shareholder proposal no-action requests submitted under Rule 14a-8 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, effective immediately. Previously, the Staff provided no-action responses, including limited consideration for requests under Rule 14a-8(i)(1) concerning precatory proposals and issuing "no-objection" letters for Rule 14a-8(j) notices. This change means companies will no longer receive Staff guidance on whether they can exclude shareholder proposals, potentially increasing uncertainty in corporate governance and leading to more direct engagement or litigation regarding shareholder resolutions.
