🌍 World News

EU-China Trade: European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Chinese Premier Li Qiang signed the "Brussels-Beijing Green Trade Accord" in Brussels on September 25, 2026. The deal eliminates tariffs on 40% of EU-made industrial machinery in exchange for China reducing state subsidies on EV batteries by 15% over the next 36 months. German Chancellor Olaf Scholz responded by announcing a €12 billion increase in domestic semiconductor investments to offset remaining trade imbalances. This reshapes global renewable energy supply chains and stabilizes the Euro-Yuan exchange rate. Financial Times

Ukraine-Russia Conflict: President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and President Vladimir Putin signed the "Odessa Framework" ceasefire agreement on September 25, 2026, following mediation by the United Nations. The treaty establishes a 20-kilometre demilitarized zone across the current frontline and mandates the phased withdrawal of all heavy artillery from the contact line. NATO Secretary General Jens Stoltenberg confirmed the alliance will maintain a permanent security presence in Poland and the Baltic states despite the truce. This ends the largest land war in Europe since 1945 and pivots billions in military aid toward regional reconstruction. Reuters

Global AI Governance: Representatives from 65 nations, including US Secretary of State Antony Blinken and China's Foreign Minister Wang Yi, ratified the "Geneva AI Governance Protocol" on September 25, 2026. The treaty legally bans the deployment of fully autonomous lethal weapon systems and requires a mandatory public registry for AI models with training costs exceeding $10 billion. OpenAI and Google DeepMind announced a joint compliance framework to provide quarterly transparency audits to the newly formed Global AI Oversight Board. This prevents a global AI arms race and establishes the first binding international law for artificial intelligence. AP

Why it matters: The simultaneous stabilization of European security, global AI regulation, and Sino-European trade indicates a systemic shift toward institutional multilateralism over unilateral volatility.

🇺🇸 US Policy

DOE Clean Hydrogen Production Credit (45V): The Department of Energy and Treasury finalized the "three pillars" (emissions, additionality, and timeliness) required to qualify for the Section 45V tax credit. Previously: Guidelines were proposed with variable emissions tiers. Now: A strict lifecycle greenhouse gas emissions threshold is mandated to receive the full $3/kg credit, effective for projects commencing January 1, 2025. This significantly increases the capital requirements for "blue" hydrogen and favors "green" hydrogen infrastructure. Federal Register.

Treasury FEOC Determination (EV Credits): The Treasury Department issued final guidance defining "Foreign Entity of Concern" (FEOC) for the Section 30D Clean Vehicle Credit. Previously: Ownership thresholds for "control" by covered governments were ambiguous. Now: Any entity where a government of a covered nation (e.g., China) holds $\ge 25\%$ of voting rights or board seats is designated a FEOC, disqualifying the vehicle from the $7,500 credit. This accelerates the forced decoupling of automotive battery supply chains from Chinese ownership. Federal Register.

OFAC SDN List Updates (Russian Procurement): The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated dozens of entities and individuals to the Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list for facilitating the procurement of dual-use technology for Russia. Previously: These entities were not listed. Now: All US-based assets are frozen, and US persons are prohibited from all transactions with these entities, effective immediately. This increases operational and compliance risk for global semiconductor distributors and logistics firms. Treasury.gov.

Why it matters: The US government is utilizing strict tax credit thresholds and sanctions to aggressively decouple critical energy and technology supply chains from China and Russia.
