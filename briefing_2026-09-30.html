Regime Aplicável à Assessoria dos Juízes e dos Magistrados do Ministério Público (Decreto-Lei n.º 196/2026): Este decreto-lei, publicado a 29 de setembro de 2026, estabelece um quadro jurídico unificado e mais claro para a assistência prestada por assessores não magistrados a juízes e procuradores do Ministério Público. Anteriormente, as regras eram dispersas, levando a aplicações heterogéneas; agora, são consolidadas as competências e a dependência hierárquica e funcional dos assessores em gabinetes dedicados. O número máximo de lugares de assessor será fixado por portaria conjunta dos membros do Governo responsáveis pelas áreas das finanças, administração pública e justiça, após consulta aos conselhos judiciais competentes. O impacto prático visa aumentar a eficiência e celeridade dos processos judiciais e do Ministério Público, o que pode resultar numa resolução mais rápida de casos para cidadãos e empresas.

Alteração aos Pesos e Dimensões Máximos Autorizados para Veículos em Circulação (Decreto-Lei n.º 195/2026): Publicado a 25 de setembro de 2026, este decreto-lei revê os pesos e dimensões máximos autorizados para as configurações de veículos Euro-Modulares em Portugal. Anteriormente, as configurações Euro-Modulares tinham um comprimento máximo de 25,25 metros. Agora, é permitida a circulação de conjuntos de veículos Euro-Modulares com um peso máximo de até 72,00 toneladas e um comprimento máximo de até 32,00 metros, ajustando também os raios de viragem (exterior 14,50m e interior 6,5m) desde que o itinerário garanta compatibilidade geométrica. O impacto prático é o aumento significativo da capacidade de transporte de mercadorias por estrada, reduzindo custos logísticos para as empresas do setor e alinhando as regulamentações nacionais com as de Espanha.

Extensão do Passe Ferroviário Verde (Decreto-Lei n.º 195-A/2026): Este decreto-lei, publicado a 25 de setembro de 2026, alarga a abrangência do "Passe Ferroviário Verde" aos serviços ferroviários urbanos nas áreas metropolitanas de Lisboa e Porto. Previamente, o passe de 20€ cobria serviços ferroviários nacionais e regionais, mas não incluía explicitamente as redes urbanas nestas cidades. Agora, pelo mesmo custo mensal de 20€ (IVA incluído), o passe integra estes serviços urbanos, eliminando a necessidade de bilhetes separados para os passageiros. O impacto prático simplifica e potencialmente torna mais acessível as deslocações para os cidadãos que utilizam transportes públicos em Lisboa e Porto, promovendo uma maior utilização do comboio.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU 'Right to Repair' Rules (amending Goods Directive (EU) 2019/771 and Delegated Directive (EU) 2026/74): These rules, effectively in force as of September 23, 2026, require manufacturers of goods subject to legislative repairability requirements to prioritize repair over replacement once the seller's liability under the Goods Directive has expired. They must also provide consumers with repair information without impeding third-party repairs. Previously, consumers often faced limited options for repair, frequently leading to product replacement. Now, the emphasis shifts to extending product lifecycles through mandatory manufacturer repair and open access to repair information, impacting manufacturers established within and outside the EU who sell goods in the bloc. Portugal is among the Member States that recently received a letter of formal notice for failing to completely transpose Delegated Directive (EU) 2026/74, highlighting the need for national legislative alignment.

Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (ECGT Directive): This Directive entered into application on September 27, 2026, establishing strict new rules against misleading sustainability-related marketing, commonly known as "greenwashing." It bans generic unverified environmental claims (e.g., "environmentally friendly," "biodegradable") and claims of neutral, reduced, or positive emissions based solely on Greenhouse Gas (GHG) offsetting schemes. Companies making forward-looking climate pledges must now provide detailed, realistic, publicly available implementation plans with time-bound targets and allocated resources, verified by an independent third-party expert. Previously, over half of green claims in the EU were vague or misleading, and 40% unsubstantiated. This impacts all businesses operating in the EU, including those in Portugal, by requiring greater transparency and substantiation for environmental claims and sustainability labels.

Regulation revising EU rules on coordination of national social security systems: Formally adopted by the Council on September 28, 2026, this regulation revises rules concerning unemployment benefits, long-term care benefits, access to welfare benefits for economically inactive persons, family benefits, and applicable legislation for mobile workers. A key change for companies with mobile workforces (e.g., those posting employees to other EU countries) is the shift from retrospective to prospective compliance for posting situations; previously, A1 applications could often be submitted after work began, but now they must be submitted *before* work in another country commences to establish coverage and ensure exemption from local social security. The revised coordination rules will take effect approximately two years from the regulation's publication in the Official Journal. This is significant for Portuguese businesses with employees working across EU borders and for Portuguese citizens seeking to work or claim benefits in other Member States.

Recast Hydrogen and Decarbonised Gas Directive (EU) 2024/1788: The European Commission initiated infringement procedures on September 25, 2026, against 26 Member States, including Portugal, for failing to notify the complete transposition of this Directive by the August 5, 2026, deadline. This Directive updates the previous Gas Directive 2009/73/EC and Regulation 715/2009, introducing a comprehensive regulatory framework for dedicated hydrogen infrastructure alongside natural gas. Its aim is to facilitate the uptake of renewable and low-carbon gases while ensuring energy security and affordability. Previously, the regulatory framework primarily focused on traditional natural gas. This action requires Portugal and other non-compliant Member States to accelerate national legislative changes to incorporate the new rules for hydrogen and decarbonized gases, impacting energy companies and investors in these sectors.
