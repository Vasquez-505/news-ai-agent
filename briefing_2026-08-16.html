Prestação Social Única (PSU): O Decreto-Lei n.º 166/2026, de 13 de agosto, cria a Prestação Social Única (PSU), um benefício pecuniário de natureza não contributiva que agrega 13 prestações sociais existentes num modelo integrado. Anteriormente, existia um sistema fragmentado de apoios sociais não contributivos; agora, a PSU visa simplificar o acesso, eliminar desincentivos ao regresso ao trabalho e assegurar uma transição progressiva para o mercado de trabalho, sem prejudicar os atuais beneficiários. O impacto prático é a simplificação do acesso a apoios sociais e uma maior clareza para famílias, em particular aquelas que recebem o Rendimento Social de Inserção, agregados numerosos e beneficiários de subsídios de parentalidade, orfandade e viuvez. (Entrou em vigor a 13 de agosto de 2026).

Novo Regime de Apoios Financeiros do Estado às Artes: O Decreto-Lei n.º 164-A/2026, de 12 de agosto, e a Portaria n.º 345-A/2026/1, de 14 de agosto, revisam e melhoram os mecanismos de apoio financeiro do Estado às artes visuais e performativas. O Decreto-Lei introduz novos domínios de apoio, como artes e ofícios tradicionais e artes digitais, e cria um fator de atualização para o apoio quadrienal, reforçando a estabilidade financeira das estruturas. A Portaria aprova um novo Regulamento dos Programas de Apoio às Artes, consolidando normas e reforçando a monitorização, auditoria e prevenção do duplo financiamento. Anteriormente, os apoios eram regidos por diplomas que se mostravam menos abrangentes e com menor estabilidade de financiamento; agora, as estruturas artísticas e os profissionais da cultura beneficiarão de financiamento mais estável, transparente e diversificado. (Decreto-Lei n.º 164-A/2026 entrou em vigor a 13 de agosto de 2026; Portaria n.º 345-A/2026/1 entrou em vigor a 15 de agosto de 2026).

Regime para Estudantes com Necessidades Educativas Específicas no Ensino Superior e Acessibilidade: A Lei n.º 44/2026, de 10 de agosto, aprova o regime jurídico dos estudantes com necessidades educativas específicas no ensino superior e altera pela quinta vez o Decreto-Lei n.º 163/2006, de 8 de agosto, que aprova o regime da acessibilidade a edifícios e estabelecimentos que recebem público, via pública e edifícios habitacionais. Antes, não existia um regime jurídico consolidado para estes estudantes; agora, as instituições de ensino superior deverão aprovar regulamentos internos para o ano letivo de 2027/2028, em conformidade com o novo regime, e as regras de acessibilidade em vários tipos de edifícios são melhoradas. Isto significa que os estudantes com necessidades específicas terão um quadro legal mais claro de apoio, e a acessibilidade em espaços públicos e habitacionais é ampliada. (As alterações ao Decreto-Lei n.º 163/2006 entraram em vigor a 11 de agosto de 2026; o novo regime para estudantes aplica-se a partir do ano letivo 2027/2028).

O impacto prático é uma melhoria no enquadramento profissional e nas condições de trabalho para estes profissionais no sistema público de saúde. (Entrou em vigor a 12 de agosto de 2026).

Limite da Taxa de Esforço no Crédito Habitação: Uma nova Recomendação Macroprudencial do Banco de Portugal, em vigor desde 1 de agosto de 2026, torna a análise de novos pedidos de crédito à habitação mais exigente, reduzindo o limite recomendado da taxa de esforço (DSTI) de 50% para 45%. EUROPEAN UNION POLICY

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (EU) 2025/40: From August 12, 2026, this regulation introduces new requirements for all packaging placed on the EU market. Previously, national rules based on the 1994 Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive allowed for varied implementation. Now, it sets harmonized standards, with initial obligations including a near-total ban on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging and a strict combined limit of 100 mg/kg for lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium in all packaging. Businesses must ensure compliance with these substance restrictions and maintain documentation confirming packaging conformity, including an EU Declaration of Conformity and Technical Documentation. Importers from outside the EU must verify compliance and may need to appoint an authorized representative in each Member State. This significantly impacts manufacturers, importers, and distributors across all sectors, including Portuguese businesses, by standardizing environmental and safety requirements for packaging and potentially requiring costly reformulation and supply chain audits. [European Commission / EUR-Lex]

Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) – Transparency Obligations (Article 50): As of August 2, 2026, providers and deployers of certain AI systems must comply with new transparency obligations. Previously, there were no specific, comprehensive EU-wide transparency rules for AI systems. Now, providers of AI systems that directly interact with individuals (e.g., chatbots) must disclose that users are engaging with AI, unless it's obvious. Providers of AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic audio, image, video, or text must embed machine-readable markings and provide a detection mechanism. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals. For deepfakes and AI-generated text on public-interest matters, deployers must disclose artificial generation or manipulation. These obligations apply immediately to all in-scope systems regardless of when they were placed on the market, with a transitional period until December 2, 2026, for marking and detection obligations for generative AI systems already on the market. Non-compliance can result in fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. This affects a wide range of businesses, including tech companies and those utilizing AI in customer-facing applications or content generation, by imposing strict disclosure requirements. [European Commission / Cooley]

Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744): Entered into force on July 27, 2026, following publication in the Official Journal on July 24, 2026. This regulation amends the AI Act by delaying the enforcement of most obligations related to high-risk AI systems. Previously, core obligations for Annex III high-risk AI systems were set to apply from August 2, 2026. Now, obligations for stand-alone high-risk AI systems (Annex III) are deferred to December 2, 2027, and for AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) to August 2, 2028. The Digital Omnibus also introduced new prohibited AI practices and clarified the term "safety component". While providing businesses, including Portuguese investors in AI, with additional time to prepare for high-risk AI system compliance, the transparency obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act remain unchanged and took effect on August 2, 2026. [EUR-Lex / White & Case LLP]

Why it matters: The EU has initiated a significant regulatory overhaul in both environmental sustainability and artificial intelligence this week, with the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation imposing immediate chemical restrictions and the AI Act's transparency rules taking effect, while simultaneously providing extended deadlines for high-risk AI systems to allow for better industry preparation.

FinCEN Final Rule on Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting Exemptions: The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued a final rule, effective August 14, 2026, permanently exempting domestic companies and U.S. persons from beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting requirements under the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA). The rule also eliminates FinCEN ID update obligations for U.S. persons who obtained them and narrows reporting for foreign companies by removing the requirement to report U.S. person company applicants or U.S. person beneficial owners. Previously, many domestic companies and U.S. persons were required to report BOI to FinCEN, with the initial reporting rule effective January 1, 2024. This significantly reduces compliance burdens for domestic businesses under the CTA.

Presidential Proclamation on Adjusting Imports of Unmanned Aircraft Systems and Components: On August 13, 2026, President Donald Trump signed a proclamation imposing new tariffs on imported Unmanned Aircraft Systems (UAS) and their components under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962. The proclamation establishes a 100% ad valorem duty on UAS with a maximum take-off weight exceeding 25 kilograms, UAS that integrate thermal imagers, and UAS docking stations. A 25% ad valorem duty is applied to smaller UAS (25 kilograms or less) and certain other UAS components. The proclamation also establishes an incentive program for new U.S. production capacity for UAS and components. Tariffs for sensitive items take effect 21 days from August 13, 2026, while those for certain components take effect 180 days from that date. This action aims to bolster national security by encouraging domestic production and reducing reliance on foreign supply chains for drone technology.

HHS Final Rule Prohibiting Federal Funding for Sex-Rejecting Procedures for Minors: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), an agency within the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), published a final rule on August 13, 2026, prohibiting the use of Federal Medicaid and Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) funding for "sex-rejecting procedures" furnished to individuals under the age of 18 for Medicaid and under 19 for CHIP. The regulations are effective October 13, 2026. For beneficiaries actively receiving cross-sex hormone therapy, State Medicaid and CHIP agencies may continue to claim Federal Financial Participation for those medications for up to 6 months from the effective date. Previously, federal funding could be used for such procedures, subject to state regulations. This rule significantly alters federal healthcare funding policies regarding gender-affirming care for minors.

SEC Division of Corporation Finance Discontinuation of No-Action Responses for Shareholder Proposals: Effective August 14, 2026, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) Division of Corporation Finance announced it will discontinue responding to all Rule 14a-8 no-action requests regarding the exclusion of shareholder proposals. This expands upon a previous 2025–2026 policy that suspended most responses but still considered certain requests and issued "no-objection" letters. Companies intending to exclude shareholder proposals must still notify the Commission and proponents, but the Staff will no longer provide guidance or express views on the exclusion arguments. This change significantly alters the regulatory guidance framework for public companies navigating shareholder proposals during proxy season.
