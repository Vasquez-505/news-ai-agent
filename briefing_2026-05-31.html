🇪🇺 EU Policy

Internal Market Emergency and Resilience Act (IMERA): This Act entered into application on May 29, 2026, establishing a comprehensive framework for crisis preparedness and coordination across the Single Market. Previously, the EU lacked a structured, unified approach to internal market resilience during crises. Now, IMERA introduces a three-tier system (contingency planning, vigilance mode, and emergency mode) allowing the Council to activate closer monitoring of supply chains for critical goods and services, and, in cases of severe disruption, to trigger targeted measures like coordinated procurement, information requests to companies, and restrictions on national measures that fragment the Single Market, including export bans within the EU. This will increase predictability and resilience for all EU businesses, including those in Portugal, ensuring a more coordinated response to potential supply chain disruptions.

Council Regulation (EU) 2026/1181 on Common Customs Tariff duties for certain fertilisers: Published on May 29, 2026, this regulation suspends Common Customs Tariff duties for specific fertilisers and establishes autonomous tariff quotas for their management. Previously, these duties were applicable to such imports. Now, for the specified fertilisers, these duties are suspended, aiming to alleviate import costs and ensure supply within the EU. This directly benefits importers and users of these fertilisers in the EU, potentially reducing operational costs for Portuguese agricultural and industrial businesses reliant on these materials.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1144 on electronic licences for the export of cultural goods: Published on May 29, 2026, this regulation lays down provisions for electronic licences required for the export of cultural goods from the EU, thereby repealing and replacing Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 1081/2012. Previously, businesses navigated a potentially varied or less digitised system for export licensing. Now, a harmonised electronic licensing system is established, streamlining administrative procedures for businesses and individuals engaged in exporting cultural goods from any EU Member State.

Commission Infringement Procedures for the Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (Directive (EU) 2024/825): On May 27, 2026, the European Commission initiated infringement procedures by sending letters of formal notice to 20 Member States, including Portugal, for failing to fully transpose this Directive into national law. The deadline for transposition was March 27, 2026. Previously, national rules on green claims and product information varied. Now, Member States must adopt national measures that improve the reliability and transparency of green claims and sustainability labels, and ensure consumers have better information on product durability, repairability, and legal guarantee rights within two months, or face further action from the Commission. This means Portuguese businesses making green claims or selling consumer goods must ensure compliance with stricter standards on environmental information and product transparency.

Commission Infringement Procedures for Directive (EU) 2024/869 on Protection of Workers from Carcinogens, Mutagens and Reprotoxic Substances: On May 27, 2026, the European Commission initiated infringement procedures by issuing letters of formal notice to 10 Member States, including Portugal, for failing to fully transpose this Directive into national law.
