🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Prestação Social Única: O Decreto-Lei n.º 166/2026, de 13 de agosto, criou a Prestação Social Única (PSU) no âmbito do subsistema de solidariedade, que entrou em vigor a 14 de agosto de 2026. A nova medida extingue e incorpora 13 prestações sociais anteriormente existentes, como a pensão social de velhice, o subsídio social de desemprego e o rendimento social de inserção. A PSU também consagra um regime de incentivo ao trabalho, com um mecanismo de redução gradual da prestação em função da evolução dos rendimentos do trabalho, garantindo que não há perda de rendimento disponível quando estes aumentam. A alteração simplifica o acesso a apoios sociais não contributivos e incentiva a inserção profissional.

Incentivos Fiscais ao Mecenato Cultural: O Decreto-Lei n.º 167/2026, de 17 de agosto, que entrou em vigor a 18 de agosto de 2026, alterou o Estatuto dos Benefícios Fiscais e criou a Plataforma Nacional do Mecenato. A legislação revista os limites de dedutibilidade dos donativos e aumenta as percentagens de majoração aplicáveis ao mecenato cultural, além de fixar um limite global à dedução dos donativos. O regime anterior, considerado menos abrangente, é substituído por um modelo mais transparente e eficaz. A prática desta medida é um maior estímulo ao envolvimento do setor privado no apoio à cultura, através de benefícios fiscais reforçados para empresas e indivíduos.

Prazo de Pagamento de Contribuições à Segurança Social em Agosto: A Segurança Social Direta anunciou, a 19 de agosto de 2026, que o prazo para o pagamento das contribuições referentes ao mês de agosto é alargado até 31 de agosto de 2026, em vez da data habitual de 25 de agosto. Esta medida abrange trabalhadores independentes, serviço doméstico, seguro social voluntário, entidades contratantes, entidades beneficiárias de profissionais da cultura e produtores agrícolas das Regiões Autónomas. A extensão do prazo oferece maior flexibilidade para que empresas e trabalhadores efetuem os seus pagamentos durante o período de férias.

Gratuitidade de Medicamentos para Idosos: O Decreto-Lei n.º 169/2026, de 18 de agosto, que entrou em vigor a 19 de agosto de 2026, introduz ajustamentos ao regime de benefícios adicionais de saúde para os beneficiários do complemento solidário para idosos. Embora a gratuitidade dos medicamentos sujeitos a prescrição médica para este grupo se mantenha, a nova legislação visa promover uma maior utilização de medicamentos genéricos e sustentáveis, otimizando a racionalidade e equidade na utilização dos recursos públicos. A alteração garante o acesso ininterrupto a medicamentos gratuitos para os idosos com complemento solidário, ao mesmo tempo que promove a sustentabilidade do Serviço Nacional de Saúde.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40) – General Application: From August 12, 2026, this regulation applies across the EU, replacing the previous Directive 94/62/EC. Previously, Member States had discretion in implementing packaging rules, leading to divergent national approaches. Now, the regulation introduces harmonised standards, requiring packaging placed on the EU market to comply with new substance restrictions, including minimizing "substances of concern" and limiting the sum of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium to 100 mg/kg. It also establishes new restrictions for PFAS in food-contact packaging. Manufacturers, importers, and distributors must hold specific documentation like the EU Declaration of Conformity and Technical Documentation, and importers from outside the EU must appoint an authorized representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State. This is a significant change for manufacturing, import, and distribution companies, including Portuguese businesses, as packaging compliance becomes a precondition for placing products on the EU market.

EU Artificial Intelligence (AI) Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) – Transparency Obligations and Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) – Amendments: Effective August 2, 2026, key transparency obligations of the AI Act began to apply. Previously, there were no specific EU-wide rules requiring AI transparency. Now, providers of AI systems interacting with individuals (e.g., chatbots) must disclose the AI interaction, and providers of AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic audio, image, video, or text must embed machine-readable markings (watermarking) and provide a detection mechanism. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals. Additionally, the Digital Omnibus on AI, which entered into force on July 27, 2026, amended the AI Act by postponing the application deadlines for obligations related to stand-alone high-risk AI systems to December 2, 2027, and for AI systems embedded as safety components to August 2, 2028 (both previously August 2, 2026). It also introduced a grace period for generative AI systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, allowing until December 2, 2026, to comply with watermarking. Furthermore, new prohibitions ban AI systems used to generate child sexual abuse material or non-consensual intimate content, with businesses required to review safeguards by December 2, 2026. This is crucial for tech companies, AI developers, and businesses using AI in the EU, including those in Portugal, as it provides more time for compliance with complex high-risk AI rules while immediately enforcing new transparency standards and prohibiting harmful AI applications.

EU e-Evidence Regulation – Application Date: As of August 18, 2026, the EU e-Evidence Regulation is now applicable, streamlining the process for law enforcement to obtain electronic evidence across the Union. Previously, law enforcement relied on often lengthy national legal assistance procedures for cross-border data requests. Now, the regulation introduces a European Production Order, allowing authorities to request data directly from service providers in other Member States. Organizations offering certain online services in an EU Member State must register an 'addressee' to receive and reply to requests for e-Evidence by August 18, 2026, or within six months of their establishment. This directly impacts online service providers operating in the EU, including Portuguese firms, by simplifying cross-border data access for law enforcement but imposing new administrative obligations on businesses to designate a contact point.

EU Platform Work Directive – Transposition Deadline: EU Member States have until December 2, 2026, to transpose this Directive into national law. Previously, the legal status of platform workers (gig economy) varied significantly across Member States, often categorizing them as self-employed with limited protections. The Directive now introduces a rebuttable presumption of employment status for platform workers based on certain criteria, potentially reclassifying many as employees and granting them associated labor rights (e.g., minimum wage, collective bargaining, social security). It also sets stricter requirements for the use of automated monitoring and decision-making systems by platforms. This will significantly impact digital platforms and the gig economy business models throughout the EU, including in Portugal, likely increasing labor costs and requiring substantial operational adjustments for companies relying on platform workers.

Why it matters: This week marks a significant shift towards more harmonized and stringent digital and environmental compliance in the EU, particularly with the general application of new packaging rules and the immediate enforcement of transparency obligations under the AI Act, alongside critical amendments delaying some high-risk AI compliance deadlines.
