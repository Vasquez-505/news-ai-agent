Redução das Taxas de IRS para 2026: Foram anunciadas novas reduções nas taxas de Imposto sobre o Rendimento das Pessoas Singulares (IRS) para os seis primeiros escalões, com efeitos retroativos a 1 de janeiro de 2026. As taxas são ajustadas da seguinte forma: o 1º escalão passa de 12,50% para 12,20%; o 2º de 15,70% para 15,20%; o 3º de 21,20% para 20,20%; o 4º de 24,10% para 23,60%; o 5º de 31,10% para 30,60%; e o 6º de 34,90% para 34,60%. A prática destas alterações será sentida nas retenções na fonte a partir de novembro de 2026.

Novo Modelo de Simplificação do Ciclo Contributivo (SCC) da Segurança Social: O Governo publicou o Decreto-Lei 127/2025, que institui um novo sistema para a comunicação de remunerações à Segurança Social, tornando a entrega de declarações mensais desnecessária para as empresas aderentes. Anteriormente, as empresas submetiam declarações mensais de remunerações; agora, devem introduzir inicialmente os dados de remunerações fixas e apenas os componentes variáveis necessitam de ser adicionados, com o sistema a pré-preencher os dados para confirmação ou alteração pelas empresas até ao dia 20 do mês seguinte, e o prazo de pagamento passa do dia 15 para o dia 25 do mês seguinte. A adesão ao SCC é facultativa em 2026, mas torna-se obrigatória para todas as entidades empregadoras a partir de 1 de janeiro de 2027. As empresas devem, portanto, assegurar o registo atualizado de trabalhadores ativos e inativos, bem como a atualização dos enquadramentos e remunerações até 31 de dezembro de 2026.

Proteção de Indicações Geográficas para Produtos Artesanais e Industriais (Decreto-Lei n.º 194/2026): Este Decreto-Lei, de 25 de setembro de 2026, assegura a execução em Portugal do Regulamento (UE) 2023/2411, que confere proteção unitária às indicações geográficas de produtos artesanais e industriais em toda a União Europeia. Previamente, esta proteção era maioritariamente restrita a vinhos, bebidas espirituosas e produtos agrícolas; agora, abrange produtos como joalharia, vestuário, cerâmica e cestaria, estabelecendo um processo de registo em duas fases (nacional e europeia) para evitar múltiplos registos. Esta medida entrou em vigor a 26 de setembro de 2026.

Criação do Julgado de Paz do Concelho da Figueira da Foz (Decreto-Lei n.º 192/2026): O Decreto-Lei n.º 192/2026, de 22 de setembro, cria um Julgado de Paz no concelho da Figueira da Foz. Alteração à Tramitação Eletrónica de Processos Judiciais (Portaria n.º 435/2026/1): A Portaria n.º 435/2026/1, de 23 de setembro, altera a Portaria n.º 350-A/2025/1, que regulamenta a tramitação eletrónica de processos nos tribunais judiciais, administrativos, fiscais e no Ministério Público. 🇪🇺 EU Policy

Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (Directive (EU) 2024/825): Previously, vague and unsubstantiated environmental claims could be made under general consumer protection laws. Now, this Directive explicitly bans generic environmental claims (e.g., "eco-friendly," "biodegradable," or "climate-neutral" based on offsets) unless supported by verifiable, recognized excellent environmental performance. It also restricts sustainability labels to those based on official certification or established by public authorities and mandates that future environmental pledges from companies must be backed by detailed, realistic, publicly available implementation plans, time-bound targets, allocated resources, and independent third-party verification. Furthermore, it prohibits marketing a product feature as unique if it is a general legal requirement. This Directive entered into application on September 27, 2026, and all businesses operating in the EU and communicating environmental claims to consumers must comply. This significantly impacts marketing, advertising, and packaging practices for products and services across the EU, including in Portugal, by demanding greater transparency, with potential fines up to 4% of related product revenue for non-compliance.

Commission Delegated Regulations (EU) 2026/1563 (Revised ESRS) and (EU) 2026/1560 (Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard): Previously, companies within the scope of the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) applied an initial set of European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS). Now, Regulation (EU) 2026/1563 replaces these with a revised, simplified set of ESRS, becoming mandatory for CSRD-scope companies for financial years beginning on or after January 1, 2027 (with first reports in 2028), though early adoption for FY2026 is an option. Simultaneously, Regulation (EU) 2026/1560 establishes a voluntary sustainability reporting standard for undertakings outside the mandatory CSRD scope, entering into force on September 24, 2026. This voluntary standard also introduces a "value chain cap" from January 1, 2027, preventing mandatory CSRD reporters from requiring information beyond a defined short list of essential environmental, social, and governance (ESG) data points from their value chain undertakings with 1,000 or fewer employees. This simplifies sustainability reporting for larger EU businesses and provides a structured voluntary standard for smaller entities, potentially easing the reporting burden on SMEs, including those in Portugal, that are part of larger companies' value chains.

Council Decision (CFSP) prolonging restrictive measures concerning actions undermining or threatening Ukraine's territorial integrity: Previously, restrictive measures targeting over 3,000 individuals and entities were in effect. Now, the Council has decided to prolong these measures for another 36 months, extending them until September 22, 2029. These measures include travel restrictions for natural persons, the freezing of assets, and a ban on making funds or other economic resources available to the listed individuals and entities. This decision was formally taken on September 22, 2026, with immediate effect. It continues to impact financial institutions, businesses, and investors in Portugal and across the EU, who must ensure compliance with these extended sanctions.

Council Decisions (EU) 2026/2144 and (EU) 2026/2145 (Fisheries with UK): Previously, the EU's position in annual consultations with the United Kingdom on fishing opportunities and total allowable catches (TACs) was based on decisions with an earlier expiry. Now, these Decisions extend the application of the underlying legal frameworks (Decision (EU) 2021/1765 on the EU's position in the Specialised Committee on Fisheries and Decision (EU) 2021/1875 on TACs with the UK) until June 30, 2038, with a review scheduled by December 31, 2036. Both decisions entered into force on September 22, 2026. This provides enhanced long-term clarity and stability for the EU fishing sector, including Portuguese fishing interests, regarding access to fishing grounds and quotas negotiated with the UK.

Why it matters: The entry into application of new anti-greenwashing rules significantly alters how businesses communicate environmental claims to consumers, fostering greater transparency and accountability across the EU market.

🇺🇸 US Policy

US and China Announce Reciprocal Tariff Reductions: On September 28, 2026, the United States and China announced an agreement to reciprocally cut tariffs on approximately $30 billion worth of non-sensitive products each, totaling $60 billion in trade, for a temporary period of 90 days. This action, a result of recent leader meetings, reduces tariffs on 77 Chinese products imported into the U.S. and 1,619 American products exported to China. Previously, tariffs on these goods were significantly higher, with some U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods reaching 145% and Chinese tariffs on U.S. goods at 125%; now, a 10% base tariff remains for these specific goods, excluding existing fentanyl tariffs. The reductions are effective as of September 28, 2026. This temporary de-escalation of trade tensions could positively impact companies involved in the specified product categories by reducing import/export costs and fostering market access.

FTC and States Settle Antitrust Case with Corteva Inc.: On September 28, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and a coalition of 12 state attorneys general reached a $35 million settlement with pesticide manufacturer Corteva Inc. The agreement prohibits Corteva from implementing crop loyalty programs that require customers to meet purchasing targets across multiple active ingredients or tie benefits to multi-year purchases. Specifically, Corteva is now barred from requiring customers to purchase over 50% of certain pesticide needs from the company to qualify for benefits and from retaliating against customers for buying competing products. Previously, Corteva was alleged to have used these programs to maintain monopoly power. The settlement is effective immediately. This action aims to increase competition in the pesticide market, potentially leading to lower prices for farmers and altering business practices for agricultural input suppliers.

Department of War (DOW) Launches CLASS ACT Program to Ease Contractor Security Clearances: On September 24, 2026, the U.S. Department of War's (DOW) Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) initiated the Classified Access to Address Critical Technology (CLASS ACT) program, a 36-month pilot. This program allows the DOW to sponsor nontraditional defense contractors for facility security clearances *before* a specific contracting requirement exists, eliminating the traditional prerequisite of holding a DOW prime contract or subcontract. Initially, it targets technology companies developing sensors and effectors for maritime counter-unmanned systems and private capital firms with relevant investment experience. This program is effective as of September 24, 2026. This initiative seeks to broaden the defense industrial base by lowering barriers for commercial technology companies and investors to participate in classified defense projects, potentially accelerating innovation in critical military technologies.

HHS/CMS Imposes Temporary Moratorium on Healthcare Exchange Agent Registration: Effective September 22, 2026, the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) issued an interim final rule (CMS-9872-IFC) establishing and immediately enacting a temporary moratorium. This moratorium pauses the registration for Plan Year 2027 of agents and brokers seeking to enter into agreements with Federally-facilitated Exchanges if they did not have a current Plan Year 2026 registration. The moratorium will remain in effect until February 1, 2027, unless modified or lifted earlier. This action aims to implement enhanced program-integrity safeguards. This regulatory action will temporarily impact the ability of new or unregistered agents and brokers to participate in Federally-facilitated Exchange enrollments, aiming to reduce fraud and improve the integrity of the healthcare marketplace.
