🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Lei da Nacionalidade (Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, de 18 de maio): Aumenta os prazos mínimos de residência legal em Portugal para a aquisição da nacionalidade portuguesa. Anteriormente, era exigido um período de 5 anos de residência legal para todos os estrangeiros; agora, cidadãos de países de língua oficial portuguesa (CPLP) e Estados-membros da União Europeia necessitam de 7 anos, enquanto os nacionais de outros países requerem 10 anos. Para crianças nascidas em Portugal de pais estrangeiros, um dos progenitores deve residir legalmente há pelo menos cinco anos no momento do nascimento. A lei também elimina o regime especial para descendentes de judeus sefarditas e alarga a possibilidade de obtenção da nacionalidade aos bisnetos de cidadãos portugueses. O impacto prático é que muitos estrangeiros enfrentarão um período mais longo para obter a nacionalidade, embora se abram novas vias para descendentes mais afastados. (Fonte: Diário da República, Observador, Portugal.gov.pt)

Medidas de Desagravamento Fiscal para a Habitação (Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026, de 20 de maio): Introduz alterações fiscais para impulsionar a oferta e acessibilidade da habitação. O limite anual de deduções de rendas no IRS para inquilinos sobe de um valor anterior não especificado para €900 em 2026 e para €1.000 a partir de 2027. O IVA (Imposto sobre o Valor Acrescentado) para empreitadas de construção de imóveis para Habitação Própria e Permanente (HPP) é reduzido do padrão de 23% para 6%. Para os senhorios, as rendas até €2.300/mês passam a ser tributadas a uma taxa fixa de 10% em IRS, anteriormente sujeitas a taxas progressivas mais elevadas. Além disso, prevê a isenção de mais-valias na venda de imóveis quando o valor é reinvestido em habitação para arrendamento a rendas moderadas. Estas medidas visam aliviar a carga fiscal de inquilinos e proprietários e incentivar o aumento da oferta de arrendamento a preços mais acessíveis. (Fonte: Diário da República, Economia e Finanças)

Prova de Vida para Pensionistas no Estrangeiro (Portaria n.º 225/2026/1, de 19 de maio): Altera a regulamentação existente sobre a "prova de vida" para pensionistas de invalidez, velhice e sobrevivência do regime geral de segurança social residentes no estrangeiro. Anteriormente, o processo era mais burocrático, exigindo documentação específica. A nova portaria visa maior celeridade e eficiência na prova de vida documental, reduzindo os requisitos burocráticos, e aplica explicitamente o mecanismo de prova de vida aos pensionistas residentes no Canadá em 2026, juntamente com os já abrangidos (Suíça, Luxemburgo, Países Baixos, Bélgica, Cabo Verde e Reino Unido). O impacto prático é uma simplificação do processo para os pensionistas portugueses a viver no estrangeiro, em particular para os residentes no Canadá. (Fonte: Diário da República)

Moratória de Créditos para Empresas e Famílias Afetadas por Tempestades (Decreto-Lei n.º 98/2026, de 21 de maio): Prorroga por 12 meses, com efeitos a partir de 29 de abril de 2026, a moratória de créditos para empresas e famílias que foram gravemente afetadas por fenómenos meteorológicos adversos, como a "Tempestade Kristin". Enquanto as moratórias gerais da pandemia já haviam terminado, esta medida específica exige que as empresas tenham uma quebra homóloga de faturação comprovada de pelo menos 20% no primeiro trimestre de 2026, e as famílias devem ter créditos para habitação própria e permanente em municípios afetados e estar em situação de "lay-off" ou desemprego resultantes diretamente das tempestades. Esta medida proporciona um alívio financeiro fundamental por um ano para empresas e agregados familiares que cumpram os critérios de elegibilidade nos concelhos mais afetados. (Fonte: Diário da República, RTP)

Repartição de Resultados Líquidos de Jogos Sociais para o MAI (Portaria n.º 224/2026/1, de 19 de maio): Fixa as normas regulamentares para a distribuição da quota de 3,60% dos resultados líquidos da exploração dos jogos sociais (Santa Casa da Misericórdia de Lisboa) atribuída ao Ministério da Administração Interna (MAI). Previamente, a distribuição era definida, mas esta Portaria introduz maior flexibilidade. Agora, 2,65% (anteriormente 2,50%) é destinado especificamente à Autoridade Nacional de Emergência e Proteção Civil para fins de proteção civil, emergência e socorro, incluindo apoio a associações de bombeiros voluntários, mantendo-se as percentagens para sinistralidade rodoviária/prevenção da criminalidade (0,20%) e policiamento de espetáculos desportivos (0,60%). Garante um financiamento contínuo e mais flexível para serviços essenciais de segurança e proteção pública, com um reforço para a proteção civil. (Fonte: Diário da República)

Why it matters: O conjunto de medidas fiscais para a habitação e as alterações à Lei da Nacionalidade representam as mudanças mais significativas, impactando diretamente a vida de cidadãos e residentes no que diz respeito ao acesso à casa própria ou arrendada e à obtenção da cidadania portuguesa.

Securitisation (Overseas STS Equivalence) (European Union, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway) Regulations 2026: These UK regulations were formally made on May 20, 2026. They remove the time limit on the temporary recognition of certain Simple, Transparent and Standardised (STS) securitisations originating in European Economic Area (EEA) States (EU Member States, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway), making them eligible for STS preferential prudential treatment in the UK. Previously, such EEA STS securitisations had temporary recognition in the UK; now, this time limit is removed, providing greater stability and certainty for EU investors, including those in Portugal, whose securitisation products may seek recognition in the UK. These Regulations come into force at 11:00pm on June 30, 2026.

Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) Regulation: This comprehensive EU framework reaches its final full implementation deadline on July 1, 2026, meaning all Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) must be fully authorized or cease regulated operations in the EU. Previously, crypto-assets were subject to fragmented national laws across EU member states; now, MiCA establishes a harmonized, EU-wide framework for licensing, capital requirements (minimum €125,000 to €150,000 depending on services), stablecoin reserve requirements, and oversight for automated trading. This creates legal certainty for crypto businesses and investors across the EU, including those in Portugal, by standardizing regulatory expectations.

EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act) – General Provisions Applicability: While the AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024, most of its general provisions will apply from August 2, 2026. This means that operators of AI systems with potential for significant consequences in areas like biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, and public services must comply with its requirements. Previously, there was no overarching EU legal framework for AI; now, the Act introduces a risk-based approach with prohibitions on unacceptable practices (e.g., social scoring), strict obligations for high-risk systems, and transparency duties. This will significantly impact Portuguese tech companies and businesses utilizing AI by mandating compliance with a new set of rules to ensure safety and fundamental rights.

EU Migration and Asylum Pact – Full Legal Effect: The Migration and Asylum Pact, adopted in May 2024, takes full legal effect on June 12, 2026, reorganizing the EU's approach to border control, asylum claims, and migrant returns through ten interconnected legislative acts. Previously, the EU operated with a fragmented system that placed disproportionate burdens on frontline Member States; now, it establishes a common framework with stronger external border protection, firmer asylum rules, and a mandatory solidarity mechanism for responsibility-sharing among Member States during migration pressure. All EU Member States, including Portugal, must adapt their national legislation and procedures to this new framework, impacting national authorities and potentially affecting border crossings for non-EU nationals.

Why it matters: This week highlights the European Union's continuing push to regulate critical sectors, with key digital and financial market regulations nearing full application, while also seeing a significant step in the formalization of cross-border financial market recognition.

🇺🇸 US Policy

SEC Rescinds "No-Deny" Settlement Policy: The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) rescinded Rule 202.5(e), eliminating its long-standing policy that prohibited parties settling SEC enforcement actions from publicly denying the Commission's allegations. Previously, settling defendants agreed to neither admit nor deny wrongdoing but were barred from publicly disputing the allegations; now, they may do so. The SEC also confirmed it will not enforce existing no-deny provisions in prior settlements. This final rule is effective May 21, 2026. This change grants greater freedom of speech to settling parties in SEC enforcement cases, potentially altering settlement negotiations and public relations strategies for entities facing SEC scrutiny.

Treasury Sanctions Sinaloa Cartel's Fentanyl and Crypto Laundering Network: The U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) sanctioned over a dozen individuals and entities, including Armando de Jesus Ojeda Aviles, Jesus Alonso Aispuro Felix, Rodrigo Alarcon Palomares, and the entity Gorditas Chiwas, linked to the Sinaloa Cartel. These designations target a network involved in laundering fentanyl proceeds from the United States to Mexico using cryptocurrency. Previously, these individuals and entities were not subject to these specific sanctions; now, their property and interests in property subject to U.S. jurisdiction are blocked, and U.S. persons are generally prohibited from engaging in transactions with them. This action was taken on May 20, 2026. This action intensifies U.S. efforts to combat illicit fentanyl trafficking and its financial infrastructure, particularly targeting the use of cryptocurrencies for money laundering by major drug cartels, affecting the broader crypto ecosystem and international illicit finance.

FTC Begins Enforcement of TAKE IT DOWN Act: The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) announced it has begun enforcing the TAKE IT DOWN Act (TIDA), sending warning letters to a dozen websites. TIDA, signed into law in May 2025, mandates that online platforms provide a clear mechanism for individuals to request the removal of non-consensual intimate imagery and must remove such images and known identical copies within 48 hours of a valid request. Companies failing to comply face potential civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation. Previously, platforms had one year to come into compliance with TIDA (until May 2026); now, the FTC has initiated enforcement actions. This action imposes significant new compliance obligations and potential financial liabilities on online platforms, driving a shift towards more proactive content moderation and greater protection against non-consensual image sharing.

SEC Proposes Major Reforms for Public Company Offerings and Reporting: The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed two companion rulemakings on May 19, 2026, aimed at significantly reforming the registered offering process and simplifying public company reporting requirements. Key proposals include expanding Form S-3 eligibility by eliminating the $75 million public float requirement and other transaction-specific limitations, replacing the "well-known seasoned issuer" (WKSI) designation with new categories (Eligible Listed Issuers and Seasoned Eligible Listed Issuers), and streamlining the existing five-category public company filer status framework into two: large accelerated filers and non-accelerated filers. Previously, smaller issuers faced stricter eligibility criteria for Form S-3 and a more complex reporting framework; if adopted, these changes would substantially broaden access to efficient capital raising and reduce compliance burdens. This represents a significant deregulatory push by the SEC to facilitate capital formation, particularly for smaller and mid-sized public companies, potentially reinvigorating the U.S. IPO market and increasing the appeal of public listings.

Executive Order on Financial Technology Innovation and Regulatory Frameworks: On May 19, 2026, President Trump signed an Executive Order directing federal financial regulators to review and identify existing regulations, guidance, supervisory practices, and application processes that could be updated to facilitate innovation and greater competition in financial technology (fintech). The order aims to streamline regulatory processes and encourage collaboration between fintech firms, federally regulated financial institutions, and federal regulators, while maintaining safety and soundness. This represents a formal directive for agencies to proactively adapt regulations to foster fintech growth. This executive order signals a federal policy priority to promote fintech development and integration, potentially leading to a more accommodating regulatory environment and increased investment opportunities in the fintech sector.
