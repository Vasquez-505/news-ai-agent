Alarga-se a possibilidade de tradução das provas teóricas para todas as categorias de habilitação. 🇪🇺 EU Policy

Pact on Migration and Asylum: This comprehensive framework enters into full legal application on June 12, 2026. Previously, the EU's migration and asylum system was largely fragmented, with varying national approaches often leading to disproportionate burdens on frontline states and inconsistent handling of asylum applications. Now, the Pact introduces a common system with stronger external border protection and mandatory solidarity mechanisms among Member States. It requires faster and more rigorous identity checks ("screening") at external borders, to be completed within 7 days, and mandates EU-wide standards for reception conditions for asylum seekers. This aims to streamline asylum procedures and ensure a more equitable distribution of responsibilities across the EU.

New EU Anti-Corruption Directive: Agreed upon by the European Parliament and the Council on April 29, 2026, and highlighted in a publication on June 11, 2026, this Directive aims to harmonise criminal law frameworks across EU member states. Previously, national anti-corruption laws varied significantly. Now, it establishes minimum rules concerning the definition of criminal offences, penalties, and prevention measures. Notably, it introduces corporate liability for failure of supervision or control, with substantial fines ranging from 3% to 5% of a company's global turnover, depending on the criminal offence. Compliance programs are formally recognised as a mitigating factor. Member States have 24 months from adoption to transpose the Directive into national law, with an additional 12 months for provisions relating to risk assessments and national strategies, meaning by April 2028 or April 2029.

New EU Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) Screening Regulation: This regulation was adopted by the Council on June 8, 2026, replacing the 2019 FDI Screening Regulation. Previously, while many Member States had national screening mechanisms, there was no legal obligation for all to have one, leading to fragmentation and inconsistencies across the EU. Now, all 27 EU Member States are legally required to establish a national screening mechanism with minimum common standards for inbound FDI. The regulation defines a mandatory minimum sectoral scope, requiring prior authorisation for investments in critical areas such as dual-use items. It also expands coverage to investments by foreign investors' EU subsidiaries and enhances coordination between Member States and the European Commission. Member States have 18 months from its entry into force (20 days after publication in the Official Journal) to adapt their national frameworks.

EU Listing Act amendments to the Prospectus Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/2809): These amendments fully entered into application on June 5, 2026, modifying the existing Prospectus Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/1129). Previously, companies issuing securities had to adhere to the then-current prospectus content requirements, with base prospectuses approved before this date grandfathered for their 12-month validity. Now, the changes affect the format, sequence, content, scrutiny, and approval processes for relevant prospectuses. Specifically, for non-equity securities marketed with ESG factors or objectives, new specific disclosure requirements apply, which are considered Category C information and can be incorporated into final terms.

Commission Delegated Directive (EU) 2026/374 amending MiFID II research provisions: Published in the Official Journal on June 3, 2026, this Directive enters into force on June 21, 2026. It amends Delegated Directive (EU) 2017/593. Previously, MiFID II imposed strict rules on how investment firms could pay for research and execution services. Now, this amendment provides investment firms with greater flexibility in the payment methods for obtaining execution services and research. It also introduces new operational and disclosure requirements concerning these payments.
