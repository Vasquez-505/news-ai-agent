🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Prohibition of Face Concealment in Public Spaces (Lei n.º 58/2026): This law, which entered into force on September 23, 2026, prohibits the use of clothing that conceals or obstructs the display of the face in public spaces. Previously, there was no specific national law directly prohibiting this. Now, individuals violating this prohibition face fines ranging from €150 to €750 for negligence, and from €400 to €3,000 for intentional infractions. Coercing someone to conceal their face can lead to up to two years in prison or a fine of up to 240 days, with the penalty aggravated by one-third if the victim is a minor. Exceptions are permitted for reasons of health, profession, art, advertising, security, climatic conditions, or legal provisions. Practical impact for citizens: Affects individuals' choice of attire in public and introduces legal penalties for non-compliance or coercion.

Extraordinary Pension Supplement (Decreto-Lei approved by Council of Ministers): Approved by the Council of Ministers on September 17, 2026, this measure introduces an extraordinary, one-time pension supplement to reinforce the purchasing power of lower-income pensioners. Previously, this specific extraordinary supplement did not exist. Now, pensioners with monthly pensions up to €1611.39 will automatically receive a single payment in December 2026, varying between €100, €150, or €200, depending on their income bracket. Practical impact for citizens: Provides direct financial support to a significant portion of the pensioner population to help mitigate the increased cost of living.

Extension of €25 Gas Cylinder Support (Government Announcement): Announced by the government on September 24, 2026, the support of €25 per gas cylinder will be maintained until the end of 2026. This measure extends an existing support that was set to expire. Practical impact for citizens: Continues to provide financial relief to families and businesses on a recurring essential energy cost.

Public Procurement Code Amendment (Decreto-Lei n.º 177/2026): This decree-law, published on September 4, 2026, and entering into force in October 2026, simplifies and updates the Public Procurement Code (CCP). Previously, the thresholds for direct awards were €30,000 for public works contracts and €20,000 for goods and services. For prior consultations, they were €150,000 for public works and €75,000 for goods and services. Now, these thresholds are significantly raised to €150,000 for direct awards in public works (€75,000 for goods/services) and €1,000,000 for prior consultations in public works (€130,000 for goods/services). It also allows for non-award due to "unsatisfactory proposals" and mandates digital integration. Practical impact for businesses: Facilitates and expedites access to public contracts for many companies, particularly SMEs, by increasing the thresholds for simpler procurement procedures.

Renewable Energy Directive (RED III) Transposition (Decreto-Lei n.º 188/2026): Published on September 21, 2026, and entering into force on October 1, 2026, this decree-law partially transposes Directive (EU) 2023/2413 (RED III) into Portuguese law. Previously, the framework for renewable energy targets and fuel composition was based on older European directives. Now, it introduces more ambitious targets for increasing the share of renewable energy in gross final energy consumption, particularly in the transport sector, and mandates that diesel available on the market must contain a maximum FAME (Fatty Acid Methyl Ester) content of 7%. Practical impact for businesses: Requires adjustments in the energy and transport sectors to meet new renewable energy targets and fuel composition standards, potentially influencing fuel supply and pricing.

Why it matters: The government has introduced and brought into force several measures providing direct financial relief to citizens, such as an extraordinary pension supplement and continued gas cylinder support, alongside significant regulatory changes impacting public procurement for businesses and personal freedoms in public spaces.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU Customs Reform (Modernised Union Customs Code): The reform entered into force, marking the start of a more digital, data-driven, and streamlined customs system across the EU. It requires a single customs entry point for all operations. For e-commerce, it abolishes the previous €150 customs duty exemption for low-value consignments, replacing it with a temporary €3 customs duty from July 1, 2026, until July 2028, after which normal duties will apply. An EU-wide handling fee for small parcels will also be introduced from November 2026. Previously, the EU customs system comprised multiple national systems and a €150 customs duty exemption for low-value e-commerce consignments. Now, a unified EU customs system with a single entry point is established, with new customs duties and handling fees for low-value goods. All businesses involved in international trade, particularly e-commerce, must comply. This will simplify procedures but introduce new costs for Portuguese businesses engaged in low-value imports.

EU KIDS Act: The European Commission adopted this legislative proposal which prohibits social media platforms from providing access to children under the age of 13. For minors between 13 and 15, only parent-controlled "mini accounts" are permitted, while autonomous personal accounts are restricted to individuals aged 15 and older. The Act mandates that platforms must be "safe by design" for all under-18 accounts and reverses the burden of proof, requiring platforms to demonstrate their services are age-appropriate and safe. Previously, varying national regulations and often no explicit EU-wide minimum age for social media access existed, relying largely on platforms' self-regulation. Now, a harmonized EU-wide minimum age of 13 for any social media access and 15 for autonomous accounts is established. Social media platforms and app developers are directly affected. This fundamentally reshapes how social media companies operate within the EU and offers enhanced online protection for children in Portugal.

Delegated Regulation establishing a common Union rating scheme for data centres: The Commission adopted this Delegated Regulation to enhance transparency regarding the energy and water usage of data centres. It requires that from August 2027, every data centre already reporting under the EU's 2024 regime will receive an automatically generated label, graded A to G, on separate scales for energy and water efficiency. Previously, data centres were required to report on energy use without a public, standardized rating system. Now, a common A-G rating scheme for both energy and water efficiency will be applied, making their environmental performance transparent. Operators of data centres in the EU with a capacity of 500 kW or more are affected. This increases transparency on the environmental footprint of data centres, encouraging more sustainable practices and influencing investment decisions in the tech sector across the EU, including Portugal.

Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (Directive (EU) 2024/825): This Directive will apply from September 27, 2026, aiming to protect consumers from "greenwashing." It prohibits vague environmental claims and unverified sustainability labels, amending the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (UCPD) and the Consumer Rights Directive (CRD). Businesses are specifically prohibited from making generic environmental claims (e.g., "eco-friendly") without demonstrating recognized "excellent environmental performance" backed by clear, objective, and verifiable commitments. Claims about future environmental performance, including carbon neutrality, are prohibited without a detailed and realistic implementation plan. Previously, environmental claims were subject to general consumer protection laws, allowing for more ambiguous "green" marketing. Now, specific vague claims and unverified labels are explicitly banned, requiring businesses to provide concrete evidence for environmental claims, with potential fines of at least 4% of annual turnover for infringements. All businesses selling products or services to EU consumers are affected. This is particularly relevant for Portuguese businesses given that Portugal is among the Member States that failed to transpose the Directive by the March 27, 2026 deadline and received a letter of formal notice.

Council Regulation on temporary trade-liberalisation measures for Armenian products: The Council formally adopted temporary measures to support Armenia's economy, suspending import duties on approximately 80% of Armenian exports to the EU for a period of two years. This preferential arrangement includes safeguard measures to protect the EU market. Previously, Armenian products were subject to standard EU import duties. Now, for a two-year period, around 80% of these exports will benefit from suspended import duties. The regulation will enter into force the day after its publication in the Official Journal of the EU. EU importers of products originating from Armenia and Armenian exporters are affected. This aims to strengthen trade flows and offers Portuguese businesses potentially lower costs for importing a wide range of goods from Armenia.

Why it matters: This week saw significant advancements in harmonising EU regulations across critical sectors, from digital security for children to the environmental accountability of businesses and trade relations, ensuring a more integrated and transparent Single Market.
