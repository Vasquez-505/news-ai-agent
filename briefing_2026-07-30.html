🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Autorização para a Prestação Social Única (PSU): A Lei n.º 36/2026, publicada a 27 de julho de 2026, autoriza o Governo a criar uma Prestação Social Única (PSU), que agregará 13 prestações sociais existentes. Anteriormente, as famílias com insuficiência de rendimentos tinham de gerir múltiplos apoios separadamente; agora, o Governo está autorizado a simplificar e harmonizar as regras de acesso, reduzindo procedimentos administrativos e reforçando o combate à fraude, com um investimento anual adicional superior a 30 milhões de euros em proteção social. Esta medida visa simplificar significativamente o acesso das famílias vulneráveis aos apoios sociais.

Exclusão de Tributação para Vítimas de Abuso Sexual (Decreto da AR): O Presidente da República promulgou, a 27 de julho de 2026, um decreto da Assembleia da República que estabelece a exclusão de tributação em Imposto sobre o Rendimento das Pessoas Singulares (IRS) para as compensações financeiras atribuídas a vítimas de abusos sexuais ocorridos no contexto da Igreja Católica em Portugal ou em contextos similares. Previamente, estas compensações poderiam estar sujeitas a IRS; agora, as vítimas receberão o valor total da indemnização sem deduções fiscais.

Adiamento da Entrada em Vigor do Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação (RJUE): Um Decreto-Lei aprovado em Conselho de Ministros a 23 de julho de 2026 adia para 1 de outubro de 2026 a entrada em vigor da revisão do Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e da Edificação (RJUE). Esta medida concede mais tempo aos municípios para adaptarem e parametrizarem as suas plataformas informáticas e implementarem a regulamentação complementar necessária. Anteriormente, a revisão do RJUE estava prevista para entrar em vigor antes desta data; agora, há um prazo adicional de aproximadamente dois meses e meio para a sua plena aplicação.

Atualização de Valores na Rede Nacional de Cuidados Continuados Integrados (RNCCI): A Portaria n.º 315/2026/1, publicada a 29 de julho de 2026, procede à oitava alteração da Portaria n.º 45/2021, atualizando os valores por utente/dia atribuídos a todas as tipologias da Rede Nacional de Cuidados Continuados Integrados (RNCCI). Anteriormente, os valores por utente/dia seguiam as tabelas pré-existentes; agora, estes valores foram revistos, o que implicará um ajustamento nos financiamentos. Esta atualização visa melhorar a sustentabilidade e a qualidade dos serviços prestados aos utentes que necessitam de cuidados de longa duração.

Why it matters: A Lei que autoriza a criação da Prestação Social Única representa a mudança mais significativa, pois simplificará drasticamente o acesso a apoios sociais para milhares de famílias portuguesas, agregando 13 benefícios numa só prestação.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

21st Package of Restrictive Measures against Russia: This package, adopted by the Council on July 23, 2026, significantly expands the scope of EU sanctions. Previously, an existing sanctions regime under Regulations (EU) No 269/2014, (EU) No 833/2014, and (EC) No 765/2006 applied. Now, 48 new natural persons and 168 new entities, primarily from the banking sector and non-EU entities involved in circumvention or the trade of Russian oil, have been added to the asset freeze list under Regulation (EU) No 269/2014. It prohibits transactions with several additional Russian banks, with a new derogation allowing transactions strictly necessary to withdraw funds or close accounts with newly listed entities from July 24, 2026, under specific conditions. Furthermore, the package introduces the possibility of a full third-country ban for crypto-asset services used to evade EU sanctions and strengthens legal protection for EU operators in third-country judicial proceedings, including provisions for compensation, non-recognition of Russian judicial/arbitral decisions, and an anti-suit mechanism. This directly impacts EU financial institutions, businesses engaged in trade with Russia or Belarus, and crypto-asset service providers, requiring immediate compliance with expanded prohibitions and due diligence.

Amendments to the EU AI Act (Digital Omnibus on AI): Published in the Official Journal on July 24, 2026, and entering into force on July 27, 2026, this omnibus significantly alters the compliance timeline for the EU AI Act. Previously, standalone high-risk AI systems (Annex III) were facing an August 2, 2026, compliance deadline, and a paradox existed where GDPR prohibited processing sensitive data needed for AI bias detection. Now, the application date for standalone high-risk AI systems (covering areas like employment, education, critical infrastructure, and law enforcement) is deferred from August 2, 2026, to December 2, 2027. For AI systems embedded in products already subject to EU sectoral safety legislation (Annex I), the deadline is pushed further to August 2, 2028. However, core user-transparency obligations under Article 50, such as disclosing when interacting with an AI system and machine-readable labelling for synthetic media, still apply from August 2, 2026. A critical new amendment also establishes a limited and heavily regulated legal exemption, allowing deployers to process "special category data" strictly for the purpose of bias detection and correction. This provides much-needed breathing room for businesses developing high-risk AI, while simultaneously imposing immediate transparency requirements for any entity deploying generative AI.

Council Decision reinstating temporary regulation for Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) detection: On July 24, 2026, the EU Council reinstated a crucial temporary regulation, closing a legal gap that had emerged. Previously, an interim derogation from data-protection rules, which allowed online service providers to voluntarily detect and report CSAM, expired on April 3, 2026. Now, this derogation is reinstated, permitting providers of certain number-independent interpersonal communication services to continue voluntary detection, reporting, and removal of child sexual abuse material on their platforms. This temporary measure is effective until April 3, 2028, allowing for continued action against CSAM while a long-term legislative framework is negotiated. This decision restores legal certainty for tech and communication companies, ensuring they can continue essential efforts to protect children online without legal ambiguity regarding data protection rules.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1824 on lysine imports from China: Adopted on July 28, 2026, this Regulation imposes a new requirement on imports from China. Previously, imports of lysine originating in the People's Republic of China were not subject to specific registration for anti-dumping purposes. Now, these imports are subject to registration, which is a preparatory step to allow for the potential retroactive levy of anti-dumping duties. This directly affects EU importers of lysine from China by introducing a new administrative burden and signals a potential increase in import costs due to future anti-dumping duties, impacting the agricultural and animal feed sectors.

Why it matters: The most significant EU-level change this week is the dual impact of the Digital Omnibus on AI, which delays compliance for high-risk AI systems while imposing immediate transparency obligations for generative AI, along with the 21st sanctions package that broadens restrictive measures against Russia, creating both relief and new compliance challenges for businesses operating in the EU.
