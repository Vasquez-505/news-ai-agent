Anteriormente, não havia este suplemento extraordinário. As famílias verão um aumento do rendimento disponível devido à menor retenção na fonte de IRS a partir de novembro, e os pensionistas receberão um apoio financeiro adicional com a pensão de dezembro.

Obrigatoriedade da Venda Judicial por Leilão Eletrónico: Um Decreto-Lei aprovado torna a venda judicial de bens em processos executivos e de insolvência obrigatoriamente realizada através de leilão eletrónico. Anteriormente, esta modalidade era uma opção entre outras formas de venda judicial. Esta medida aumenta a transparência e a celeridade na recuperação de créditos e na aquisição de bens em leilões judiciais, facilitando o processo para credores e potenciais compradores.

Apoio Complementar Extraordinário aos Operadores de Transporte Rodoviário: Foi aprovado um Decreto-Lei que cria um apoio complementar extraordinário para operadores de transporte rodoviário de mercadorias por conta de outrem e de transporte público rodoviário de passageiros, cobrindo até 70% dos custos extraordinários com combustíveis suportados entre outubro e dezembro. Anteriormente, existia um regime de mitigação de custos de combustíveis, mas este apoio complementar específico não estava em vigor. Reduz o impacto da subida dos combustíveis nos custos operacionais das empresas de transporte, potencialmente contribuindo para a estabilidade dos preços e a manutenção da oferta de transportes.

Lei n.º 58/2026 (Proibição de Ocultação do Rosto em Espaços Públicos): Esta lei, que entrou em vigor a 23 de setembro de 2026, proíbe a utilização, em espaços públicos, de roupas ou acessórios que ocultem ou obstaculizem a exibição do rosto. Anteriormente, não existia uma proibição nacional específica com este âmbito. Cidadãos devem adequar o uso de vestuário em espaços públicos para não cobrir o rosto, com possíveis implicações para determinadas práticas culturais ou religiosas.

Alteração aos Pesos e Dimensões Máximos de Veículos Euro-Modulares: O Decreto-Lei n.º 195/2026, publicado a 25 de setembro de 2026, altera os limites máximos de peso e comprimento para conjuntos de veículos em configuração Euro-Modular. 🇪🇺 EU Policy

Directive (EU) 2024/825 on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition: Prohibits generic environmental claims (e.g., "green," "eco-friendly") unless supported by recognized excellent environmental performance and verifiable evidence. It also bans claims of carbon or climate neutrality relying on offsetting without clear, objective, publicly available, and verifiable commitments and a detailed, realistic implementation plan. Furthermore, it outlaws sustainability labels that are not based on a certification scheme or established by a public authority. Previously, vague or unsubstantiated environmental claims were harder to challenge under general unfair commercial practices rules; now, these specific greenwashing practices are explicitly blacklisted. All companies selling products to consumers in the EU, including those established outside, must comply. Infringements can lead to maximum fines of at least 4% of a company's annual turnover. The directive applies from today, September 27, 2026.

Impact: This directive significantly raises the bar for environmental claims in marketing and advertising across all sectors, compelling businesses, including those in Portugal, to ensure transparency and substantiation or face substantial penalties.

Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) – Reporting Obligations for Products with Digital Elements: Manufacturers of "products with digital elements" (both hardware and software with direct or indirect data connection) placed on the EU market must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe cybersecurity incidents. An early warning must be submitted to the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and relevant national Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs) via a single EU reporting platform within 24 hours of becoming aware, followed by a full notification within 72 hours. A final report must be provided no later than 14 days after a corrective measure is available for an actively exploited vulnerability, or within 1 month for a severe incident. Manufacturers must also inform impacted users of vulnerabilities and mitigation measures. Previously, there was no uniform, mandatory EU-wide reporting framework for such incidents, leading to fragmented or voluntary disclosures. These specific reporting obligations began to apply from September 11, 2026.

Impact: This imposes immediate and stringent cybersecurity incident reporting duties on tech and IoT manufacturers, affecting supply chains and requiring robust internal incident response procedures for businesses operating in or importing into the EU, including Portuguese tech companies.

Commission Delegated Regulations (EU) 2026/1563 (Revised ESRS) and (EU) 2026/1560 (Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard): Regulation (EU) 2026/1563, published on September 21, 2026, amends Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772, replacing current European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) with a revised, simplified set. For financial year 2026, undertakings within the scope of the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) can choose to apply the existing ESRS, the existing ESRS supplemented by a set of reliefs, or the new simplified ESRS in full, and must clearly state which version was applied in their sustainability statement. Previously, CSRD-mandated companies followed a more complex initial set of ESRS. Regulation (EU) 2026/1560, also published on September 21, 2026, establishes a new voluntary sustainability reporting standard for undertakings not subject to CSRD, available for use from September 24, 2026. It also introduces a "value chain cap" effective January 1, 2027, preventing larger companies from requesting information beyond a short list of essential environmental, social, and governance datapoints from their smaller suppliers (with an average of 1,000 employees or fewer).

Impact: This reduces the administrative burden for many larger EU companies subject to CSRD by offering simpler reporting options, encourages standardized sustainability disclosures for SMEs, including in Portugal, and protects small businesses in value chains from excessive data requests.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2101 imposing a definitive anti-dumping duty on imports of pea protein originating in the People's Republic of China: This regulation, published on September 24, 2026, imposes a definitive anti-dumping duty on imports of pea protein from the People's Republic of China and definitively collects the provisional duties previously in place. Importers must now pay the established definitive duty rate on these products, where previously only provisional duties applied. The regulation entered into force on September 24, 2026.

Impact: This increases the cost of importing pea protein from China, directly affecting EU food manufacturers, particularly those in the plant-based food sector, and may lead to a shift in sourcing for Portuguese businesses reliant on these imports.

Why it matters: The application of the anti-greenwashing directive marks a significant shift towards greater transparency and accountability for environmental claims, directly impacting consumer trust and marketing strategies across the EU.
