🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

IRS Rate Reduction Decree-Law: The Council of Ministers approved a decree-law on October 29, 2026, reducing the personal income tax (IRS) rates for the first six income brackets, with retroactive effect from January 1, 2026. Previously, the rates for these brackets were 12.5%, 15.7%, 21.2%, 24.1%, 31.1%, and 34.9% respectively. Now, the rates are set at 12.2% (1st bracket), 15.2% (2nd), 20.7% (3rd), 23.6% (4th), 30.6% (5th), and 34.6% (6th). Furthermore, a government order issued on October 13 and authorized by Parliament on October 22 mandates a 50% reduction in withholding tax for November for most salaries and pensions.

Practical impact for citizens or businesses: This measure directly increases the disposable income for approximately three million families, particularly benefiting the middle class, with an immediate effect through reduced tax deductions from November salaries and pensions.

Nationality Law Implementing Regulations: The Council of Ministers approved a decree-law on October 29, 2026, to regulate the new Nationality Law (Organic Law No. 1/2026, which entered into force on May 19, 2026). These regulations specify new requirements for the practical assessment of citizenship applications, including demonstrable knowledge of the Portuguese language, history, culture, institutions, and proof of financial independence. Previously, while the underlying law had already increased the minimum legal residence requirement from five years to seven years for EU citizens and nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries, and to ten years for other nationalities, and now counts residence from the date the first residence permit is issued rather than the application date, the exact assessment methods for broader integration were less defined.

Practical impact for citizens or businesses: This means applicants for Portuguese citizenship will face clearer, and in many cases more stringent, criteria and processes for demonstrating their integration into the national community, potentially affecting the duration and complexity of their applications.

Alterations to the Hunting Legal Regime: The Council of Ministers approved a decree-law on October 29, 2026, that modifies the legal regime for hunting in Portugal. This decree-law reinforces principles of ethics, safety, and sustainability in hunting activities. Practical impact for citizens or businesses: Hunters and organizations involved in hunting will be subject to updated and more explicit regulations emphasizing ethical conduct, enhanced safety protocols during certain types of hunts, and a stronger focus on environmental conservation.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2393 imposing definitive anti-dumping duty on imports of PET Spunbond from China: Previously, provisional anti-dumping duties were in place on these imports. Now, definitive anti-dumping duties have been imposed, requiring EU importers of PET spunbond originating in the People's Republic of China to pay these duties, which vary by company, to offset unfair pricing. This directly impacts EU businesses by increasing the cost of sourcing PET spunbond from China.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2391 making imports of certain cellulose ethers from China and South Korea subject to registration: Previously, imports of specific cellulose ethers from the People's Republic of China and the Republic of Korea were not systematically monitored for potential anti-dumping measures. Now, all such imports must be registered by customs authorities, enabling the potential retroactive collection of anti-dumping duties if definitive measures are imposed in the future. This introduces administrative requirements and financial uncertainty for EU importers of these chemicals.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2396 making imports of certain steel wire ropes and cables from India, Thailand, and Türkiye subject to registration: Previously, imports of specific steel wire ropes and cables from the Republic of India, the Kingdom of Thailand, and the Republic of Türkiye were not subject to systematic monitoring for potential anti-dumping measures. Now, these imports must be registered by customs authorities, allowing for the potential retroactive application of anti-dumping duties. This affects EU businesses importing these products by increasing administrative burdens and the risk of future financial liabilities.

eIDAS 2.0 Regulation (European Digital Identity Framework) – European Digital Identity (EUDI) Wallet Implementation: Previously, there was no mandated, uniform EU digital identity wallet system. Now, with November 2026 beginning, all EU Member States, including Portugal, are obligated to make at least one fully operational and certified European Digital Identity (EUDI) Wallet available to their citizens and businesses. This wallet will facilitate secure storage and sharing of official documents and identity information for both public and private services.

Why it matters: The active deadline for EU Member States to provide citizens and businesses with a European Digital Identity Wallet marks a significant step towards a more integrated and secure digital single market, fundamentally changing how digital identification and access to services will operate across the Union.
