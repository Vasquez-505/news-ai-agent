🇪🇺 EU Policy

Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) - Reporting Obligations Entry into Force: Manufacturers of "products with digital elements" (both hardware and software) placed on the EU market must now report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe cybersecurity incidents affecting their products within 24 hours of becoming aware. Previously, there was no comprehensive, mandatory, all-sector cybersecurity framework for these products in the EU, and manufacturers could follow voluntary schemes or self-certify. The new binding obligation ensures quicker identification and response to digital security threats. This significantly impacts tech companies and manufacturers across the EU, including Portuguese businesses, by increasing their cybersecurity compliance burden and accountability.

Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) - "Access by Design" Rule Entry into Application: From September 12, 2026, newly placed connected products, smart home devices, connected cars, appliances, and industrial machines in the EU must be designed so that users can easily, securely, and directly access the data they generate by default, in a usable, machine-readable format, free of charge where technically feasible. While other aspects of the Data Act applied from September 2025, this "access by design" rule for new products is now in force. This change empowers users with greater control over their data, compelling manufacturers of connected devices, including those in Portugal, to fundamentally redesign products to ensure data accessibility by default.

Directive on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (ECGT) - Entry into Effect: From September 27, 2026, this Directive amends the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and the Consumer Rights Directive, introducing stricter requirements for environmental claims and sustainability labels in business-to-consumer (B2C) contexts. Previously, environmental claims were subject to general unfair commercial practices rules. The new rules prohibit vague or generic environmental claims without reliable and verifiable evidence, and ban practices like falsely claiming product durability or inducing early replacement of consumables. All businesses selling products or services to EU consumers, including Portuguese entities, must ensure their green claims and product information are substantiated and transparent, with no transitional relief.

Commission Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/2022 and 2026/2023 - Anti-Dumping Registration for Steel Products: These Regulations, adopted on September 10, 2026, make imports of welded steel mesh originating in the People's Republic of China and Türkiye, and bolted and boltless steel shelves originating in the People's Republic of China, subject to registration. This registration is a preliminary step to allow for the potential future levy of anti-dumping duties. Previously, these imports were not subject to this specific registration. This introduces new administrative obligations for EU importers of these specific steel products and signals potential future cost increases due to anti-dumping duties, affecting relevant sectors, including those in Portugal.

Why it matters: This week marks a significant stride in the EU's digital and sustainability agendas, with new regulations enhancing cybersecurity accountability and consumer data control for connected products, alongside stricter rules to combat greenwashing.
