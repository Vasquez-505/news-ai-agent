🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Prestação Social Única (PSU): O Decreto-Lei n.º 166/2026, de 13 de agosto, cria uma Prestação Social Única (PSU) de natureza não contributiva, destinada a unificar e simplificar os apoios sociais para cidadãos em situação de carência económica. Anteriormente, existiam diversas prestações sociais separadas, como o Rendimento Social de Inserção (RSI). Agora, o valor de referência da PSU será de 50% do Indexante dos Apoios Sociais (IAS), atualmente €268,6, com majorações para parentalidade e desemprego. Para os atuais beneficiários de RSI e certas pensões, o novo regime garante que o montante recebido não será inferior ao valor atual. Os cidadãos em idade ativa, sem trabalho, devem estar inscritos em centro de emprego e disponíveis para trabalho ou formação, e para atividades de solidariedade social (até 15 horas semanais). Esta medida entra em vigor em 14 de agosto de 2026, mas os seus efeitos na conversão das prestações só se iniciam a 31 de dezembro de 2026.

Fundos Europeus Portugal 2030 e PRR: O Decreto-Lei n.º 165/2026, de 13 de agosto, altera o modelo de governação dos fundos europeus Portugal 2030, permitindo, de forma excecional, que operações de entidades públicas e do setor social/solidário cujo financiamento pelo Plano de Recuperação e Resiliência (PRR) tenha sido revogado ou cessado, possam ser integradas no Portugal 2030. Anteriormente, não existia um mecanismo expedito para a transição de projetos com financiamento PRR revogado para outros programas de fundos europeus. O diploma também flexibiliza a aplicação do Instrumento Territorial Integrado Redes Urbanas e elimina a necessidade de homologação plenária de reprogramações do Portugal 2030 já aprovadas pelo Comité de Acompanhamento. O objetivo é acelerar a execução dos fundos europeus. Entrou em vigor em 14 de agosto de 2026.

Apoios Financeiros do Estado às Artes: O Decreto-Lei n.º 164-A/2026, de 12 de agosto, introduz alterações ao regime de atribuição de apoios financeiros do Estado às artes visuais e performativas. Anteriormente, o regime não previa explicitamente um fator de atualização para apoios quadrienais nem um programa específico para novos criadores. O novo diploma estabelece um fator de atualização para o apoio financeiro na modalidade quadrienal, reforçando a estabilidade financeira das estruturas apoiadas. Cria ainda um novo programa de apoio direcionado a novos criadores e fortalece a dimensão territorial do regime, permitindo a fixação de montantes globais ou de um número mínimo de apoios a atribuir por circunscrição territorial. Entrou em vigor em 13 de agosto de 2026.

Regulamento da Residência Farmacêutica: A Portaria n.º 332/2026/1, de 11 de agosto, aprova o novo Regulamento da Residência Farmacêutica. Este regulamento visa simplificar os procedimentos no âmbito da formação farmacêutica pós-graduada, cobrindo áreas como Análises Clínicas, Farmácia Hospitalar e Genética Humana. Anteriormente, os procedimentos podiam ser mais complexos. As novas regras simplificam processos relativos a pedidos de suspensão, transferência e mudança de especialidade. Entrará em vigor em 1 de setembro de 2026.

Regras para o Setor da Apicultura (PEPAC Portugal): A Portaria n.º 334/2026/1, de 12 de agosto, procede à sétima alteração à Portaria n.º 54-G/2023, que estabelece as regras nacionais complementares para o apoio ao setor da apicultura no âmbito do Plano Estratégico da Política Agrícola Comum para Portugal (PEPAC Portugal). A principal alteração é a adaptação do limite geográfico permitido para a venda direta de mel e outros produtos apícolas por unidades de produção primária ao consumidor final, agora alinhado com a classificação territorial NUTS (Regulamento (UE) 2023/674). Entrou em vigor em 13 de agosto de 2026.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40): This regulation establishes harmonized EU-wide standards for packaging, replacing fragmented national rules under the previous Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (Directive 94/62/EC). Previously, Member States had discretion in implementing packaging waste rules, leading to divergences across the EU. Now, from August 12, 2026, all companies placing packaged goods on the EU market (including manufacturers, importers, exporters, and e-commerce sellers) must comply with new substance requirements, including a ban on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) above strict limits in food-contact packaging, and a cap of 100 mg/kg for the combined concentration of lead, cadmium, mercury, and hexavalent chromium in packaging components. Companies must also hold an EU Declaration of Conformity and Technical Documentation, and importers from outside the EU must appoint an authorized representative for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in each Member State. This provides greater legal certainty and efficiency for cross-border businesses, including Portuguese companies, and reduces compliance costs in the medium term by streamlining requirements.

EU AI Act – Transparency Obligations (Article 50 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689): These transparency obligations for certain AI systems entered into application on August 2, 2026, impacting tech businesses and digital services across the EU. Previously, there were no harmonized EU-wide requirements for disclosing AI interaction or generating AI-content markings. Now, providers of AI systems that directly interact with individuals (e.g., chatbots) must disclose that users are engaging with AI, unless it's obvious. Furthermore, providers of AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic audio, image, video, or text must embed machine-readable markings and provide a detection mechanism. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform affected individuals, and those using deep fakes or AI-generated text on public-interest matters must disclose the artificial generation, unless subject to human editorial review. A limited transitional period for generative AI systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, allows until December 2, 2026, for implementing technical labeling. This ensures greater transparency for citizens and consumers and significantly impacts tech companies and investors, including those in Portugal, operating in the digital and AI sectors.

End-of-Life Vehicles (ELV) Regulation: This new regulation entered into force on August 13, 2026, revising the rules for vehicle design, production, collection, and end-of-life treatment to enhance circularity in the automotive sector. Previously, the focus was on preventing waste and increasing reuse/recycling under an older Directive. Now, the regulation encourages vehicle design for easier dismantling, reuse, recycling, and recovery, with improved information on component removal. It sets mandatory targets for recycled plastic content, starting with 15% from 2032 and 25% from 2036, and will establish targets for steel and aluminium from 2033. Additionally, from mid-2031, only roadworthy vehicles may be exported outside the EU, and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) is strengthened to ensure producers contribute to the costs of collection and treatment. This impacts vehicle manufacturers, the automotive supply chain, and recycling industries operating in Portugal and across the EU, driving investment towards more sustainable and circular practices.

Why it matters: This week saw the application of crucial EU regulations that will significantly reshape product design, manufacturing, and digital services towards greater sustainability and transparency, compelling businesses to adapt their operations and supply chains across the single market.

🇺🇸 US Policy

Adjusting Imports of Unmanned Aircraft Systems and Components: On August 13, 2026, the President signed a Proclamation imposing ad valorem tariffs on imports of unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) and their components, with rates ranging from 10% to 100%. The specific tariff rate depends on factors such as drone size, capabilities (e.g., 100% on drones over 25 kilograms maximum takeoff weight or with thermal imaging), and country of origin (e.g., 15% on certain drones from the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland, and Taiwan; 10% from the UK, provided substantial domestic content). The Proclamation also authorizes the Secretary of Commerce to establish an onshoring program to incentivize domestic production of UAS and components. The 100% and 25% tariffs will take effect 21 days after signing, while tariffs on certain critical components will be delayed by 180 days. Previously, these imports were subject to general tariff rates. This action is expected to significantly increase import costs for foreign-made drones and components, potentially boosting demand and investment in the domestic U.S. drone manufacturing sector.

Rebuilding the United States Navy and America's Shipbuilding Industrial Base: The President signed a National Security Presidential Memorandum (NSPM) on August 13, 2026, directing the Secretary of War to address long-term issues in Navy shipbuilding and repair programs. The NSPM mandates the establishment of a fifth Naval shipyard (the first in over 80 years) to expand submarine and aircraft carrier repair capacity, the creation of a Component Repair Center for major submarine programs, and a comprehensive review and reorganization of Naval Sea Systems Command. It also directs the replacement of Advanced Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch Systems and Advanced Weapons Elevators with traditional steam and hydraulic systems during the construction of the CVN-81 aircraft carrier. This memorandum signals a significant long-term commitment to expanding U.S. naval capabilities and domestic shipbuilding capacity.

Expanding Capabilities to Combat Transnational Cyber-Enabled Crime: On August 12, 2026, the President signed a National Security Presidential Memorandum (NSPM) empowering U.S. federal law enforcement to utilize cyber tools to disrupt transnational criminal organizations (TCOs) operating in foreign jurisdictions. The NSPM directs the Homeland Security Task Force's National Coordination Center (NCC) to create a program for conducting cyber operations against foreign TCOs, with leadership from the Departments of Justice and Homeland Security, and a framework for integrating private sector capabilities under U.S. government oversight. Previously, the coordination and scope of such federal cyber operations with private sector involvement were less formally defined. This action enhances the government's ability to counter cyber threats from foreign criminal organizations.

Streamlining Export Controls for Drone Exports: Effective August 13, 2026, the Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) issued a final rule easing export controls on certain Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs or drones) and related parts, components, accessories, attachments, technology, and software under the Export Administration Regulations (EAR). The rule eliminates wind gust tolerance as a control parameter, increases the national security control threshold for certain UAVs from 30 minutes to 3 hours endurance, and removes national security controls on associated software and technology for UAVs with less than 3 hours endurance, while maintaining military end-use and end-user controls. Previously, a broader range of drones faced stricter export controls. This regulatory change will facilitate the export of certain U.S.-manufactured drones and related technologies.

Upholding Rescission of De Minimis Tariff Exemption: On August 13, 2026, the U.S. Court of International Trade (USCIT) upheld the President's authority to rescind the "de minimis" tariff exemption, which previously allowed imports of goods valued under $800 to enter the U.S. duty-free. This ruling, made under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), means that goods valued under $800 are now subject to the same duties as higher-valued imports. Previously, these lower-value imports were exempt from duties. This decision will increase import costs for many low-value goods, affecting retailers and consumers.

Why it matters: This week's U.S. policy actions reflect a sharpened focus on national security and economic nationalism, through new tariffs aimed at bolstering domestic industries, significant investments in the defense industrial base, enhanced cyber capabilities against foreign threats, and a judicial affirmation of broad presidential trade authority.
