Alterações à Lei de Estrangeiros: O Parlamento aprovou medidas que impedem a regularização de estrangeiros em Portugal através da entrada como turista com recurso a cursos profissionalizantes, exigindo agora um visto de estudante obtido no país de origem. Anteriormente, estas vias eram frequentemente utilizadas para a obtenção de residência.

Autorização Legislativa para a Criação da Prestação Social Única (PSU): O Presidente da República promulgou a autorização legislativa para o Governo criar a Prestação Social Única. Esta iniciativa agregará 13 prestações sociais existentes, incluindo o rendimento social de inserção, diversos subsídios de parentalidade, pensões sociais (velhice, invalidez, viuvez), o complemento extraordinário de solidariedade, a pensão de orfandade e o subsídio social de desemprego. Anteriormente, estas prestações eram geridas de forma individualizada.

Lei da Proibição de Ocultação do Rosto em Espaços Públicos: A Assembleia da República aprovou uma lei que proíbe a utilização de vestuário ou acessórios destinados a ocultar ou a obstaculizar a exibição do rosto em espaços públicos. Anteriormente, não existia uma proibição nacional explícita e generalizada para tal prática.

Decreto-Lei sobre Prazos de Pagamento de Entidades Públicas: O Conselho de Ministros aprovou um decreto-lei que clarifica as regras aplicáveis aos prazos de pagamento das entidades públicas. Dívidas que excedam 30 dias (ou 60 em situações excecionais legalmente previstas) passam a ser consideradas pagamentos em atraso, e os credores têm agora direito ao recebimento automático de juros de mora após o termo dos prazos legais. Anteriormente, a definição de pagamentos em atraso e o direito a juros de mora não eram tão explicitamente formalizados.

Why it matters: As mudanças na lei de estrangeiros e a aprovação da lei da ocultação do rosto em espaços públicos representam alterações significativas nas liberdades individuais e nos processos de imigração, enquanto a promulgação da autorização para a Prestação Social Única antecipa uma profunda reforma no sistema de apoio social português, e o decreto-lei sobre prazos de pagamento de entidades públicas visa melhorar a previsibilidade financeira para empresas e cidadãos que transacionam com o Estado.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Commission Delegated Act updating Annex I of the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) and Commission Implementing Act on the EUDR Information System: Previously, the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) covered a specific list of products in its Annex I. Now, the Delegated Act removes cattle hides, skins and leather, re-treaded tyres, soybeans for sowing, articles of vulcanised rubber, conveyor and transmission belts, and aircraft and motor vehicle seats from the scope. Concurrently, it adds soluble coffee, certain palm oil derivatives, and frozen cattle tongues. The Implementing Act specifies the functioning of the online Information System where businesses must submit due diligence statements and simplified declarations, with enhanced user-friendliness and simplifications for micro and small primary operators. New products added to the scope will be subject to the Regulation from December 30, 2027. This provides greater legal clarity for businesses involved in these supply chains by specifying which products are covered and simplifying the compliance process, particularly for smaller operators.

Council Decision extending temporary protection for persons fleeing Ukraine: Previously, the temporary protection status for those fleeing Ukraine was set to expire earlier. Now, EU countries have agreed to extend this status until March 4, 2028. A new condition has been introduced, requiring that temporary protection be granted only to those individuals who comply with their military obligations in Ukraine. This provides legal certainty and continued support for approximately 4.38 million displaced persons within the EU, while also addressing concerns related to military service obligations.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 laying down implementation arrangements for the digital product passport registry: Previously, the Digital Product Passport (DPP) was established by Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, but lacked detailed implementation specifics for its registry. Now, this Implementing Regulation sets out the precise arrangements for the digital product passport registry, including technical specifications and procedural requirements for data collection and submission. Companies placing products on the EU market must adhere to these new arrangements for creating and managing their digital product passports. This regulation mandates specific digital data management practices for businesses, facilitating increased transparency and traceability across supply chains to support circular economy objectives and consumer information.

Commission letters of formal notice regarding the EMIR Targeted Review Directive (Directive EU 2024/2994): Previously, Member States, including Portugal, were required to transpose the EMIR Targeted Review Directive into national law by June 25, 2026, which introduced uniform rules for managing counterparty risk in derivative transactions for UCITS (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities), CRD (Capital Requirements Directive), and IFD (Investment Firms Directive). Now, Portugal and 20 other Member States have received letters of formal notice from the European Commission for failing to fully transpose this Directive by the deadline. These Member States have two months to respond and complete their transposition measures. This directly affects Portuguese credit institutions and investment firms, which must now expedite the integration of the Directive's enhanced risk management practices and monitoring tools into their operations to comply with EU law and avoid further legal action.

Why it matters: This week saw the EU finalize key technical details for environmental and digital regulations, ensuring smoother implementation for businesses, alongside an important extension of protection for displaced persons and a clear signal to Member States, including Portugal, about the need for timely financial market rule transposition.

Previously, CMMC Phase II would have required defense contractors handling controlled unclassified information (CUI) to obtain Level 2 third-party assessments as a condition of contract award; now, only Level 1 (self-assessment) and Level 2 (self-assessment) are required until further notice. This action significantly reduces compliance burdens for defense contractors, particularly small and non-traditional businesses, potentially boosting participation in the Defense Industrial Base.

The wind-down period for transactions under the revoked license ended July 17, 2026. Previously, certain transactions involving Iranian oil were temporarily permitted; now, full U.S. sanctions on Iranian oil are again in effect. FTC Reaches Settlement with Pharmacy Benefit Manager Caremark to Lower Drug Costs: On July 14, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) secured a settlement agreement with Caremark Rx LLC and Zinc Health Services LLC, two major pharmacy benefit managers. The agreement mandates that Caremark implement business practice changes, including ceasing discrimination against low wholesale acquisition cost versions of drugs, providing a standard offering to plan sponsors to pass rebates through to members at the point of sale, delinking PBM/GPO fees from list prices, and increasing transparency for plan sponsors. Previously, Caremark was alleged to have artificially inflated insulin drug list prices through anticompetitive rebating practices. This action is intended to lower out-of-pocket drug costs for patients, particularly for insulin, increase transparency in drug pricing, and promote fairer competition for community pharmacies, potentially affecting pharmaceutical and healthcare sector profits.

This policy change impacts the ability of foreign journalists to operate long-term in the U.S., raising concerns about press freedom and potentially leading to reciprocal actions from other countries, particularly China, affecting international relations.

Presidential Proclamations Modify National Monuments and Grant EPA Regulatory Relief for Chemical Manufacturers: On July 13, 2026, President Trump issued proclamations modifying the Bears Ears National Monument and the Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument, restoring sensible land management and potentially opening up areas previously under stricter protection.
