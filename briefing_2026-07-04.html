🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Revisão do Regime de Inspeção Técnica de Veículos (Decreto-Lei n.º 132/2026): Este decreto-lei, publicado a 3 de julho de 2026, procede à revisão do regime jurídico de acesso e permanência na atividade de inspeção técnica de veículos a motor e seus reboques, bem como o regime de funcionamento dos centros de inspeção. Anteriormente, as regras e requisitos podiam ser menos específicos; agora, são atualizados e adensados os requisitos de capacidade técnica e idoneidade, e revista a organização espacial dos centros para garantir uma cobertura mais equitativa no território, incluindo zonas de baixa densidade. O impacto prático é que os operadores de centros de inspeção terão de cumprir novos requisitos de licenciamento e operação, enquanto os cidadãos poderão beneficiar de centros de inspeção com padrões de segurança e qualificação mais elevados.

Criação de Certificados do Tesouro «Série 5» e Suspensão de CTPV (Resolução do Conselho de Ministros n.º 141-A/2026): Esta resolução, publicada a 3 de julho de 2026 e com entrada em vigor a 6 de julho de 2026, cria os Certificados do Tesouro «série 5» e suspende novas subscrições dos Certificados do Tesouro Poupança Valor (CTPV). Anteriormente, os investidores podiam subscrever CTPV; agora, a nova série oferece um prazo de 10 anos, um valor nominal de €1,00 por unidade e uma subscrição mínima de 1000 unidades, com taxas de juro crescentes até 3,35% no décimo ano, substituindo o produto de poupança anteriormente disponível. O impacto prático para os investidores é a mudança nas opções de poupança de baixo risco garantidas pelo Estado, com novas condições e rentabilidades.

Entrada em Vigor de Novas Regras para Criptoativos: As novas regras para a regulação e supervisão de criptoativos entraram em vigor a 1 de julho de 2026. Previamente, a regulação era menos abrangente, focada principalmente em questões de combate ao branqueamento de capitais por parte do Banco de Portugal; agora, o Banco de Portugal e a CMVM são explicitamente definidos como supervisores competentes, com um regime mais robusto para prevenir atividades ilícitas e impondo novos deveres aos prestadores de serviços, incluindo a formação de trabalhadores, e estabelecendo multas para infrações muito graves que podem ir até 5 milhões de euros para empresas ou 15% do volume de negócios em casos de abuso de mercado. O impacto prático é que as empresas e indivíduos que operam com criptoativos em Portugal estão sujeitos a um quadro regulatório mais exigente, aumentando a transparência e a proteção do consumidor.

Declaração de Situação de Alerta devido a Elevado Risco de Incêndio Rural: O Governo decretou a situação de alerta em todo o território continental português, com início a 3 de julho de 2026 (00h00) e fim a 6 de julho de 2026 (23h59). 🇪🇺 EU Policy

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1457 on Steel Tariff Quotas: This regulation distributes tariff quotas for 26 categories of steel products under the new Steel Regulation (EU) 2026/1384. Previously, the EU's steel safeguard was in place; now, tariff-free quotas are set at 18.3 million tonnes per year, with an out-of-quota duty of 50% for imports exceeding these limits. Half of the annual import quota (9.15 million tonnes) is exclusively reserved for countries with existing or future free trade agreements with the EU, while the remaining half is available to all trading partners. It also introduces a traceability requirement for the EU steel supply chain. Businesses importing steel products to the EU must comply with these new quota limits and duties from July 1, 2026. This significantly impacts the EU steel industry and importers, aiming to protect against global overcapacity.

Amendments to the EU Artificial Intelligence (AI) Act (Omnibus VII package): The Council gave its final approval to a new regulation simplifying certain rules regarding AI. Previously, most core obligations for "high-risk" AI systems were set to apply from August 2, 2026. Now, the application dates for stand-alone high-risk AI systems are postponed to December 2, 2027, and for high-risk AI systems embedded in products to August 2, 2028. The new rules also ban AI practices generating non-consensual sexual and intimate content or child sexual abuse material, with systems for generating nude images or editing clothes from photos prohibited from December 2026. The deadline for Member States to establish AI regulatory sandboxes is extended to August 2, 2027. The grace period for providers to implement transparency solutions for artificially generated content is reduced from six to three months, with a new deadline of December 2, 2026. These changes provide greater legal certainty for tech companies and developers, fostering innovation while strengthening ethical safeguards in AI development and deployment.

Council Regulations Implementing EU-U.S. Trade Deal Tariff Commitments: The Council adopted two regulations on June 25, 2026, which formally implement the EU's tariff commitments under the August 2025 EU-U.S. Joint Statement on trade, entering into force the day after their publication. Previously, various U.S. goods faced import duties. Now, the main regulation eliminates import duties on most U.S. industrial goods (including chemicals, pharmaceuticals, plastics, textiles, metals, machinery, vehicles, and aircraft) and reduces duties on certain fresh produce. It also creates 20 new duty-free or reduced-duty import quotas for specific U.S. agricultural and seafood products (e.g., pork, bison, dairy, cheese, nuts, soybean oil). A separate "Lobster Regulation" reinstates the 0% import duty on U.S. lobster imports (retroactive from August 1, 2025) and expands its scope to include prepared or preserved lobster. This directly benefits EU businesses importing these products from the U.S. by reducing costs and stabilizing transatlantic trade relations.

Regulation on Circularity Requirements for Vehicle Design and End-of-Life Vehicles: Formally adopted by the Council on June 29, 2026, this regulation introduces new circularity requirements across the entire lifecycle of vehicles, from design and production to end-of-life treatment. Previously, no such formal EU-wide targets existed. Six years after the regulation enters into force, at least 15% of the plastic used to manufacture new vehicles must come from recycling, increasing to a final target of 25% within ten years, with at least 20% of this recycled plastic originating from end-of-life vehicles. It also mandates extended producer responsibility for promoting circular design, free take-back, and proper treatment of all end-of-life vehicles. This regulation, which will apply two years after its entry into force, significantly impacts the automotive industry by driving the adoption of more sustainable manufacturing practices and increasing demand for recycled materials.

Infringement Procedures for Non-Transposition of Green Transition Directive: The European Commission initiated infringement procedures on June 27, 2026, by sending letters of formal notice to 20 Member States, including Portugal, for failing to fully transpose Directive (EU) 2024/825 on Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition by the March 27, 2026, deadline. This Directive, once transposed, will improve the reliability and transparency of green claims and sustainability labels, encourage businesses to adopt more sustainable practices, prevent greenwashing, and ensure consumers have better information on product durability and repairability. Portuguese businesses, particularly those making environmental claims about their products or services, should be aware that the national legal framework is currently non-compliant and will require updates to align with these EU requirements, with Member States having two months to respond to the Commission.

Why it matters: This week's developments underscore the EU's commitment to both industrial protection and the ongoing evolution of its digital economy, notably through the strategic adjustments to AI Act implementation timelines to balance innovation with critical ethical safeguards and the protection of the steel industry.

This action aims to alleviate rising agricultural input costs for American farmers and bolster food security.

U.S. Blocks USMCA 16-Year Extension, Initiating Annual Review: On July 1, 2026, the U.S. declined to extend the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) for another 16 years, instead initiating an annual review process for the trade pact. Previously, the USMCA included a mechanism for a long-term extension. Now, while the agreement remains in effect until at least 2036, it is subject to yearly reviews and ongoing negotiations as the U.S. seeks to address perceived shortcomings and trade deficits with Canada and Mexico. This move introduces a new phase of uncertainty into North American trade relations, signaling a U.S. push for revised terms.

FTC Final Order Against Travel App Hopper for Deceptive Practices: On July 2, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) finalized an order requiring travel app Hopper to pay $35 million in redress and prohibiting it from deceiving consumers about fees. The action settled allegations that Hopper had charged hidden fees and misrepresented the benefits of its services. Previously, Hopper was alleged to have engaged in practices such as presenting total prices that did not include pre-selected, hidden fees. Now, the company must clearly and conspicuously disclose all fees and total prices, and is legally prohibited from such deceptive conduct. This enforcement action underscores the FTC's commitment to protecting consumers from undisclosed charges and deceptive advertising in the digital marketplace.

DOJ Declination and BIS Penalty on Robert Bosch GmbH for Export Violations: On July 2, 2026, the Department of Justice's (DOJ) National Security Division issued its first declination of prosecution under its revised Corporate Enforcement Policy to Robert Bosch GmbH, while the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) imposed a $36 million civil penalty on Bosch. The actions stemmed from Bosch subsidiaries exporting foreign-produced sensor products and software to Huawei without required authorizations. Previously, companies facing such export control violations would typically face prosecution. Now, due to Bosch's voluntary self-disclosure, full cooperation, and extensive remediation efforts, the DOJ declined criminal prosecution, but Bosch agreed to disgorge approximately $11.4 million in profits, contributing to the $36 million civil penalty from BIS. This case sets a precedent for the DOJ's policy, encouraging corporate self-reporting and cooperation in national security-related compliance matters.
