🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Reforma da Justiça Administrativa e Fiscal: O Conselho de Ministros aprovou um conjunto de diplomas destinados a desburocratizar e agilizar a justiça administrativa e fiscal. Inclui a criação de uma Câmara de Resolução de Litígios de Contratação Pública, respostas especializadas para processos de imigração e proteção internacional, e um novo procedimento administrativo para cidadãos pedirem indemnizações ao Estado por prejuízos causados pela Administração Pública. Anteriormente, os cidadãos tinham que recorrer diretamente aos tribunais para estas indemnizações, e os processos administrativos eram geralmente mais longos e complexos. Agora, ações administrativas de valor inferior a €15.000 terão um processo mais simples e rápido, com menos formalidades. A medida visa reduzir a pendência de processos nos tribunais administrativos e fiscais, que em 2025 registava 152.000 processos em primeira instância, com uma taxa de resolução de apenas 34%.

Lei da Representação de Interesses (Lei do Lobby): O Presidente da República promulgou o Decreto da Assembleia da República que estabelece a orgânica do Registo de Transparência da Representação de Interesses. Esta lei, que entra em vigor a 27 de julho de 2026, impõe novas obrigações de transparência para empresas e outras entidades que interagem com decisores públicos, exigindo o registo das suas atividades de representação de interesses. Anteriormente, não existia um quadro legal abrangente para a atividade de lobby. A nova lei visa aumentar a transparência e a responsabilidade na interação entre entidades privadas e o Estado.

Decreto-Lei n.º 148/2026 (Ação Social no Ensino Superior): Publicado em 20 de julho de 2026, este Decreto-Lei define os princípios e um novo modelo para a política de ação social escolar no ensino superior. O novo modelo visa promover a igualdade de oportunidades no acesso e frequência do ensino superior, através de apoios sociais diretos (bolsas de estudo) e indiretos (residências estudantis, apoio psicológico e adaptação para estudantes com necessidades educativas especiais). O diploma revoga despachos anteriores e altera a Lei n.º 71/2017, de 16 de agosto, que estabelece os preços máximos de refeição e alojamento para estudantes do ensino superior.

Portaria n.º 306/2026/1 (Condições de Trabalho de Administrativos): Publicada em 22 de julho de 2026, esta Portaria procede à oitava alteração da Portaria n.º 182/2018, de 22 de junho, que regula as condições de trabalho dos trabalhadores administrativos não abrangidos por regulamentação coletiva específica. A alteração atualiza a tabela salarial, refletindo o aumento do valor da Retribuição Mínima Mensal Garantida (RMMG) para 2026, previsto no Acordo Tripartido Sobre Valorização Salarial e Crescimento Económico 2025-2028. Anteriormente, a tabela salarial não refletia o valor atualizado da RMMG de 2026. A medida assegura a diferenciação salarial equilibrada nos níveis remuneratórios subsequentes ao mínimo.

Medidas do Pacote Fiscal da Habitação (Entrada em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026): Embora o Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026 tenha sido publicado em 20 de maio de 2026, algumas das suas medidas mais significativas para o fomento da oferta de habitação entram em vigor a 1 de setembro de 2026. Entre elas, destacam-se a implementação do Regime Simplificado de Arrendamento Acessível (RSAA) e os Contratos de Investimento para Arrendamento (CIA), que preveem benefícios fiscais (como isenções de IMT, IMI, AIMI e Imposto do Selo) por um período de até 25 anos para investidores que construam ou reabilitem imóveis para arrendamento com rendas moderadas (até €2.300/mês). Estes regimes visam aumentar a oferta de habitação no mercado de arrendamento a preços acessíveis.

Why it matters: As aprovações e entradas em vigor desta semana demonstram um foco contínuo do Governo na reforma da justiça para torná-la mais eficiente, na promoção da transparência nas relações entre privados e o Estado, e na melhoria das condições de vida e de trabalho, com especial atenção à ação social no ensino superior e a um incentivo mais forte à oferta de habitação no mercado.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

Temporary Regulation on Voluntary Detection of Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM): Online communication service providers offering number-independent interpersonal communication services can now voluntarily detect, remove, and report child sexual abuse material on their platforms until April 3, 2028. Previously, the interim derogation from EU privacy rules that allowed such voluntary detection expired on April 3, 2026, creating a legal gap. This new regulation explicitly excludes communications protected by end-to-end encryption (e.g., WhatsApp, Signal) from its scope. This restores a legal basis for platforms to continue vital CSAM detection efforts while safeguarding the privacy of end-to-end encrypted communications.

21st Package of Restrictive Measures against Russia: The Council adopted a new package of economic and individual sanctions on July 23, 2026. Previously, existing sanctions were in place. The new measures include asset freezes and transaction bans on 94 additional Russian banks and financial institutions, extending existing bans to 33 more, and imposing transaction bans on a Kyrgyz bank and three other non-Russian banks for sanctions circumvention. A new instrument now allows the EU to ban any transaction between an EU operator and any crypto provider used by Russia. Furthermore, it introduces a comprehensive visa ban for Russian combatants and ex-combatants. This significantly broadens the scope of EU sanctions, further restricting Russia's economy and its ability to fund its war of aggression, impacting EU financial institutions, crypto service providers, and businesses.

Commission Decision Fining Google for Digital Markets Act (DMA) Breaches: The European Commission fined Google €890 million (approximately $1.013 billion) on July 24, 2026, for non-compliance with the Digital Markets Act (DMA). Previously, under the DMA, designated "gatekeepers" like Google were prohibited from giving preferential treatment to their own services and were required to allow app developers to inform users of alternative offers. Google was found to be giving preferential treatment to its own services (such as shopping, hotels, transport, and sports results) in Google Search and restricting app developers from guiding customers to alternative, potentially cheaper, offers outside Google Play. This decision mandates structural changes to Google's advertising, search, and app ecosystems in Europe, promoting fair competition and potentially benefiting smaller businesses and consumers.

Why it matters: This week saw significant EU action in digital policy, foreign relations, and competition, with the reinforcement of child protection measures alongside strong privacy safeguards for encrypted communications, a substantial expansion of sanctions against Russia, and a landmark fine against Google enforcing fair competition in digital markets.

These tariffs became effective on July 24, 2026, at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time, with a limited in-transit exemption for goods loaded before July 24, 2026, and entered for consumption before July 28, 2026. Previously, such broad, forced-labor-related tariffs under Section 301 were not in place on a near-global basis; now, affected imports from nearly all major U.S. trading partners are subject to these new tariffs. This action signals a significant shift in global trade policy, increasing import costs for numerous goods and potentially reconfiguring supply chains for U.S. businesses.

Executive Order on Securing America's Defense Supply Chains: On July 20, 2026, President Trump signed Executive Order 14415, "Securing America's Defense Supply Chains and Ensuring Domestic Acquisition of Critical Materials," strengthening prohibitions on defense contractors from acquiring critical materials from foreign adversary countries. The order directs the Department of Defense (referred to as the Department of War) to develop policies within 180 days requiring all contractors supporting national security to map their supply chains back to raw material origin, vet suppliers for various risks (e.g., financial uncertainty, foreign ownership/control/influence, sole-sourcing), and implement mitigation plans, with authorization to use AI for this mapping. Previously, comprehensive supply chain mapping and strict vetting requirements for defense contractors at all tiers, tied to raw material origin and risks, were not uniformly mandated by executive order. This directive will increase scrutiny on defense industrial base supply chains, likely driving domestic sourcing and impacting contractors' operational costs and supplier relationships.

Section 301 Tariffs on Brazilian Imports: On July 20, 2026, the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) formally imposed an additional 25% ad valorem duty on a substantial volume of imports from Brazil, effective July 22, 2026, at 12:01 a.m. ET. This action concludes a year-long Section 301 investigation into Brazil's acts, policies, and practices related to digital trade, electronic payment services, preferential tariffs, ethanol market access, anti-corruption enforcement, intellectual property protection, and illegal deforestation. Previously, these specific imports from Brazil were not subject to these additional duties; now, U.S. importers face increased costs, with a limited in-transit exemption for goods loaded before July 22, 2026, and entered before July 29, 2026. This tariff increase will directly impact U.S. businesses importing goods from Brazil, potentially leading to higher consumer prices or shifts in sourcing.

Treasury Sanctions on Iranian Sanctions Evasion Network: On July 24, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated four individuals and nine entities linked to Iranian financier Babak Zanjani, accusing his network of evading sanctions through financial services, digital asset trading, and other commercial activities. This action targets Zanjani's Iran-based "Dot One" conglomerate and associated firms in Turkey and the United Arab Emirates. These individuals and entities are now subject to asset blocking and prohibitions on transactions with U.S. persons. This broadens the U.S. campaign of economic pressure against Iran, further complicating financial and trade dealings for any entities involved with the designated network.
