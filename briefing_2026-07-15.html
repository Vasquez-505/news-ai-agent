🇵🇹 Portugal Policy

Criação da Universidade de Leiria e Oeste (Decreto-Lei n.º 135/2026): Transforma o Instituto Politécnico de Leiria (IPL) na Universidade de Leiria e Oeste (ULO). Anteriormente, o IPL operava como um instituto politécnico, focado no ensino aplicado; agora, como universidade, terá um âmbito académico mais vasto, incluindo a capacidade de oferecer doutoramentos e expandir a sua investigação. Esta alteração eleva o estatuto académico e a capacidade de investigação nas regiões de Leiria e Oeste, visando atrair mais estudantes e docentes e contribuir de forma mais significativa para o desenvolvimento regional.

Inclusão de Jovens Trabalhadores no Apoio à Mobilidade (Portaria n.º 287/2026/1): Corrige o tratamento desigual anteriormente existente, eliminando a exclusão dos jovens trabalhadores de uma medida de apoio à mobilidade nos transportes públicos. Previamente, estes jovens eram inelegíveis para este apoio específico; agora, estão incluídos e podem beneficiar da medida. Esta mudança permite que jovens trabalhadores acedam a apoio financeiro relacionado com os transportes públicos, potencialmente reduzindo os seus custos diários de deslocação.

Reforma do Mercado de Arrendamento (Programa "Construir Portugal"): Aprovada pelo Conselho de Ministros em 9 de julho de 2026, esta reforma visa aumentar a oferta de habitação. Elimina o limite anterior de aumento de rendas em novos contratos (que antes impedia subidas superiores a 1,02 vezes a renda prévia) e permite agora total liberdade de negociação entre senhorios e inquilinos para definir novos valores. Aumenta também o limite máximo para rendas antecipadas de duas para três meses e remove o teto máximo anterior de dois meses para as cauções. Adicionalmente, a reforma procura acelerar os processos de despejo por incumprimento e estabelece um Fundo de Emergência para a Habitação (FEH) para conceder apoio financeiro direto e não reembolsável para o realojamento rápido de famílias em situações de emergência habitacional, como despejos por carência económica ou violência doméstica. Esta medida aguarda ainda autorização legislativa do Parlamento.

🇪🇺 EU Policy

EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) - Scope and Information System Updates (Commission Delegated and Implementing Acts): The European Commission adopted measures updating the product scope and the functioning of the EUDR's Information System. Previously, the Regulation covered a set of commodities including cattle hides and soybeans, which are now removed from Annex I of the EUDR. Conversely, soluble coffee, certain palm oil derivatives, and frozen cattle tongues have been added to the scope. The Implementing Act introduces simplified declarations for micro and small primary operators within the Information System. New products added to the scope will become subject to the Regulation from December 30, 2027. The Regulation itself will apply from December 30, 2026, for large and medium-sized operators and for micro and small operators already covered by the EU Timber Regulation, and from June 30, 2027, for other micro and small operators. This provides greater legal certainty for businesses by clarifying the exact products covered and simplifying reporting for smaller entities. Portuguese businesses engaged in the import or export of these specific commodities will need to update their due diligence and supply chain monitoring processes to ensure compliance.

Infringement Proceedings for Non-Transposition of NIS2 Directive (Directive (EU) 2022/2555): The European Commission has referred Ireland, Spain, France, and the Netherlands to the Court of Justice of the EU for failing to transpose the NIS2 Directive into their national legal systems. Previously, Member States were required to transpose this Directive, which sets high common cybersecurity standards for entities in 18 critical sectors (e.g., health, energy, transport, public administration), by October 17, 2024. Now, these Member States face potential financial sanctions, including a lump sum and daily penalties, until full transposition is notified. While Portugal is not directly targeted in these specific referrals, this action signals the EU's firm stance on cybersecurity compliance. Portuguese investors and businesses operating in these critical sectors across the EU should ensure their national cybersecurity measures align fully with the NIS2 Directive to avoid future legal action.

EU Commission's July Infringement Package - Focus on Portugal's Non-Compliance: As part of its regular infringement package, the European Commission initiated legal actions against several Member States, including Portugal, for failing to comply with EU law. Previously, Portugal was obliged to adhere to the Fisheries Control Regulation (Regulation (EC) No 1224/2009) and to notify the transposition of Directive (EU) 2024/2839. Now, Portugal has received a letter of formal notice for failing to fulfill its obligations under the Fisheries Control Regulation. Furthermore, Portugal has received a reasoned opinion for not notifying the transposition of Directive (EU) 2024/2839, a directive aimed at reducing reporting requirements for Member States and economic operators in various fields. This requires Portugal to take corrective measures to align with EU law, impacting Portuguese fishing operators and potentially streamlining reporting burdens for businesses if the directive is properly transposed.

Why it matters: This week highlights the EU's dual focus on regulatory clarification and stringent enforcement, providing businesses with clearer operational guidelines while holding Member States accountable for national implementation of critical policies.
